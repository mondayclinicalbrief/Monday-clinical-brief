There are more genuinely free ways to track CPD than most doctors realise. The question is rarely cost — it is which part of the job each one actually does.

Below is a straight account of the free options, what each covers, and where the cost sits when it is not the price.

## First, a distinction that saves confusion

Three different jobs get lumped together under "CPD tracker":

1. **Logging** — recording what you read or attended, with reflection.
2. **Portfolio** — assembling everything into the appraisal submission your appraiser signs off.
3. **Sourcing** — finding the learning material in the first place.

Most tools do one or two of these well. Free tools usually do the first. Paid appraisal platforms do the second. Nothing free does the third, because sourcing is the part that costs someone money to produce.

Knowing which job you are trying to solve makes the choice obvious.

## The free options

### BMJ Portfolio — free, any source

Free for all healthcare professionals, with no subscription. BMJ Group relaunched it in July 2026, and it now records CPD from any source alongside BMJ Learning activity, which it tracks automatically. It is not tied to a single college, and it is the strongest free option on this list.

Three things arrived with the relaunch and are worth knowing: **it integrates directly with FourteenFish**, it offers AI-powered reflection support to help draft and refine reflective entries, and it makes personalised CPD recommendations by role. Portfolio summaries export for appraisal.

**Covers:** logging, reflection, automatic BMJ Learning tracking, organisation across the year, export for appraisal.
**Doesn't cover:** it is not itself an appraisal submission platform, though BMJ describes the FourteenFish integration as reducing manual data entry.
**Best for:** doctors who want a free, source-agnostic log that is not locked to one membership.

### Your Royal College diary — free with membership

Most colleges include a CPD diary. RCGP members get theirs at no extra cost, along with a substantial library of free CPD content. The RCP, RCS and others run equivalent schemes.

**Covers:** logging, with prompts aligned to your own college's scheme — which is a real advantage at appraisal.
**Doesn't cover:** it is only free if you already pay membership. RCGP fees for 2026–27 run from £162 to £645 depending on earnings; BMA standard membership is £46.25 a month. Free at the margin, not free.
**Best for:** anyone already holding membership. If you are paying the fee, use what it includes.

### NHS appraisal platforms — free because your employer pays

SARD, PReP, L2P and trust-specific systems, plus MARS in Wales. Provided through your employer or deanery.

**Covers:** the appraisal submission itself — the job the free logging tools do not do.
**Doesn't cover:** they are generally built for the annual cycle rather than for weekly logging, and most require manual entry.
**Best for:** everyone, because you will be submitting through one of these anyway. Worth knowing which before you choose a logging tool.

### GP Tools — free for NHS GPs

A free appraisal and revalidation portfolio for NHS GPs, GMC and NHS compliant, with patient survey and MSF feedback included at no extra cost.

**Covers:** portfolio and the feedback instruments, which are usually the expensive extras.
**Doesn't cover:** GPs only.
**Best for:** NHS GPs who want a full portfolio without a subscription.

### FourteenFish — free until you submit

Worth including because the pricing is widely misunderstood. You can use the Appraisal Toolkit and only pay when you submit to your appraiser. List price is around £40–50 a year, RCGP members get 30% off, and the RCGP fully funds five years for GPs who gained CCT from 2021 onwards.

**Best for:** most UK GPs, given it is the default platform and the effective cost is low or nil. We cover [the alternatives to it here](/blog/cpd-reading-tool/fourteenfish-alternatives-cpd-logging/).

### A spreadsheet — free, and better than it sounds

Five columns: date, source, time spent, what you learned, impact on practice. That is the whole structure the GMC asks for.

**Covers:** logging, completely.
**Doesn't cover:** nothing does it for you. No prompts, no reminders, no export formatting.
**Best for:** doctors between systems, working across sites, or who simply prefer plain files. A scruffy spreadsheet filled in weekly beats an elegant portfolio left empty. We have a [template you can paste into it](/blog/cpd-reading-tool/cpd-reflection-template-journal-reading/).

## Where the real cost is

None of these reduces the amount of reading you have to do. That is the honest gap in the free-tracker question, and it is worth naming.

A tracker records that you read something. It does not help you work out what was worth reading in the first place — which, for most doctors, is where the time actually goes. The logging is five minutes. Working out which of the week's papers matter is the hour.

That is the problem The Monday Clinical Brief exists to solve, and it is a different problem from tracking. We summarise every new paper from the five highest-impact journals in your specialty, weekly, across 31 UK specialties, with the citation and a reflection prompt attached to each. The MCB CPD Tracker is included free with an annual subscription and is built around that weekly rhythm.

If your problem is purely logging, take one of the free options above — genuinely, they are fine, and the spreadsheet is fine. If your problem is that you cannot keep up with the literature in the first place, no tracker will fix it.
