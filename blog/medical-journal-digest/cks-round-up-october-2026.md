NICE's October 2026 list has seven updated topics, all of them reviews. One changes the order of treatment: greater trochanteric pain syndrome now puts load-management education and a structured exercise programme first, with a corticosteroid injection kept for short-term relief alongside exercise.

NICE Clinical Knowledge Summaries (CKS) are updated on a rolling basis. This round-up gives a one-line takeaway for each of the month's changes, with a link to the source. The authoritative record is the [NICE CKS site](https://cks.nice.org.uk/); this is a summary for awareness, not a substitute for the topic page itself.

Five of the seven topic pages show a September 2026 revision date. NICE published them in its October list, which is why they appear here.

This is part of our monthly series — the [September 2026 edition](/blog/medical-journal-digest/cks-round-up-september-2026/) is here if you missed it.

## The updates that matter this month

- **[Greater trochanteric pain syndrome](https://cks.nice.org.uk/topics/greater-trochanteric-pain-syndrome/).** Reviewed against a September 2026 literature search. **The management recommendations have been updated.** Education on load management and a structured exercise programme are now **first-line treatment**. A **peri-trochanteric corticosteroid injection is reserved for short-term pain relief, alongside exercise**. NICE cites new systematic reviews and an international consensus statement. The topic page adds the detail: physiotherapy referral for a progressive hip abductor strengthening programme over at least 8 to 12 weeks, and an injection considered when pain stops the person engaging with exercise or daily life, or when education and exercise have not helped. Relief from an injection usually lasts up to 3 months and is not sustained. *Takeaway:* the one to read in full this month. If an injection has been an early step for lateral hip pain, the topic now puts it behind exercise and pairs it with exercise rather than using it instead.
- **[Palliative care — constipation](https://cks.nice.org.uk/topics/palliative-care-constipation/).** Reviewed against a September 2026 literature search. **The recommendations have been updated in line with current evidence.** The topic has been restructured, with the Assessment section consolidated into one node, and web links to patient and carer information have been added. NICE's note does not say which recommendations moved. *Takeaway:* read the management section rather than relying on the version you know. Our [palliative care digest](/blog/medical-journal-digest/palliative-care-journal-digest/) covers the wider evidence in this specialty.
- **[Palliative care — malignant skin ulcer](https://cks.nice.org.uk/topics/palliative-care-malignant-skin-ulcer/).** Reviewed against an October 2026 literature search. **The recommendations have been updated in line with current evidence.** Minor restructuring, and new web links to patient and carer information. As with constipation, the note does not list what changed. *Takeaway:* the same advice. If you manage malignant wounds, re-read the topic before your next review.
- **[Vitamin D deficiency in children](https://cks.nice.org.uk/topics/vitamin-d-deficiency-in-children/).** Reviewed against an August 2026 literature search. NICE's note records the search and nothing more: it does not say whether any recommendation changed. *Takeaway:* nothing to act on from the note itself. The adult topic, reviewed in September, gained a daily dose for deficiency in pregnancy; see the [September edition](/blog/medical-journal-digest/cks-round-up-september-2026/).
- **[Cholecystitis — acute](https://cks.nice.org.uk/topics/cholecystitis-acute/).** Reviewed against a September 2026 literature search. Updated evidence has been reviewed and cited throughout the topic. **No major changes to clinical recommendations.** *Takeaway:* no practice change; the evidence behind the recommendations has been refreshed.
- **[Contraception — natural family planning](https://cks.nice.org.uk/topics/contraception-natural-family-planning/).** Reviewed against a September 2026 literature search. **No changes to clinical recommendations.** The topic is in line with the WHO Medical eligibility criteria for contraceptive use, 6th edition (2025). *Takeaway:* no practice change; the topic has been confirmed as current.
- **[Bunions](https://cks.nice.org.uk/topics/bunions/).** Reviewed against a September 2026 literature search. **No major changes to clinical recommendations.** *Takeaway:* no practice change; the topic has been confirmed as current.

## How to use this

Three topics are worth your time this month. Greater trochanteric pain syndrome, if you see lateral hip pain: the order of treatment has changed. And the two palliative care topics, if you manage constipation or malignant wounds in palliative care. NICE has not said which of their recommendations moved, so read them rather than skim.

Everything else is awareness. A review that changes nothing still tells you something: the topic has been checked against a recent literature search and confirmed as current. NICE fully reviews every CKS topic on a five-year cycle, and sooner when significant new evidence appears.

## A note on what this post is — and is not

This is a summary for awareness. It is not a substitute for the [NICE CKS](https://cks.nice.org.uk/) topic pages, which are the authoritative and current source. Always confirm the recommendation on the CKS topic page before acting on it — that matters most for the greater trochanteric pain syndrome and palliative care changes above. Clinical decisions remain the responsibility of the treating clinician.
