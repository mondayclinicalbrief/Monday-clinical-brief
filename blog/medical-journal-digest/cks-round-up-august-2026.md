Six topics were updated in August 2026, and every one of them was a scheduled review rather than a rewrite. None carried major changes to clinical recommendations.

That is worth stating plainly rather than dressing up. But three of the six gained something genuinely useful, and one of those is a rename that will break a bookmark.

## The updates that matter this month

- **[Carpal tunnel syndrome](https://cks.nice.org.uk/topics/carpal-tunnel-syndrome/).** Reviewed against an April 2026 literature search, with **no major changes to clinical recommendations**. Within that: follow-up recommendations in primary care were amended to reflect pragmatic clinical practice, **CTS-6 is recommended to support diagnostic decisions**, and **referral for nerve conduction studies should be reserved for atypical cases and diagnostic uncertainty**. The information on the **Tinel sign** was updated to reflect a recent systematic review. *Takeaway:* the most practically useful item this month. It puts a validated clinical score at the front of the pathway and is explicit that electrodiagnostic testing is for uncertainty, not confirmation of the obvious — worth reading in full if you refer for nerve conduction studies routinely.
- **[Palliative care — breathlessness](https://cks.nice.org.uk/topics/palliative-care-breathlessness/).** **Renamed from "Palliative care — dyspnoea"**, in line with other CKS topics. The topic was restructured, including consolidation of the assessment section into a single node, and **the recommendations were updated in line with current evidence**. Web links to patient and carer information were added. *Takeaway:* note that this is more than a rename — the recommendations moved as well, so re-read it rather than assuming only the title changed. A bookmark to the old name will not find it. Our [palliative care digest](/blog/medical-journal-digest/palliative-care-journal-digest/) covers the wider evidence picture in this specialty.
- **[Otitis media with effusion](https://cks.nice.org.uk/topics/otitis-media-with-effusion/).** Reviewed against a June 2026 literature search, with no major changes to recommendations — but **a new section on signs of hearing loss in infants and children has been added**. *Takeaway:* the addition is the point. Hearing loss in a pre-verbal child is the thing that gets missed, and a prompt list of signs is exactly what a ten-minute appointment needs.
- **[Faecal incontinence in adults](https://cks.nice.org.uk/topics/faecal-incontinence-in-adults/).** Reviewed against a June 2026 literature search. **Minor** changes to recommendations, in line with guidance from **United European Gastroenterology, the European Society of Coloproctology, the European Society of Neurogastroenterology and Motility, and the European Society for Primary Care Gastroenterology**. No major changes. *Takeaway:* no change to the overall approach; the topic has been brought into line with current European specialist guidance.
- **[Contraception — barrier methods and spermicides](https://cks.nice.org.uk/topics/contraception-barrier-methods-spermicides/).** Reviewed against a July 2026 literature search, with **no changes to clinical recommendations**. The topic is confirmed as in line with the **WHO Medical eligibility criteria for contraceptive use, 6th edition, 2025**. *Takeaway:* no practice change — but note the alignment is to WHO MEC, which is a different document from the UKMEC that the contraception assessment topic was aligned to in July.
- **[Pre-patellar bursitis](https://cks.nice.org.uk/topics/pre-patellar-bursitis/).** Reviewed against a July 2026 literature search. **No major changes to the recommendations.** *Takeaway:* no practice change; the topic has been confirmed as current.

## How to use this

Two things are worth your time this month: the carpal tunnel diagnostic pathway if you see hand symptoms regularly, and the palliative care breathlessness topic if you work in that space — because its recommendations changed alongside the rename, which is easy to miss when a title change is the headline.

Everything else is awareness. A review that changes nothing still tells you something: the topic has been checked against a recent literature search and confirmed as current. NICE fully reviews every CKS topic on a five-year cycle, and sooner when significant new evidence appears, so a quiet month is the system working rather than the system idling.

## A note on what this post is — and is not

This is a summary for awareness. It is not a substitute for the [NICE CKS](https://cks.nice.org.uk/) topic pages, which are the authoritative and current source. Always confirm the recommendation on the CKS topic page before acting on it — that matters most for the carpal tunnel and palliative care breathlessness changes above. Clinical decisions remain the responsibility of the treating clinician.
