BMJ (Clinical research ed.)

Risk of hair loss associated with glucagon-like peptide-1 receptor agonists in adults with type 2 diabetes: target trial emulation

Tang H, Zhang B, Lu Y et al. · 2026 Jul 22
Study Type: Retrospective cohort study using target trial emulation methodology
Key Question: Is GLP-1 receptor agonist use associated with increased risk of alopecia compared with SGLT-2 inhibitors or DPP-4 inhibitors in adults with type 2 diabetes?
Key Findings:
  • GLP-1 receptor agonist initiation was associated with higher alopecia risk versus SGLT-2 inhibitors (HR 1.37, 95% CI 1.08–1.73) and versus DPP-4 inhibitors (HR 1.68, 95% CI 1.28–2.20).
  • Association was specific to non-scarring alopecia (HR 1.53 and 1.72 respectively), consistent across subgroup/sensitivity analyses, though attenuated after negative control outcome calibration.
Clinical Relevance: GLP-1 receptor agonists are increasingly prescribed in UK primary and secondary care for type 2 diabetes and obesity; clinicians should be aware of hair loss as a possible, low-frequency adverse effect when counselling patients and evaluating new alopecia presentations.
Limitations: Observational design using single-centre US EHR data risks residual confounding and limited generalisability to UK populations, despite negative control calibration.

A Retrospective Propensity-Matched Cohort Study of Intravenous Versus Oral Iron Formulations for Management of Iron Deficiency Anemia in Pregnancy

Choi UE, Nicholson RC, Economy KE et al. · 2026 Jul 17
Study Type: Retrospective propensity-matched cohort study (large multicentre database, TriNetX)
Key Question: Does IV iron improve maternal/obstetric outcomes compared with oral iron in pregnant patients with iron deficiency anaemia?
Key Findings:
  • IV iron associated with reduced preterm labour (7.7% vs 10.0%, RR 0.77, 95% CI 0.62–0.96) and caesarean section (18.7% vs 22.2%, RR 0.84, 95% CI 0.74–0.96).
  • Significantly higher post-treatment haemoglobin (10.6 vs 9.7 g/dL) and ferritin (138 vs 39 ng/mL), p=.0001.
  • Subgroup excluding iron sucrose showed stronger preterm labour reduction (RR 0.57, 95% CI 0.40–0.80); no significant differences in transfusion, mortality, preeclampsia, or adverse events.
Clinical Relevance: Supports wider adoption of IV iron for antenatal IDA in NHS obstetric anaesthesia pathways, potentially reducing preterm delivery and caesarean-related anaesthetic workload, though transfusion/preeclampsia rates were unaffected.
Limitations: Retrospective database design with residual confounding and reliance on coded diagnoses limits causal inference despite propensity matching.

Circulation

Caffeine and Cardiovascular Disease: A Scientific Statement From the American Heart Association

Marcus GM, Hu FB, van Dam RM et al. · 2026 Jul 20
Study Type: Scientific statement / expert narrative review (AHA)
Key Question: What is the current evidence on caffeine's effects on cardiovascular risk factors and disease outcomes?
Key Findings:
  • Naturally occurring caffeine (mainly coffee) shows an inverse J-shaped relationship with blood pressure; habitual coffee intake is associated with lower incident type 2 diabetes risk, though data are inconsistent.
  • No consistent link between caffeine and lipids, except unfiltered coffee raising LDL cholesterol; coffee consumption generally shows neutral-to-protective associations with coronary artery disease, heart failure, and stroke.
  • RCT evidence in regular coffee drinkers shows caffeinated coffee reduces atrial fibrillation risk but increases premature ventricular contraction frequency; high-dose caffeine (e.g., energy drinks) appears cardiovascularly harmful.
Clinical Relevance: Provides UK cardiologists with reassurance that moderate coffee/caffeine intake is broadly cardio-neutral or protective, informing patient counselling on lifestyle advice and arrhythmia risk, while flagging energy drinks as a concern, particularly in younger patients.
Limitations: Most underlying evidence is observational, limiting causal inference, with RCT data confined to specific populations and endpoints.

The role of selective multidetector computed tomography after transcatheter aortic valve implantation

Moscarelli M, Pibarot P, Van Mieghem N et al. · 2026 Jul 22
Study Type: State-of-the-Art review (narrative), not original research.
Key Question: What is the role of multidetector CT alongside echocardiography in surveillance for subclinical leaflet thrombosis and structural abnormalities after TAVI?
Key Findings:
  • Echocardiography alone may miss early structural/geometric changes; CT enables detailed assessment of leaflet morphology, perivalvular thrombus and prosthesis geometry.

Limitations: As a narrative review, it synthesises existing evidence without new clinical data, and lacks validated criteria for selecting which patients require CT surveillance.

Prevalence and Associated Factors of Intentional Outdoor Tanning Among US Adults

Holman DM, Boring MA, Julian AK et al. · 2026 Jul 22
Study Type: Cross-sectional survey study (2024 National Health Interview Survey, US adults)
Key Question: What is the prevalence of intentional outdoor tanning among US adults, and which demographic/behavioural factors are associated with this practice?
Key Findings:
  • Overall prevalence of intentional outdoor tanning was 30.2% (35.6% women; 26.6% men).
  • Highest prevalence among younger adults (18–29 years), non-Hispanic White individuals, physically active adults, and those reporting heavy/binge drinking (all P<.001).

Clinical Relevance: Although US-based, these findings reinforce risk-profiling for skin cancer prevention counselling in UK dermatology and public health practice, highlighting young, fair-skinned, physically active adults with higher alcohol use as key targets for sun-safety messaging.
Limitations: Cross-sectional, self-reported data preclude causal inference and may be subject to recall/social desirability bias; findings from a US population may not fully generalise to UK demographics or sun exposure patterns.

Racial and Demographic Differences in Use of a Trauma Center Confidential Status Policy

Wodecki DJ, Osowiecki I, Adams C et al. · 2026 Jul
Study Type: Mixed-methods study (retrospective cohort analysis plus survey/qualitative interviews), single-centre.
Key Question: Are there racial/demographic disparities in application of a trauma centre "confidential status" policy, and how do staff perceive its effects on care?
Key Findings:
  • Of 42,540 trauma patients (2015–2020), 319 were placed on confidential status; 62% had assault-related injuries.

Gastroenterology

Serotonergic signaling in gastric physiology and motility disorders

Konings B, Balsiger LM, Schol J et al. · 2026 Jul 22
Study Type: Narrative review
Key Question: What is the role of serotonergic signalling in gastric physiology, and how can this inform therapeutic strategies in gastroparesis (GP) and functional dyspepsia (FD)?
Key Findings:
  • 5-HT4 agonists have the most consistent guideline support for GP, improving gastric emptying and symptoms; 5-HT3 antagonists remain useful for symptomatic nausea/vomiting control.
  • In FD, serotonergic agents may be selectively used off-label by symptom phenotype: mirtazapine (5-HT2 antagonist) for early satiety with weight loss; buspirone (5-HT1A agonist) for early satiety, fullness, or bloating.

Clinical Relevance: Provides UK gastroenterologists a structured, mechanism-based framework for selecting serotonergic therapies in GP and FD, an area with limited licensed options and significant unmet need within NHS dyspepsia/motility pathways.
Limitations: As a narrative review, it highlights an absence of large controlled trials, limiting the strength of therapeutic recommendations.

Overall Outcomes of Liver Transplantation Using Upfront Normothermic Machine Perfusion: A Real-World Single-Center Experience

Nguyen MC, Ohara SY, Barnhill M et al. · 2026 Jul 22
Study Type: Retrospective single-centre cohort study
Key Question: Does normothermic machine perfusion (NMP) close the survival gap between DCD and DBD liver transplantation compared with static cold storage (SCS)?
Key Findings:
  • 1-year graft survival: DCD-NMP 94% vs DCD-SCS 86% vs DBD 93%; patient survival 98% vs 93% vs 94–95%, respectively.

Clinical Relevance: Supports wider adoption of NMP for DCD liver allografts in UK transplant centres to safely expand the donor pool without compromising outcomes, relevant amid ongoing organ shortage and NHSBT efforts to increase DCD utilisation.
Limitations: Single-centre, non-randomised design with potential selection bias in graft allocation to NMP versus SCS.

Blood

Human precursor labeling in situ reveals a conveyor belt differentiation trajectory and a 6-day neutrophil life span

van Grinsven E, Tak T, Hassani M et al. · 2026 Jul 17
Study Type: In vivo human kinetic labelling study (basic/translational research)
Key Question: What are the true kinetics and lifespan of human neutrophil development from bone marrow precursor to mature circulating cell?
Key Findings:
  • Deuterium labelling showed sequential, time-shifted enrichment across promyelocyte→myelocyte→metamyelocyte→band→mature neutrophil stages, consistent with a linear "conveyor belt" (first-in/first-out) maturation model.
  • Myelocytes did not incorporate deuterium via division, indicating they are postmitotic, contrary to current teaching.
  • Mature neutrophils showed continuous bone marrow–blood–tissue exchange, with an estimated minimum circulating lifespan of 6 days—substantially longer than the commonly cited ~1 day.
Clinical Relevance: These revised kinetics challenge assumptions underpinning interpretation of neutrophil counts, recovery timelines post-chemotherapy/G-CSF, and design of granulopoiesis-targeted therapies relevant to UK haematology practice.
Limitations: Small healthy-volunteer cohort limits generalisability to disease states such as neutropenia, sepsis, or malignancy where kinetics may differ substantially.

Clinical Utility of Next Generation Sequencing in Concurrent EBUS-TBNA and Liquid Biopsies in NSCLC

Pastis NJ, Fox AH, Ferguson T et al. · 2026 Jul 20
Study Type: Prospective observational cohort study
Key Question: Does concurrent EBUS-TBNA tissue sampling versus liquid biopsy yield different rates of clinically relevant mutation detection by NGS in NSCLC?
Key Findings:
  • Clinically relevant mutations detected more often via EBUS-TBNA (39.7%) than blood (29.6%); yield difference 15.1% (95% CI 5.5–24.2%, p=0.001).
  • Blood-based NGS had moderate sensitivity (0.53) but high specificity (0.95) versus tissue as gold standard; kappa 0.51 indicating moderate agreement.

Limitations: Single-arm observational design without randomisation limits causal inference, and results may not generalise across differing NGS platforms or turnaround-time/QNS definitions.

Uleri A, Cella L, Long-Depaquit T et al. · 2026 Jul 26
Study Type: Systematic review and pooled (meta-)analysis
Key Question: How safe and durable is active surveillance (AS) as management for selected intermediate-risk (Gleason Grade Group 1–2) prostate cancer?
Key Findings:
  • Pooled AS discontinuation-free survival: 70% (95% CI 62–78) at 3 years, 63% (95% CI 58–68) at 5 years, 53% (95% CI 38–67) at 10 years; GG2-only cohorts fared worse (40% at 10 years).
