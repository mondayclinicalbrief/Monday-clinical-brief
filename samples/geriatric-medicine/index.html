The Lancet Healthy Longevity

Efficacy and safety of statin discontinuation in patients 65 years and older according to frailty status: a population-wide retrospective cohort study in England using Clinical Practice Research Datalink data

Seeley AE, van der Veen R, Wang A et al. · 2027 Sep 21
Study Type: Population-based retrospective matched cohort study (CPRD GOLD linked to HES/ONS), Fine-Gray competing-risk models with IPTW.
Key Question: Is statin discontinuation in people aged ≥65 associated with increased all-cause hospitalisation, and does this vary by frailty status?
Key Findings:
  • Overall, discontinuation increased 1-year hospitalisation risk (sHR 1.22, 95% CI 1.20–1.25; ARD 37.6/1000) and 5-year risk (sHR 1.11; ARD 36.9/1000).
  • The association weakened with rising frailty (1-year sHR: fit 1.30, moderate 1.16, severe 1.03 [0.94–1.12]).
  • No increased risk was seen in severe frailty or dementia; care home residents had lower hospitalisation after discontinuation (1-year sHR 0.76, 0.63–0.90).
Clinical Relevance: Supports frailty-stratified statin deprescribing, suggesting discontinuation may be reasonable in severe frailty, dementia, or care home residence, but less so in fitter older adults.
Limitations: Residual confounding (including confounding by indication, e.g. discontinuation prompted by illness or declining health) limits causal interpretation, and hospitalisation is not a cardiovascular-specific outcome.

Age and Ageing

Nonlinear dose-response relationship between cumulative anticholinergic use and the risk of dementia in adults with overactive bladder: a systematic review and dose-response meta-analysis

Kim JH, Gwon YN, Shim SR et al. · 2026 Aug 03
Study Type: Systematic review and dose-response meta-analysis of 13 observational cohort studies (n = 7,891,574)
Key Question: What is the shape of the dose-response relationship between cumulative anticholinergic exposure and incident dementia in adults with overactive bladder?
Key Findings:
  • Anticholinergic use was associated with increased dementia risk (pooled HR 1.52; 95% CI 1.29–1.80).
  • The relationship was non-linear: HRs rose from 1.11 at 90 days to 1.42 at 1 year and 1.61 at 3 years, peaking at ~874 cumulative DDD (HR 1.63; 95% CI 1.32–2.00).
  • The apparent decline in risk beyond the peak was attributed by the authors to survivor bias and depletion of susceptibles, not biological protection.
Clinical Relevance: Supports early review of OAB anticholinergics in older adults and consideration of alternatives such as mirabegron, with cognitive monitoring in the first 3 years of exposure.
Limitations: Observational data are vulnerable to confounding and protopathic bias, and dementia ascertainment was heterogeneous, so causality cannot be inferred.

Journal of the American Geriatrics Society

Outcome Disparities in Geriatric Hip Fracture: Orthogeriatric Co-Management Versus Internal Medicine Admission

Stevens N, Chao J, Fagan M et al. · 2026 Nov 04
Study Type: Single-centre retrospective cohort study (n=443) with 1:1 matched sub-analysis (91 pairs).
Key Question: Among older hip fracture patients at an institution with an orthogeriatric service, does admission to internal medicine (without geriatric input) rather than orthogeriatric co-management affect outcomes?
Key Findings:
  • Internal medicine admission was associated with higher 1-year mortality (unadjusted OR 3.01, 95% CI 1.53-5.83; adjusted OR 2.66, 95% CI 1.32-5.25), and this persisted in the matched analysis (p=0.035).
  • Internal medicine patients had greater odds of surgical delay beyond 24 hours (OR 3.16, 95% CI 1.98-5.12), longer length of stay, and reduced follow-up within 3 weeks.
Clinical Relevance: Findings reinforce the NHFD/BPT emphasis on consistent perioperative pathways, and suggest outlying or non-orthogeriatric admissions may carry avoidable risk, particularly for surgical timing.
Limitations: Retrospective, single-centre design with small comparator group (n=94) and limited adjustment (age, sex, ASA), leaving residual confounding by indication likely.

Journal of the American Geriatrics Society

Identifying Adverse Drug Withdrawal Events at Scale Using Electronic Clinical Data: Development, Adjudication, and Practical Challenges

Green AR, Sheehan OC, Reeve E et al. · 2026 Nov 05
Study Type: Retrospective observational study (method development with manual adjudication and qualitative content analysis), single US integrated health system (Kaiser Permanente Colorado).
Key Question: Can adverse drug withdrawal events (ADWE) be identified at scale from electronic health data, and what difficulties arise in classifying them?
Key Findings:
  • A two-step approach (identifying intentional discontinuations, then applying diagnostic/procedure codes and clinical parameters within risk windows) was applied to five drug classes in adults ≥65. Only 51% (bladder antimuscarinics) to 81% (antihypertensives) of flagged discontinuations were confirmed as intentional on chart review.
  • Of 293 potential ADWE manually adjudicated, 60 were classed as possible, 23 probable and 2 definite; one major ADWE led to hospitalisation.
  • Adjudication revealed five classification problems: causal inference, timeframe, acute contraindication, terminology, and intent. Many flagged events were expected or intended consequences of discontinuation.
Clinical Relevance: Findings caution that automated ADWE detection will over-call harm, which matters for interpreting safety outcomes in UK deprescribing trials and structured medication review programmes.
Limitations: Single US health system with no validated reference standard, so sensitivity and specificity of the method cannot be established.

Journal of the American Geriatrics Society

Artificial Intelligence as a Bridge to Gaps in Delirium Prediction, Detection, and Management

Pagali SR, Khan A, Fu S et al. · 2026 Nov 05
Study Type: Narrative review (no systematic methodology reported in the abstract).
Key Question: Can AI address current gaps in delirium prediction, detection and management, and what is the evidence for real-world implementation?
Key Findings:
  • Multimodal prediction models (structured data plus clinical notes) report good discrimination for delirium risk, but few have been integrated into clinical practice.
  • A live machine learning model increased targeted detection, yet a pre-post study found longer length of stay, so better recognition did not translate into better outcomes.
  • NLP, LLMs, computer vision, ambient sensing and wearables could enable continuous, less labour-intensive assessment, but evidence is limited with concerns over false positives and generalisability. AI-guided management (reinforcement learning, digital twins) remains largely conceptual.
Clinical Relevance: For NHS teams considering AI-enabled delirium tools, the evidence supports use as an adjunct to evidence-based care pathways, with external validation, defined action pathways and post-deployment monitoring required.
Limitations: Few implementation studies exist, and the abstract does not report review methods, so selection and appraisal of included evidence are unclear.

Age and Ageing

Attitudes of people living with dementia and their carers towards the use of generative artificial intelligence to inform structured medication reviews

Linder B, Du J, Tavares N et al. · 2026 Oct 31
Study Type: Qualitative study (semi-structured interviews and focus groups; reflexive thematic analysis)
Key Question: How do people living with dementia (PLwD) and their carers experience polypharmacy and structured medication reviews (SMRs), and what are their attitudes to generative AI supporting SMRs?
Key Findings:
  • Twelve PLwD (interviews) and 14 carers (two focus groups) described carers as central to managing adherence and monitoring side effects; SMR experiences were variable.
  • SMRs were most valued when clinicians were empathetic and gave personalised guidance.
  • Participants were broadly supportive of AI in SMRs if validated and used to assist, not replace, clinicians. Perceived benefits: reduced administrative burden and more person-centred care. Concerns: patient safety, data security, and disclosure of AI use.
Clinical Relevance: With SMR capacity constrained in NHS primary care, this suggests AI-supported deprescribing tools may be acceptable to PLwD and carers if clinician oversight is retained and carers are actively involved.
Limitations: Small, self-selected sample interviewed remotely, which may exclude people with more advanced dementia or limited digital access, and attitudes were hypothetical rather than based on experience of AI-supported reviews.

Age and Ageing

Eight-week omega-3 polyunsaturated fatty acid supplementation alters but does not attenuate exercise-induced muscle damage in older adults: a randomised controlled trial

Li D, Göger L, Krüger N et al. · 2026 Oct 31
Study Type: Double-blind, placebo-controlled RCT (n=43)
Key Question: Does eight weeks of n-3 PUFA supplementation attenuate exercise-induced muscle damage and improve recovery in healthy older adults?
Key Findings:
  • No functional recovery benefit. The time × group interaction for quadriceps MVC loss was borderline and favoured placebo (P=.064). MVC was lower in the n-3 group at 24 h (−10.9%, 95% CI −21.4 to −0.40) and 72 h (−9.8, 95% CI −18.3 to −1.27).
  • No significant group differences in CK, IL-6 or CRP. CK rose significantly from baseline at 24 h in the placebo group only (+81.1%), suggesting an altered molecular response rather than attenuation.
  • Exploratory analysis: habitual physical activity and sex moderated molecular recovery.
Clinical Relevance: Algae-derived n-3 supplementation (2 g/day) cannot be recommended to support muscle recovery or resistance training in older adults, which is relevant to sarcopenia and rehabilitation advice.
Limitations: Small sample of healthy, community-dwelling older adults using an experimental damage model, limiting generalisability to frail or sarcopenic patients and leaving moderator analyses underpowered.

Age and Ageing

The trajectories of comprehensive capacity of bedridden older people based on latent growth mixture models: a longitudinal study

Luo R, Zhao Y, Jian X et al. · 2026 Oct 31
Study Type: Prospective longitudinal cohort study (latent growth mixture modelling), two Chinese nursing homes, n=226, 1-year follow-up.
Key Question: Are there distinct trajectories of comprehensive capacity in bedridden older adults, and do they predict pressure injury and DVT?
Key Findings:
  • Three trajectories identified: severe impairment/stable (63.5%); moderate impairment/slowly deteriorating (16.3%); moderate-to-severe impairment/fluctuating (20.2%).
  • Tooth loss, hypertension, indwelling gastric tube and dysphagia distinguished trajectory membership (effect sizes not reported in abstract).
  • Relative to the stable-severe group, both other groups had higher pressure injury risk; the slowly deteriorating group also had higher DVT risk (no effect estimates given).
Clinical Relevance: Capacity trajectories in bedbound patients are heterogeneous, which may help UK care home and community teams target pressure injury and VTE prevention, though applicability is uncertain.
Limitations: Small sample from two Chinese nursing homes limits generalisability to UK care settings; confounding is unclear, and the abstract gives no effect sizes or confidence intervals.
