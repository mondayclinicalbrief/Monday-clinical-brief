Prevalence and Associated Factors of Intentional Outdoor Tanning Among US Adults

Holman DM, Boring MA, Julian AK et al. · 2026 Jul 22
Study Type: Cross-sectional survey study (2024 National Health Interview Survey, US adults)
Key Question: What is the prevalence of intentional outdoor tanning among US adults, and which demographic/behavioural factors are associated with this practice?
Key Findings:
  • Overall prevalence of intentional outdoor tanning was 30.2% (35.6% women; 26.6% men).
  • Highest prevalence among younger adults (18–29 years), non-Hispanic White individuals, physically active adults, and those reporting heavy/binge drinking (all P<.001).
  • Sunburn prevalence nearly doubled among intentional tanners vs non-tanners (49.9% vs 28.3%, P<.001).
Clinical Relevance: Although US-based, these findings reinforce risk-profiling for skin cancer prevention counselling in UK dermatology and public health practice, highlighting young, fair-skinned, physically active adults with higher alcohol use as key targets for sun-safety messaging.
Limitations: Cross-sectional, self-reported data preclude causal inference and may be subject to recall/social desirability bias; findings from a US population may not fully generalise to UK demographics or sun exposure patterns.

JAMA dermatology

Proportion of Antiepileptic Drug-Associated Stevens-Johnson Syndrome and Toxic Epidermal Necrolysis: A Meta-Analysis

Lee EY, Knox C, Sugumar C et al. · 2026 Jul 22
Study Type: Systematic review and meta-analysis (50 observational studies, n=4403)
Key Question: What proportion of SJS/TEN cases worldwide are attributable to antiepileptic drugs (AEDs), and which agents are most implicated?
Key Findings:
  • AEDs accounted for 23% (95% CI, 19–26) of SJS/TEN cases; 96% (95% CI, 92–99) of these involved aromatic AEDs, led by carbamazepine (39%), phenytoin (19%), and lamotrigine (15%); non-aromatic AEDs were rarely implicated (2%).
  • Children had significantly higher AED-attributable proportions than adults (35% vs 23%).
  • Marked geographic variation was seen, from 42% in West Asia to 10% in Africa (P<.001).
Clinical Relevance: Reinforces UK prescribing guidance (e.g., MHRA/HLA-B*15:02 and HLA-A*31:01 considerations) favouring non-aromatic AEDs where feasible, particularly in paediatric patients and those of relevant ethnic backgrounds at higher pharmacogenomic risk.
Limitations: Substantial heterogeneity across included studies (design, population, and case ascertainment) limits precision and generalisability of pooled estimates.

JAMA dermatology

Enfortumab Vedotin-Induced Cutaneous Toxic Effects and Survival in Urothelial Carcinoma

Lee E, Karagenova R, Lu C et al. · 2026 Jul 29
Study Type: Multi-institutional retrospective cohort study
Key Question: Does enfortumab vedotin (EV)-induced cutaneous toxicity independently predict survival benefit in urothelial carcinoma, separate from concurrent immune checkpoint inhibitor effects?
Key Findings:
  • Of 449 patients, 45.9% developed a cAE (61.7% attributed to EV); pruritus (41.3%), desquamating dermatitis (37.3%), and morbilliform dermatitis (27.7%) were most common.
  • At the 30-day landmark, EV-induced cAEs were associated with improved PFS (HR 0.60, 95% CI 0.43–0.82) and OS (HR 0.46, 95% CI 0.31–0.67), independent of ICI exposure and immortal time bias.
  • Both early-onset and high-grade EV-induced cAEs remained protective, with no worse survival seen in high-grade reactions.
Clinical Relevance: UK dermatologists co-managing EV-treated bladder cancer patients should recognise cAEs as a potential positive prognostic marker, supporting proactive dermatologic management rather than premature drug discontinuation.
Limitations: Retrospective design with attribution of cAEs based on likelihood scoring introduces potential misclassification and residual confounding.

JAMA dermatology

Early Respiratory Infection Burden and Atopic Dermatitis in Childhood

Brustad N, Wang T, Sultan T et al. · 2026 Jul 29
Study Type: Cohort study (two independent birth cohorts: COPSAC2010 and VDAART)
Key Question: Is early-life respiratory infection burden associated with the risk of developing atopic dermatitis (AD) in childhood?
Key Findings:
  • In COPSAC2010 (n=663), children with the highest infection burden (episodes/days) before age 3 had higher AD risk through ages 0–10 (AOR 1.61, 95% CI 1.05–2.47; AOR 1.69, 95% CI 1.11–2.58), independent of FLG status and antibiotic use.
  • Replicated in VDAART (n=707): highest infection tertile associated with AD by age 6 (OR 1.76, 95% CI 1.16–2.70), with a dose-dependent increase per infection episode (OR 1.03, 95% CI 1.01–1.05).
  • Associations were driven predominantly by respiratory infections rather than gastroenteritis or other infection types.
Clinical Relevance: Supports the exposome hypothesis in AD pathogenesis, suggesting early respiratory infection burden may be a modifiable early-life risk marker relevant to UK paediatric dermatology and allergy prevention strategies, independent of known genetic (FLG) risk.
Limitations: Observational design precludes causal inference; residual confounding (e.g., childcare exposure, family atopy) cannot be excluded.

Journal of the American Academy of Dermatology

Clinical and Dermoscopic Triage of head and neck Macules: The Role of Reflectance Confocal Microscopy in a Prospective Study of 2006 Lesions

Guida S, Pellacani G, Mazzoni L et al. · 2026 Jul 23
Study Type: Prospective multicentre cohort study
Key Question: Does adding reflectance confocal microscopy (RCM) to dermoscopy improve diagnostic accuracy for equivocal flat pigmented macules on the head and neck?
Key Findings:
  • Dermoscopy alone detected only 54.8% of lentigo maligna/LMM cases versus 91.9% correctly diagnosed with RCM integration; BCC/SCC detection improved from 90.4% to 94.2%.
  • Of 2006 lesions, 11.8% were managed as malignant; histology confirmed 114 malignancies (62 LM/LMM, 52 BCC/SCC).
  • A small residual risk remained: 26 lesions changed on follow-up, yielding 4 delayed malignancy diagnoses (0.4%).
Clinical Relevance: For NHS dermatology services managing high volumes of equivocal facial/scalp lesions in an ageing population, RCM-guided triage could reduce unnecessary biopsies in cosmetically sensitive areas while improving LM/LMM detection, supporting non-invasive pathway development where RCM access exists.
Limitations: Selection and verification bias (only suspicious/discordant lesions biopsied) and losses to follow-up limit generalisability of sensitivity/specificity estimates.
