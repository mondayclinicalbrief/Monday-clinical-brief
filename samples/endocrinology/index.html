Diabetes Care

A Recipe for Better Diabetes Management: The DASH4D Dietary Pattern

Selvin E, Sullivan V, Yeh HC et al. · 2026 Oct 20
Study Type: Commentary/perspective article (summarising the DASH4D RCT and prior DASH evidence; not original research)
Key Question: Should a specific evidence-based dietary pattern, DASH4D, be recommended as the starting point for individualised nutrition therapy in diabetes?
Key Findings:
  • Argues that the 2026 ADA Standards of Care recommend individualised nutrition therapy without endorsing a specific dietary pattern, leaving clinicians without a clear starting point.
  • Cites DASH4D, a modified DASH diet for adults with diabetes, as reducing blood pressure and improving glycaemic control in people with type 2 diabetes and hypertension.
  • Concludes DASH4D should be a recommended diet alongside individualised counselling. The abstract gives no effect sizes, confidence intervals, or trial details.
Clinical Relevance: Provides a trial-supported dietary framework that could inform structured education and dietitian-led advice for type 2 diabetes with hypertension, though NICE guidance does not currently specify a single pattern.
Limitations: As an opinion piece, it offers no new data, and the abstract does not report effect sizes, so benefit and generalisability to UK populations and food environments remain unquantified.

Diabetes Care

Low-Carbohydrate Diets, Nutritional Adequacy, Glycemia, and Perinatal Outcomes in Women With Gestational Diabetes Mellitus: A Secondary Analysis of the DiGest Trial

Ahmad E, Dib S, Jones D et al. · 2026 Oct 20
Study Type: Secondary analysis of an RCT (DiGest); observational, baseline cross-sectional/cohort comparison of self-reported dietary intake.
Key Question: Is low or very low carbohydrate intake in GDM associated with altered nutrient adequacy, glycaemia, or perinatal outcomes, given guidance recommending ≥175 g/day?
Key Findings:
  • Of 215 women, 30.7% reported ≥175 g/day, 34.4% 120–175 g/day, and 34.9% <120 g/day. Lower carbohydrate intake coincided with lower energy, fibre, B vitamin, vitamin E and mineral intake.
  • <120 g/day was associated with lower time above range >120 mg/dL (−6.8%, 95% CI −13.4 to −0.2) and lower birthweight (−9.6 centiles, 95% CI −18.7 to −0.6), with no difference in LGA/SGA.
  • Low carbohydrate proportion of energy (<35%) without energy restriction reduced TAR but did not change birthweight.
Clinical Relevance: Informs dietetic advice in GDM, suggesting lower carbohydrate intake improves glycaemia, but micronutrient and fibre adequacy need active monitoring.
Limitations: Single 24-hour dietary recall at baseline is a weak measure of habitual intake, and findings are observational with borderline-significant associations and likely residual confounding.

Diabetes Care

Predictors of Type 2 Diabetes Remission and Relapse After Metabolic-Bariatric Surgery or Medical-Lifestyle Therapy in the ARMMS-T2D Study

Simonson DC, Cummings DE, Schauer PR et al. · 2026 Oct 20
Study Type: Long-term follow-up (median 11 years, maximum 14) of pooled randomised trials (ARMMS-T2D), with post-hoc predictor analysis using time-to-event methods and LASSO.
Key Question: Which baseline characteristics predict T2D remission and subsequent relapse after metabolic-bariatric surgery (MBS) versus intensive medical-lifestyle therapy?
Key Findings:
  • Remission (HbA1c <6.5% off glucose-lowering medication) occurred in 58% of MBS participants (n=166) versus 3% with medical-lifestyle management (n=96).
  • Multivariate predictors of remission: shorter diabetes duration, no baseline insulin use, and greater weight change at 1 year (all P<0.01).
  • Relapse after initial remission in the MBS group was predicted by longer baseline diabetes duration and more glucose-lowering medication classes.
Clinical Relevance: Supports earlier surgical referral for T2D with obesity in NHS pathways, and helps counsel patients on likelihood and durability of remission.
Limitations: Modest sample size, pooled heterogeneous procedures and trials, and predictor analyses were not randomised comparisons, limiting generalisability to current UK practice.

Diabetologia

CCKBR signalling controls human definitive endoderm specification and pancreatic lineage differentiation

Saber L, Kozawa K, Karampelias C et al. · 2026 Oct 20
Study Type: Preclinical in vitro mechanistic study (human iPSC differentiation model with CRISPR/Cas9 knockout, bulk and single-cell RNA-seq).
Key Question: What role does gastrin/CCK signalling via CCKBR play in human definitive endoderm specification and pancreatic endocrine versus ductal fate decisions?
Key Findings:
  • CCKBR expression peaked at definitive endoderm, declined through differentiation, and was largely absent in endocrine cells; GAST and CCK were expressed only transiently in progenitor and endocrine stages.
  • CCKBR knockout shifted cells towards ectodermal and mesodermal transcriptional programmes and reduced pancreatic lineage specification.
  • Sustained CCKBR activation reduced endocrinogenesis and promoted epithelial and ductal-like cell formation.
Clinical Relevance: No direct clinical application yet, but it may inform optimisation of stem cell-derived beta-cell protocols relevant to future cell replacement therapy for diabetes.
Limitations: Findings derive solely from in vitro iPSC models, so relevance to in vivo human pancreatic development or disease remains unestablished (abstract gives no quantitative effect sizes).

Diabetologia

A prospective controlled study assessing postpartum breastfeeding in women with type 2 diabetes: lower rates associated with elevated BMI rather than diabetes

Britten FL, Duncan EL, Callaway LK · 2026 Oct 22
Study Type: Prospective matched controlled cohort study (single-centre, small sample)
Key Question: Are lower breastfeeding rates in women with type 2 diabetes explained by type 2 diabetes itself or by co-existing obesity?
Key Findings:
  • At 4 months, full breastfeeding rates were 24% (T2DM), 38% (BMI-matched controls) and 64% (normal-BMI controls); T2DM vs BMI-matched p=0.26, vs normal BMI p=0.002.
  • Any breastfeeding rates were 55%, 54% and 82% respectively; T2DM vs BMI-matched p=0.79, vs normal BMI p=0.03.
  • Breastfeeding outcomes in T2DM mirrored BMI-matched normoglycaemic women, suggesting obesity is the main driver.
Clinical Relevance: Breastfeeding support for women with T2DM in UK antenatal and postnatal pathways should address obesity-related barriers, not just diabetes-specific factors.
Limitations: Only 28-29 women per group, so the study is underpowered to confirm no difference between T2DM and BMI-matched controls (the abstract does not report confidence intervals or adjusted analyses).

Diabetologia

Prediabetes and diabetes as drivers of fibrosis in MASLD: roles of obesity, adipose tissue dysfunction and insulin resistance

Godinez Leiva E, Barb D, Kalavalapalli S et al. · 2026 Oct 24
Study Type: Prospective cross-sectional study (n=1446), primary care and endocrinology/diabetes clinics; US-based authorship, setting not stated in abstract.
Key Question: Which metabolic factors (obesity, adipose tissue dysfunction, insulin resistance) determine clinically significant liver fibrosis (≥F2) across the glycaemic spectrum in MASLD?
Key Findings:
  • MASLD prevalence rose stepwise: 39% (normoglycaemia), 55% (prediabetes), 68% (T2DM). Significant fibrosis was 1%, 6% and 12% respectively, but did not track BMI/glycaemia stepwise.
  • Fibrosis was rare in lean/overweight prediabetes; risk rose fourfold with obesity, especially with severe adipose tissue and systemic insulin resistance. Obesity plus T2DM carried the highest prevalence (16%), again mainly with insulin resistance.
  • Most participants with significant fibrosis were in the top two quintiles of Adipo-IR or HOMA-IR.
Clinical Relevance: Suggests HOMA-IR could refine fibrosis risk stratification in primary care beyond glycaemic status alone, relevant to targeting FibroScan referral in UK pathways.
Limitations: Cross-sectional design precludes causal inference, and fibrosis was defined by elastography thresholds with confirmatory imaging/biopsy only when ≥F2 was suspected.

European Journal of Endocrinology

BMI as a partitioning factor for estradiol and estrone reference intervals in middle-aged men and postmenopausal women

Kruijt M, de Mutsert R, van Hylckama Vlieg A et al. · 2026 Oct 19
Study Type: Cross-sectional reference interval analysis within a large population-based cohort (n=6,671; oversampled for BMI >27 kg/m²), following CLSI EP28-A3c.
Key Question: Does BMI warrant partitioning of estradiol and estrone reference intervals in middle-aged men and postmenopausal women?
Key Findings:
  • Sex was the strongest partitioning factor, followed by menopausal status in women.
  • Under a shared interval, values above the upper limit (ideal 2.5%) were 4.9% (estradiol) and 5.2% (estrone) in men with obesity, and 11.4% and 7.7% in postmenopausal women with obesity.
  • In postmenopausal women, upper reference limits for normal weight, overweight and obesity were 138, 157 and 210 pmol/L (estradiol) and 32, 41 and 74 pmol/L (estrone).
Clinical Relevance: Estradiol or estrone results from postmenopausal women with obesity, or men with obesity, may be over-flagged as abnormal using standard laboratory intervals, so BMI should inform interpretation until labs adopt stratified ranges.
Limitations: The abstract does not state the assay method (e.g., immunoassay vs LC-MS/MS), and the cohort is middle-aged and BMI-oversampled, so generalisability to other ages, ethnicities and assay platforms is uncertain.

The Journal of Clinical Endocrinology & Metabolism

Impact of setmelanotide on metabolic syndrome risk in patients with proopiomelanocortin or leptin receptor deficiencies

Wabitsch M, Farooqi IS, Chung WK et al. · 2026 Oct 19
Study Type: Post hoc analysis of two open-label phase 3 trials (POMC: NCT02896192; LEPR: NCT03287960).
Key Question: Does setmelanotide improve metabolic parameters and metabolic syndrome severity in patients with severe obesity due to POMC or LEPR deficiency?
Key Findings:
  • Of 21 enrolled patients, 18 had complete data. Baseline means were largely normal except weight measures, fasting glucose and triglycerides; mean MetS-Z-BMI score was 1.49 (SD 1.33).
  • At ~52 weeks, most patients improved in at least one metabolic parameter; mean MetS-Z-BMI change was -1.05 (SD 0.88).
  • Greater MetS-Z-BMI reductions occurred in patients meeting weight-based response thresholds versus non-achievers (P=.0187) and in POMC versus LEPR deficiency (P=.0278).
Clinical Relevance: For the small NHS cohort eligible for setmelanotide, this suggests benefit beyond weight loss, which may inform treatment-response monitoring and commissioning discussions.
Limitations: Very small sample, no control group, and post hoc subgroup analyses, so findings are exploratory and cannot establish causality.
