KDOQI US Commentary on the KDIGO 2024 Clinical Practice Guideline for the Management of Lupus Nephritis

Norouzi S, Contreras GN · 2026 Aug
Study Type: Commentary (KDOQI expert work group response to KDIGO 2024 guideline)
Key Question: How should the KDIGO 2024 lupus nephritis (LN) guideline be interpreted and implemented within US (and by extension, wider international) clinical practice given recent therapeutic advances?
Key Findings:
  • Treatment landscape has expanded since guideline publication: obinutuzumab now FDA-approved alongside belimumab and voclosporin; CAR T-cell therapy trials are emerging for systemic lupus erythematosus/LN.
  • Practice is shifting toward combination immunosuppressive regimens and reduced corticosteroid dosing, reflecting a move toward individualised, less steroid-dependent management.
  • Commentary addresses practical implementation challenges and clinical utility of KDIGO statements rather than presenting new trial data.
Clinical Relevance: UK nephrologists managing LN should note evolving international consensus favouring combination therapy, steroid-sparing strategies, and newer biologics, which may inform local protocols and future NICE/UK Kidney Association guidance.
Limitations: As an expert commentary rather than original research or systematic review, conclusions reflect consensus opinion and US practice context, with limited direct generalisability or new empirical evidence.

Reproductive Health in CKD and Kidney Failure: Core Curriculum 2026

Alleyne SA, Brewster UC, Shah S · 2026 Jul 22
Study Type: Educational review (Core Curriculum series) — not original research.
Key Question: What is the current understanding of reproductive health disruption—including fertility, sexual dysfunction, and pregnancy risk—across the spectrum of CKD, dialysis, and transplantation, and how should it be managed in nephrology practice?
Key Findings:
  • CKD disrupts the hypothalamic-pituitary-gonadal axis in both sexes, causing sexual dysfunction and impaired fertility, which are underrecognised, particularly in men.
  • Up to 6% of women of childbearing age have CKD, with increased risk of adverse pregnancy outcomes (e.g., preeclampsia, preterm birth, CKD progression).
  • Reproductive counselling remains inconsistently embedded in routine nephrology care across CKD stages, dialysis, and transplant populations.
Clinical Relevance: UK nephrologists should proactively integrate stage-specific reproductive and pre-pregnancy counselling into routine CKD clinics to reduce maternal, fetal, and renal complications, aligning with NHS multidisciplinary obstetric-renal care models.
Limitations: As a narrative curriculum review, it synthesises existing literature without new primary data or systematic methodology.

Steroid Minimization in Kidney Transplantation: Rationale, Implementation, and Future Directions

Cheungpasitporn W, Axelrod DA, Caliskan Y et al. · 2026 Jul 24
Study Type: Narrative review
Key Question: What is the current evidence base and practical approach for minimising or avoiding corticosteroids in kidney transplant immunosuppression?
Key Findings:
  • Long-term glucocorticoid use in kidney transplantation is linked to post-transplant diabetes, hypertension, dyslipidaemia, osteoporosis, infection, and cardiovascular disease.
  • Modern immunosuppressive protocols now allow steroid reduction or withdrawal in selected, typically lower-immunological-risk recipients.
  • Emerging biomarkers—donor-specific antibody monitoring, donor-derived cell-free DNA, and gene-expression profiling—may enable individualised patient selection for steroid-sparing regimens.
Clinical Relevance: For UK nephrology teams managing long-term transplant recipients under NHS Blood and Transplant protocols, this review supports a more personalised approach to immunosuppression that could reduce metabolic and cardiovascular morbidity while highlighting monitoring tools relevant to risk-stratified steroid withdrawal decisions.
Limitations: As a narrative review, it synthesises existing literature without new original data, systematic methodology, or quantitative pooled outcomes.

Long-Term Clinical Course of COL4A1-Associated Nephropathy and Assessment of α1 Chain of Type IV Collagen Expression in Kidney Biopsy Specimens: A Case Report

Yoshida R, Hashiguchi A, Nakayama T et al. · 2026 Jul 24
Study Type: Case report
Key Question: What is the long-term kidney functional trajectory and detailed histopathological/ultrastructural phenotype of COL4A1-associated nephropathy?
Key Findings:
  • A 39-year-old man with heterozygous COL4A1 p.G1239R had persistent microscopic haematuria and progressive eGFR decline (-1.2 mL/min/1.73m² per year over 10 years), despite only mild light microscopy changes (focal global glomerulosclerosis, mild tubular dilatation).
  • Electron microscopy showed focal GBM thinning and heterogeneous TBM abnormalities (thinning, thickening, reticulation, lamellation); correlative light and electron microscopy (CLEM) with immunofluorescence/immunoelectron microscopy (FluoroNanogold) demonstrated reduced α1(IV) collagen signal and gold particle density in TBM.
  • CLEM enabled semi-quantitative assessment of collagen expression correlating fluorescence intensity with ultrastructural changes.
Clinical Relevance: UK nephrologists evaluating thin GBM/haematuria syndromes should consider COL4A1-associated nephropathy as a differential to Alport syndrome, with implications for genetic counselling and monitoring of extrarenal (cerebrovascular, ocular) manifestations under Gould syndrome spectrum.
Limitations: Single case report limits generalisability of findings on disease progression and diagnostic utility of CLEM.

Diagnosing Mendelian Kidney Disease: Hidden Niches in the (Kidney) Genome

Ekici AB, Knaup KX, Schaeffer C et al. · 2026 Jul 24
Study Type: Case report (illustrative family case with genetic/functional analysis)
Key Question: Can standard exome sequencing reliably diagnose Mendelian kidney disease, or do certain genomic regions require specialised techniques?
Key Findings:
  • A patient inherited two distinct Mendelian kidney diseases: ADPKD (PKD1, maternal) and ADTKD (UMOD, paternal), explaining a complex family history of renal failure in middle adulthood.
  • The causative PKD1 variant (c.2180T>C, p.(Leu727Pro)) was missed by exome sequencing but detected only via long-range PCR amplification and whole genome sequencing, due to PKD1's pseudogene-rich, technically difficult genomic region.
Clinical Relevance: UK nephrologists requesting genetic testing for suspected ADPKD or overlapping hereditary nephropathies should be aware that routine exome sequencing from non-specialised labs may miss pathogenic PKD1 variants, risking misdiagnosis or diagnostic delay.
Limitations: Single-family case report; findings on assay limitations may not generalise across all PKD1 variant types or laboratory pipelines.

A Qualitative Study of Patients, Caregivers, and Providers on Barriers to Kidney Re-Transplantation Following Graft Loss

Slominska AM, Gouin-Bonenfant M, Katz E et al. · 2026 Jul 22
Study Type: Qualitative study (interpretive descriptive design; semi-structured interviews with thematic analysis)
Key Question: What barriers prevent kidney re-transplantation, particularly living donor re-transplantation, after graft loss?
Key Findings:
  • Across 23 patients, 23 caregivers, and 11 nephrologists (11 Canadian centres), four core barriers to re-transplantation emerged: sensitization limiting compatible donors, comorbidity-driven ineligibility/waitlist withdrawal, prior non-adherence affecting eligibility/referral timing, and delayed or poorly coordinated re-transplant workup.
  • Living donor re-transplantation faced additional barriers: patient preference shifting toward deceased donor transplant, perceived exhaustion of the living donor pool, unresolved psychological burden from prior LKD graft loss, and structural barriers to living donation.
  • Barriers were described as cumulative and compounding rather than simply repeating those faced pre-first transplant.
Clinical Relevance: Graft loss is a major driver of dialysis initiation in the UK, and these findings highlight modifiable system-level issues (referral timing, workup coordination) that NHS transplant services could target to improve pre-emptive re-listing and living donor re-transplantation rates.
Limitations: Findings derive from Canadian centres with purposive/snowball sampling, limiting generalisability to NHS transplant pathways and organisational structures.

Early and Stage-Dependent Cardiopulmonary Fitness Impairment in Pediatric CKD

Werner O, Fila M, Herman F et al. · 2026 Jul 22
Study Type: Prospective multicentre cohort study
Key Question: How does cardiopulmonary fitness (VO2max) vary across CKD stages in children, and what factors determine it?
Key Findings:
  • Among 77 children (CKD stages 2–5) completing valid CPET, mean VO2max Z-score was -1.8±2.1 (~10 mL/kg/min below age/sex-matched norms); 57% met criteria for impaired fitness.
  • VO2max declined progressively with advancing CKD stage (P<0.001), evident even in early-stage disease.
  • Independent determinants (explaining 77% of variability) were haemoglobin concentration, FEV1, VE/VCO2 slope, and ventilatory anaerobic threshold—all potentially modifiable.
Clinical Relevance: Cardiopulmonary fitness is significantly and early impaired in paediatric CKD, supporting routine CPET screening and multidisciplinary rehabilitation (including anaemia correction and pulmonary/ventilatory optimisation) within UK paediatric nephrology services to mitigate long-term cardiovascular risk.
Limitations: Modest sample size (n=77) and lack of a healthy control comparator group limit generalisability and causal inference.

Dialysis Organization Perspectives on the Financial Repercussions of the Expansion of Medicare Advantage

Brazier JF, Tyler DA, Shah AD et al. · 2026 Jul 22
Study Type: Qualitative study (semi-structured interviews, modified grounded theory/content analysis)
Key Question: How has the 2021 US policy expansion of Medicare Advantage eligibility for patients with kidney failure affected the financial and organisational stability of dialysis providers?
Key Findings:
  • Large dialysis organisations negotiated more favourable Medicare Advantage contract rates and margins than small regional providers, creating competitive disadvantage for the latter.
  • Out-of-pocket costs before reaching Medicare Advantage caps were reported as a major driver of patient medical debt, with small organisations absorbing these as bad debt.
  • Staff across 6 organisations (n=37 interviewees) expressed concern over long-term financial instability and market consolidation disproportionately threatening smaller providers.
Clinical Relevance: Although US-specific, this highlights how payer-model reforms can destabilise smaller dialysis providers and increase patient financial burden—relevant to NHS clinicians monitoring international commissioning/insurance models and their implications for equitable renal service provision.
Limitations: Findings are based on a small, US-based qualitative sample (six organisations) and are not generalisable to NHS-funded, tax-based dialysis systems.
