American Journal of Kidney Diseases

Rituximab in IgA Nephropathy: The RITA Multicenter Randomized Trial

Ouyang Y, Ren H, Wang W et al. · 2026 Nov 07
Study Type: Multicentre, open-label (per abstract: randomised, controlled) trial, 10 Chinese centres, 12-month treatment with exploratory follow-up to 24 months.
Key Question: Does adding rituximab to optimised RAS inhibitor therapy reduce proteinuria or preserve kidney function in adults with IgAN and persistent proteinuria >1 g/day?
Key Findings:
  • 106 patients randomised (53 per arm). 12-month change in 24-hour proteinuria did not differ (adjusted GMR 1.05, 95% CI 0.80–1.36); median change −0.21 vs −0.26 g/day (RTX vs RASI alone).
  • No significant differences in eGFR (adjusted mean difference 3.80 mL/min/1.73 m², 95% CI −0.47 to 8.06), Gd-IgA1 (GMR 1.04, 95% CI 0.87–1.24), or ≥50% proteinuria reduction (15.1% vs 20.8%). No hard renal endpoints occurred in either arm.
  • Adverse event rates were similar between groups; findings held through month 24.
Clinical Relevance: Rituximab does not appear to offer additional benefit over supportive care in IgAN, so it should not be used outside trials to treat persistent proteinuria in this setting, consistent with its absence from UK IgAN treatment pathways.
Limitations: Short follow-up and an exclusively Asian cohort limit assessment of long-term outcomes and generalisability.

American Journal of Kidney Diseases

Proliferative Glomerulonephritis With Monotypic Immunoglobulin Deposits of IgG1 Subclass: A Case Series

Bu L, Vaughan LE, Said SM et al. · 2026 Nov 02
Study Type: Retrospective case series (single-centre nephropathology archive), n=46
Key Question: What are the clinical, histological and haematological characteristics, and outcomes, of PGNMID with monotypic IgG1 deposits?
Key Findings:
  • Presentation: median age 57 years, proteinuria 4.8 g/day, eGFR 52 mL/min/1.73 m²; hypocomplementaemia uncommon (12%). Biopsy showed MPGN (54%) or mesangial proliferative GN (33%), with membranous features in 43%.
  • A nephropathic clone or monoclonal Ig was found in 19/42 evaluated patients (10 with CLL/SLL); detection was 72% in those >50 years vs 6% in younger patients. SPEP/SIF detected the mIg in only 13/40.
  • Clone-targeted therapy produced a response in 10/12 (83%) with an identifiable clone, versus 4/8 without a clone given anti-plasma cell therapy. At 60 months, cumulative incidence of dialysis or death was 31.1%; 3 of 4 transplant recipients with biopsies had recurrence.
Clinical Relevance: In patients over 50 with PGNMID-IgG1, thorough haematological evaluation (including marrow assessment and B-cell clone screening, not just SPEP/SIF) is warranted, and findings may guide B-cell versus plasma cell-directed therapy.
Limitations: Retrospective, single-centre design with non-standardised haematological work-up and treatment limits causal inference about therapy response.

Kidney International

Systematic overestimation using in silico-driven curation in a population database caused reevaluation of the genetic prevalence of Alport Syndrome

Robert T, Raymond L, Vanhoye X et al. · 2026 Nov 05
Study Type: Retrospective population genomic analysis with clinical exome cohort validation (gnomAD v4.1.1 plus a 29,055-patient exome cohort).
Key Question: What is the true population prevalence of pathogenic COL4A3/COL4A4 variants (autosomal dominant Alport syndrome), given that earlier in silico-based estimates may be inflated?
Key Findings:
  • Applying the Gibson in silico method to gnomAD v4.1.1 gave 1 in 221 (versus the original 1 in 106). The stricter Expert Strategy (stringent allele frequency thresholds, high-confidence loss-of-function filtering, ClinVar-validated missense variants) gave 1 in 283, closely matching the non-kidney control cohort (1 in 295; concordance 0.96). The Gibson method overestimated the control rate by 91%.
  • Glycine substitutions classed as pathogenic in silico showed no enrichment in kidney disease patients (OR 0.95 and 1.01 for the two variants examined).
  • Expert Strategy variants were present in 1 in 44 kidney disease patients (OR 6.76).
Clinical Relevance: Underdiagnosis of autosomal dominant Alport syndrome is likely real but smaller than previously claimed, supporting targeted COL4A3/COL4A4 testing in CKD pathways while cautioning against over-calling in silico "pathogenic" glycine variants.
Limitations: The reference standard still relies on ClinVar and population data, and the cohort was largely European and not UK-specific, so prevalence and yield may not translate directly to NHS populations.

American Journal of Kidney Diseases

B-Cell Maturation Antigen T-Cell Engager Therapy in a Patient With Refractory Plasma Cell Clonal Cryoglobulinemia Unmasked by RNA-Based Immunoglobulin Sequencing

Piron B, Han Yee Yu J, Caillon H et al. · 2026 Nov 03
Study Type: Single-patient case report.
Key Question: How can an elusive monoclonal plasma cell clone driving refractory type I cryoglobulinemic glomerulonephritis be identified, treated, and monitored?
Key Findings:
  • RNA-based immunoglobulin repertoire sequencing (RACE RepSeq) with bone marrow cell sorting, later supported by direct visualisation in kidney tissue, identified a likely culprit plasma cell clone after conventional work-up failed.
  • Disease was refractory to conventional therapies but responded to teclistamab (BCMA-directed bispecific T-cell engager); no quantitative outcome data (e.g., proteinuria, eGFR) are given in the abstract.
  • MALDI-TOF mass spectrometry detected the cryoglobulin M-protein and enabled residual disease monitoring.
Clinical Relevance: In refractory monoclonal gammopathy of renal significance where the clone cannot be found, advanced sequencing and mass spectrometry may guide clone-directed therapy, and BCMA-directed T-cell engagers may offer a salvage option, though access in the NHS would likely be via specialist haematology/MDT discussion and commissioning routes.
Limitations: A single case with limited reported outcome detail, so efficacy, safety (e.g., infection, cytokine release), durability and generalisability of these techniques are unproven.

American Journal of Kidney Diseases

Innovative Management of Non-HLA Antibody and NK Cell-Mediated Rejection After Kidney Transplantation With Imlifidase: A Case Report

Von Tokarski F, Rabant M, Goutaudier V et al. · 2026 Nov 02
Study Type: Single-patient case report
Key Question: How can graft rejection be diagnosed and managed in a highly sensitised kidney transplant recipient with AMR but no HLA donor-specific antibody (DSA) rebound after imlifidase induction?
Key Findings:
  • Day 20: AKI with dd-cfDNA 1.93% and biopsy showing g1 glomerulitis with C4d(2+); non-HLA anti-endothelial cell antibodies were positive, meeting Banff 2022 criteria for AMR. Steroids and plasma exchange did not achieve control.
  • C5 inhibition failed (dd-cfDNA rose to 3.96%, creatinine worsened). Repeat biopsy showed active AMR with raised NK-related transcripts, prompting daratumumab (anti-CD38).
  • At 6 months: stable creatinine, dd-cfDNA 0.1%, microvascular inflammation resolved, non-HLA antibodies undetectable, no HLA DSA rebound.
Clinical Relevance: Where imlifidase is used in sensitised recipients, rejection without DSA rebound may reflect non-HLA or NK-mediated injury, so dd-cfDNA and transcriptomic/non-HLA testing could inform treatment, although access to these assays in the NHS is limited.
Limitations: A single case with short follow-up cannot establish causality for daratumumab or the efficacy and safety of this strategy; confounding by concurrent therapies cannot be excluded.

American Journal of Kidney Diseases

An Albuminuria Guided Approach to Risk Stratification and Therapeutic Decision-Making in CKD

Rashid I, Heerspink HJL, Inker LA · 2026 Nov 02
Study Type: Narrative review (synthesises epidemiological, trial-level and meta-analytic evidence; no original data).
Key Question: Can serial UACR monitoring support risk stratification and a treat-to-target approach to therapy in CKD?
Key Findings:
  • Baseline UACR and its change over time show a continuous, graded association with kidney failure, cardiovascular events and mortality.
  • Trial-level meta-analysis: a treatment-induced UACR reduction of ≥30% corresponds to roughly a 20% treatment benefit on CKD progression, supporting UACR as a surrogate endpoint.
  • Residual albuminuria on treatment marks persistently high risk; combination therapies can deliver further UACR reductions, exceeding 50% in selected settings.
Clinical Relevance: Supports serial UACR monitoring and escalation of combination therapy (e.g. RAS blockade, SGLT2 inhibitors, MRAs) in UK practice, though prospective evidence that UACR-targeted titration improves hard outcomes is not presented.
Limitations: As a narrative review, it depends on trial-level surrogate associations and does not demonstrate that treating to a specific UACR target improves individual patient outcomes.

American Journal of Kidney Diseases

Therapeutic Living Kidney Donation for Symptomatic Nutcracker Syndrome: A Multicenter Experience Using Kidney Paired Exchange

Matar AJ, Kensinger C, Thomas AG et al. · 2026 Nov 05
Study Type: Multicentre case series (n = 7 donors, 3 US centres, 2022–2024)
Key Question: Is nephrectomy for refractory symptomatic nutcracker syndrome, with the kidney donated via paired exchange, feasible and effective?
Key Findings:
  • All seven donors (six female, median age 38) reported symptom relief; no perioperative complications. Techniques were laparoscopic (n=2), hand-assisted (n=4) and open (n=1).
  • Donor creatinine rose from a mean of 0.71 to 1.08 mg/dL at 6 months.
  • All kidneys were transplanted via national paired exchange. Recipient mean creatinine was 1.1 mg/dL at 6 months and 1.2 mg/dL at 1 year.
Clinical Relevance: Offers a potential definitive option for refractory nutcracker syndrome while adding to the donor pool, but UK applicability would require consideration of HTA/UK Living Kidney Sharing Scheme frameworks and ethical scrutiny of donor selection.
Limitations: Very small, uncontrolled series with short follow-up and no comparison against alternative treatments (e.g. stenting, transposition), so long-term donor renal and symptom outcomes remain unknown.

Clinical Journal of the American Society of Nephrology

Social Determinants of Kidney and Cardiovascular Outcomes: Prioritizing Action to Effectively Manage Cardiovascular-Kidney-Metabolic Syndrome

Mohottige D, Williams M, Boulware LE et al. · 2026 Nov 02
Study Type: Narrative review (US-focused). No systematic methodology described.
Key Question: How do social and structural determinants of health influence cardiovascular-kidney-metabolic (CKM) syndrome outcomes, and which policy actions could address them?
Key Findings:
  • Epidemiological evidence links adverse SDOH to advanced CKM syndrome, disease disparities, and avoidable healthcare costs. No pooled effect sizes are reported in the abstract.
  • Community-level factors (built/natural environment, commercial determinants) and individual-level factors (income, education) are proposed as policy targets.
  • Postulated mechanisms include glucocorticoid resistance, chronic sympathetic activation, pro-inflammatory transcription, weathering, and premature ageing.
Clinical Relevance: The CKM framework and SDOH-driven inequality are relevant to UK renal services, where deprivation shapes CKD incidence, late presentation, and access to transplantation, though the review draws solely on US evidence.
Limitations: Narrative design with US-only evidence limits generalisability to NHS settings. The mechanistic pathways are postulated rather than demonstrated.
