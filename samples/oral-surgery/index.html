British Journal of Oral and Maxillofacial Surgery

Contralateral sentinel node drainage patterns in early lateralised oral squamous cell carcinoma - a retrospective study

Ubhi J, Wilkinson S, Sahovaler A et al. · 2026 Nov
Study Type: Single-centre retrospective cohort study
Key Question: How often do lateralised early (cT1-T2) oral SCC tumours drain to the contralateral neck on sentinel node biopsy, and how often is this nodal disease?
Key Findings:
  • Of 64 patients analysable for drainage, 26 (40.6%) showed contralateral or bilateral drainage; this was 40.0% (20/50) in anterior two-thirds tongue tumours.
  • 22 patients (34.4%) had a positive SNB, of whom 3 (4.7%) had contralateral metastases. These three would have been inadequately staged or treated by ipsilateral elective neck dissection.
  • One false-negative SNB (1.5%). Mean follow-up 46.3 months; 3-year overall survival 77.8%, disease-free survival 80.0%.
Clinical Relevance: SNB reveals contralateral disease that an ipsilateral elective neck dissection would miss, which is relevant to individualised neck management in early OCSCC as SNB use grows in the UK.
Limitations: Retrospective single-centre design with a small sample (64 of 92 patients analysable) and few contralateral metastases limits the strength of any associations with tumour characteristics.

Journal of Cranio-Maxillofacial Surgery

Postoperative radiotherapy for early oral squamous cell carcinoma with a single ipsilateral lymph node metastasis - a retrospective cohort study

Alkhateeb A, Hawas B, Brinks R et al. · 2026 Oct 25
Study Type: Retrospective single-centre cohort study (n=85).
Key Question: Does postoperative radiotherapy (PORT) improve survival or regional control in early (pT1-2) oral SCC with a single ipsilateral node metastasis and no extranodal extension?
Key Findings:
  • 43/85 received PORT; poor differentiation, high-risk margins and bilateral neck dissection predicted PORT use.
  • At 60 months, OS (65% PORT vs 63% surgery only) and regional recurrence (25% vs 30%) were similar. Adjusted HR for OS was 0.94 (95% CI 0.46-1.92); for regional recurrence-free survival 0.81 (0.30-2.17).
  • Exploratory subgroups suggested possible PORT benefit in pT2 disease, age <70 years, and inadequate neck dissection (<18 nodes).
Clinical Relevance: Supports considering observation over PORT in carefully selected pT1-2 pN1 patients with adequate neck dissection, relevant to MDT discussions given conflicting guidance and PORT morbidity.
Limitations: Small, non-randomised cohort with confounding by indication and wide confidence intervals, leaving it underpowered to exclude a clinically meaningful PORT benefit.

International Journal of Oral and Maxillofacial Surgery

Stability and outcome after two approaches for counterclockwise mandibular setback in patients with skeletal class III high-angle malocclusion: a prospective study

Lin LL, Chen YF, Chen YA et al. · 2026 Oct 24
Study Type: Prospective comparative cohort study (non-randomised, n = 56)
Key Question: Do posterior-shortening and posterior-lengthening approaches to counterclockwise mandibular setback differ in stability and outcome in surgery-first bimaxillary surgery for skeletal class III high-angle malocclusion?
Key Findings:
  • Both groups (posterior-shortening n = 25; posterior-lengthening n = 31) showed early relapse in mandibular vertical translation and pitch at 1 week, and significant sagittal mandibular relapse by post-treatment.
  • Most post-treatment outcomes were similar between groups, but posterior-shortening had significantly smaller maxillary and mandibular projection and upper incisor angle.
  • Baseline differed: the posterior-shortening group had more gummy smile and lower projection and overbite. Effect sizes and follow-up duration are not reported in the abstract.
Clinical Relevance: Suggests either approach is viable in high-angle class III cases, but surgeons should counsel on sagittal relapse and select the approach according to baseline skeletal and soft-tissue features.
Limitations: Groups were not randomised and differed at baseline, so approach selection and outcome differences are confounded.

International Journal of Oral and Maxillofacial Surgery

Accuracy of augmented reality-assisted versus template-guided implant surgery: an in-vitro comparative study

Bosshard FA, Di Giovanni TM, Schaufelberger L et al. · 2026 Oct 24
Study Type: In-vitro comparative study (80 implants in 20 maxillary bone models, intra-model comparison).
Key Question: Is augmented reality (Hololens 2)-assisted implant placement as accurate as conventional template-guided placement?
Key Findings:
  • Angular deviation was lower with AR: mean 1.48° (±1.45) versus 2.65° (±1.29) for templates (P < 0.001).
  • Depth deviation did not differ: -0.07 mm (±0.59) AR versus -0.08 mm (±0.67) template (P = 0.6).
  • Accuracy was assessed by superimposing postoperative CBCT on the preoperative plan. The authors concluded both methods were highly precise.
Clinical Relevance: AR may offer a template-free route to guided implant placement, but this is early-stage evidence with no clinical data yet relevant to NHS practice.
Limitations: In-vitro bone models without soft tissue, saliva, patient movement or restricted access, so findings cannot be extrapolated to clinical performance; the abstract also does not report operator experience or whether the analysis accounted for clustering of implants within models.

International Journal of Oral and Maxillofacial Surgery

Contralateral condylar remodeling following unilateral total temporomandibular joint replacement

Yuan M, Zhu Z, Li H et al. · 2026 Oct 27
Study Type: Retrospective cohort study (n=17), CT-based 3D volumetric analysis.
Key Question: Does the contralateral natural condyle undergo remodeling after unilateral total TMJ replacement, and is the prosthetic condyle positionally stable?
Key Findings:
  • Contralateral condylar volume fell by a mean 139 mm³ (SD 98.9; P<0.001), approximately 9.3%, at a mean follow-up of 13.2 months.
  • Resorption was localised to the posterior and superior condylar surfaces; simultaneous orthognathic surgery did not alter the remodeling trend.
  • Prosthetic condyle displacement was <1 mm in all directions, indicating good positional stability.
Clinical Relevance: Unilateral TMJR may impose altered loading on the remaining native joint, which is relevant to case selection, counselling and long-term surveillance of the contralateral side in NHS TMJR pathways.
Limitations: Small, young cohort with short follow-up (about 1 year), no control group and no reported clinical correlation (pain, function, occlusion), so the significance and progression of the resorption remain unclear.

Journal of Cranio-Maxillofacial Surgery

Contributions of Oral and Maxillofacial Surgeons in multidisciplinary craniofacial syndrome management: a structured review

Spagnol G, Dos Santos PAF, Nogueira LM et al. · 2026 Oct 02
Study Type: Structured scoping review (JBI methodology, PRISMA-ScR), narrative synthesis
Key Question: What is the evidence base for OMFS-led surgical management of syndromic craniofacial conditions within multidisciplinary teams?
Key Findings:
  • 40 studies (1975-2026) were included from 274 screened records, covering syndromic craniosynostosis, Pierre Robin sequence, Treacher Collins syndrome and hemifacial microsomia.
  • Outcomes were generally favourable, but evidence was mostly retrospective case series and case reports, with inconsistent outcome measures and little long-term or patient-reported data.
  • Timing of intervention and distraction osteogenesis versus conventional osteotomy remain unresolved, with conflicting comparative data.
Clinical Relevance: Offers a mapped overview of OMFS contributions, particularly skeletal reconstruction and airway management, but gives no comparative evidence to guide technique or timing in UK craniofacial networks, where OMFS roles and team leadership vary.
Limitations: Evidence is predominantly low-level observational data, and no quality appraisal or quantitative synthesis is described, so conclusions on effectiveness are limited.

Journal of Cranio-Maxillofacial Surgery

Accuracy of maxillary positioning in semi-guided orthognathic surgery using 3D-printed cutting guides and bending plates: A retrospective study

Beyer M, Waldvogel OV, Alig L et al. · 2026 Oct 23
Study Type: Single-centre retrospective comparative study (n = 19 bimaxillary cases; comparator group size not stated in abstract).
Key Question: Can a semi-guided workflow (3D-printed cutting guides plus pre-bent plates) transfer virtual surgical planning for Le Fort I maxillary positioning as accurately as conventional splint-based surgery?
Key Findings:
  • Osteotomy deviation ranged 0.09–1.34 mm (mean 0.56 mm).
  • No significant differences in maxillary positioning versus the splint workflow, apart from greater absolute vertical deviation in the semi-guided group, judged of limited clinical relevance (magnitude not reported).
  • Mean operative time was 28.8 minutes shorter with the semi-guided approach, but this was not statistically significant (p = 0.173).
Clinical Relevance: A hybrid approach may offer a cheaper, faster alternative to splints or full PSI, relevant to NHS units weighing planning and manufacturing costs, though no cost data were presented.
Limitations: Small, retrospective, single-centre sample with no randomisation, so allocation bias and underpowering (particularly for operative time) cannot be excluded.

Journal of Cranio-Maxillofacial Surgery

Biomechanical and fatigue analysis of a porous titanium total temporomandibular joint prosthesis with different mandibular component lengths and designs

Gerçek AO, Meral SE, Tüz HH · 2026 Oct 24
Study Type: Finite element analysis (computational biomechanical study); no clinical or in vivo data.
Key Question: How do Voronoi lattice filling ratio (25–75% vs solid) and ramus length (mini, standard, extended) affect stress distribution and fatigue life in a porous titanium total TMJ prosthesis?
Key Findings:
  • Ramus von Mises stress fell with increasing filling ratio, from 363.5 MPa (standard, 25% lattice) to 71.3 MPa (extended, solid); all designs showed infinite fatigue life (safety factors 1.5–7.8).
  • Lower filling ratios transferred more load to bone, potentially reducing stress shielding; bone stress stayed within physiological limits.
  • Fossa and UHMWPE stresses rose with filling ratio, and UHMWPE stress exceeded polyethylene yield strength in every model.
Clinical Relevance: Lattice-structured, potentially patient-specific TMJ ramus components may support bone-sparing designs, but this remains a bench-level observation with no bearing yet on current NHS practice.
Limitations: Single-load simulation (unilateral molar clenching) using one anatomical dataset, with no experimental, biological, or clinical validation; the UHMWPE yield exceedance is unresolved.
