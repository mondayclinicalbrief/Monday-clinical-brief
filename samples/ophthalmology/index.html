Hydrus microstent combined with cataract surgery or used alone: cost-utility analysis for UK

Gazzard G, King A, Rhee DJ et al. · 2026 Oct 21
Study Type: Cost-utility analysis (cohort Markov model, 15-year horizon, UK public payer perspective).
Key Question: Is the Hydrus Microstent cost-effective in UK practice, combined with cataract surgery (CS) versus CS alone, or as standalone surgery versus iStent, in primary open angle glaucoma (POAG)?
Key Findings:
  • Hydrus + CS versus CS alone: +0.334 QALYs at +£604 over 15 years; probability of cost-effectiveness 87.6% in probabilistic analysis.
  • Hydrus versus iStent (standalone): +0.068 QALYs with £534 projected savings; probability of cost-effectiveness ~71.5%.
  • Results were consistent across scenario analyses.
Clinical Relevance: Supports commissioning arguments for Hydrus in mild-to-moderate POAG undergoing CS, though cost-effectiveness thresholds (e.g. NICE) are not stated in the abstract.
Limitations: Progression inputs derive from HORIZON and COMPARE trial data (including extrapolation of rate of progression) and are likely manufacturer-linked; the model is only as robust as these assumptions, and the abstract does not report a sensitivity analysis testing them.

JAMA Ophthalmology

Incidence and Risk Factors of Recurrent Visual Field Progression in Glaucoma

Malek DA, da Costa DR, Scherer R et al. · 2026 Oct 22
Study Type: Retrospective single-centre cohort study (Bascom Palmer registry, 7,457 eyes, mean follow-up 12.4 years)
Key Question: What is the incidence of, and what factors predict, recurrent visual field progression after an initial GPA likely-progression event in glaucoma?
Key Findings:
  • Among 635 eyes with adequate post-event data, 26.3% recurred; cumulative recurrence was 13.8% at 3 years and 43.5% at 10 years.
  • Independent predictors: faster pre-event MD loss (HR 1.73 per dB/y), higher post-event mean IOP (HR 1.09 per mmHg), less advanced MD at first event (HR 1.08 per dB), and older age (HR 1.11 per 5 years).
  • Each 10% greater IOP reduction lowered recurrence hazard (HR 0.89, 95% CI 0.83–0.94); recurrence was 42.7% vs 23.4% in eyes with pre-event slopes faster vs slower than -1.0 dB/y.
Clinical Relevance: Supports risk-stratifying post-progression follow-up, with tighter IOP targets and closer field testing for fast progressors, which is relevant to constrained UK glaucoma clinic capacity.
Limitations: Only 635 of 1,951 progressing eyes (about a third) were analysed, so selection bias is likely, and associations with IOP may be confounded by indication in this observational design.

American Journal of Ophthalmology

Diagnostic Accuracy of Paracentral Acute Middle Maculopathy for Arteritic Anterior Ischemic Optic Neuropathy

Molleti S, Lang MV, Owosela B et al. · 2026 Oct 29
Study Type: Retrospective, multicentre diagnostic accuracy study (three US centres, 2020–2025).
Key Question: Can paracentral acute middle maculopathy (PAMM) on macular OCT help distinguish arteritic from non-arteritic anterior ischaemic optic neuropathy (AAION vs NAION)?
Key Findings:
  • 119 patients (27 AAION, 92 NAION); PAMM present in 18.5% of AAION vs 5.4% of NAION (p = 0.032).
  • Sensitivity 19% (95% CI 8–37%); specificity 94.6% (88–98%); PPV 50% (24–76%); NPV 79.8% (71–86%).
  • Three of five NAION cases with PAMM followed severe systemic hypoperfusion.
Clinical Relevance: PAMM on OCT should heighten suspicion of GCA-related disease but cannot exclude it when absent, so it must not delay urgent GCA pathway assessment or treatment.
Limitations: Small AAION sample with wide confidence intervals and a retrospective design, which limits generalisability and precision of the estimates.

American Journal of Ophthalmology

Association Between Systemic TNF-α Inhibitor Use and Risk of Primary Open-Angle Glaucoma

Hong AT, Lin F, Xu BY et al. · 2026 Oct 22
Study Type: Retrospective multicentre cohort study (propensity-matched, US aggregated EHR data, 70 organisations)
Key Question: Is systemic TNF-α inhibitor use associated with altered 10-year risk of incident primary open-angle glaucoma?
Key Findings:
  • Among 11,915 matched pairs (age ≥40, uveitis excluded), 10-year POAG diagnosis was lower in TNF-α inhibitor users: 2.0% vs 3.3% (HR 0.70, 95% CI 0.59–0.82).
  • Significant reductions were seen for adalimumab (HR 0.74, 0.57–0.96) and infliximab (HR 0.68, 0.47–0.97); no significant differences between agents on pairwise comparison.
  • Etanercept, certolizumab and golimumab results were not reported in the abstract.
Clinical Relevance: Hypothesis-generating support for an inflammatory component in glaucomatous neurodegeneration, but it should not alter UK practice or influence prescribing decisions for rheumatology, gastroenterology or dermatology patients.
Limitations: Reliance on ICD-10 coding for outcomes and retrospective EHR data leaves the study open to detection bias, residual confounding (e.g., steroid exposure, disease severity, ophthalmic screening frequency in biologic users) and misclassification, with no IOP or structural data.
