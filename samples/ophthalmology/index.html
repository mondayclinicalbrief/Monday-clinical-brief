Demographic Disparities in the Likelihood of Surgical Repair Following Diagnosis of Rhegmatogenous Retinal Detachment

Babu A, Kim SB, Kaelber DC et al. · 2026 Jul 21
Study Type: Retrospective cohort study (large US electronic health record database, propensity score matched)
Key Question: Do demographic and socioeconomic factors affect the likelihood and timeliness of surgical repair following diagnosis of rhegmatogenous retinal detachment (RRD)?
Key Findings:
  • Males were significantly more likely than females to undergo surgery from within 1 day (RR 1.20, 95% CI 1.15–1.24) to 3 months (RR 1.19, 95% CI 1.15–1.23) post-diagnosis.
  • White patients were more likely than Black patients to receive timely repair, from 1 day (RR 1.47, 95% CI 1.35–1.61) to 3 months (RR 1.36, 95% CI 1.26–1.46); no significant difference between White vs Asian or Hispanic vs non-Hispanic groups.
  • Patients without socioeconomic/psychosocial conditions were markedly more likely to receive prompt surgery (RR 3.35 at 1 day; RR 2.83 at 3 months), with compounded disparities in Black and female patients with such conditions.
Clinical Relevance: Highlights inequities in access to timely, sight-saving RRD repair that UK ophthalmology services should consider when auditing referral pathways and addressing health inequalities under NHS equity frameworks, despite differing healthcare system structures.
Limitations: US-based EHR data with insurance/access differences not directly generalisable to the NHS, and residual confounding despite propensity matching cannot be excluded.

American journal of ophthalmology

Non-infectious Uveitis is Associated With Increased Psychiatric Comorbidities: An All of Us research program Analysis

Marella P, Subramanian V, Habiel M · 2026 Jul 21
Study Type: Retrospective cross-sectional matched case-control study (All of Us database)
Key Question: Is non-infectious uveitis (NIU) associated with increased psychiatric comorbidity burden compared with matched controls, and are patients adequately screened for this?
Key Findings:
  • NIU patients (n=3,266) had significantly higher odds of mood disorder (46.4%, OR 3.31), depression (44.0%, OR 3.34), anxiety (44.3%, OR 3.13), personality disorder (OR 3.74), insomnia (OR 2.93), substance abuse (OR 2.34), schizophrenia (OR 2.38), bipolar disorder (OR 2.16), and opioid abuse (OR 2.06) versus matched controls (all P<0.05).
  • Associations for depression, anxiety, and substance/opioid abuse were stronger in Black than White participants.
  • PHQ-2 and PHQ-9 screening uptake was low (4.8% and 1.4% respectively) despite this elevated risk.
Clinical Relevance: UK ophthalmologists managing NIU should consider incorporating routine mental health screening (e.g., PHQ-2) into uveitis clinics and facilitating timely psychiatry/psychology referral, particularly given health inequality implications relevant to NHS equity priorities.
Limitations: Cross-sectional design using coded EHR data limits causal inference and may underestimate true comorbidity prevalence due to diagnostic coding variability.

American journal of ophthalmology

The Stalagmite Sign: A Novel OCT-IR Biomarker for Peripheral Infectious Retinitis

Lasave AF, Schlaen A, Colombero DN et al. · 2026 Jul 21
Study Type: Multicenter retrospective case series with diagnostic accuracy analysis
Key Question: Can a novel multimodal OCT-IR imaging pattern ("Stalagmite Sign") reliably identify infectious peripheral retinitis in patients with posterior uveitis?
Key Findings:
  • The sign showed high specificity (99.1%, 95% CI 96.7–99.7%) and PPV (95.7%) for infectious retinitis, with LR+ 24.5, but low sensitivity (22.9%).
  • Toxoplasmosis (69.6%) was the commonest cause; 44/46 sign-positive eyes had confirmed infection, and PCR was positive in all 18 tested eyes.
  • BCVA improved significantly after antimicrobial therapy (logMAR 0.64→0.43, p<0.001), with deposit resolution within 3 months.
Clinical Relevance: For UK uveitis services, recognising this sign on routine SD-OCT/IR imaging could rapidly rule-in infectious aetiology, guiding urgent PCR testing and avoiding harmful corticosteroid monotherapy before confirming diagnosis.
Limitations: Retrospective design and low sensitivity limit its use as a standalone screening tool—absence of the sign does not exclude infection.

American journal of ophthalmology

Comparison of Retinal Nonperfusion in Diabetic Macular Edema Treated with Brolucizumab Versus Aflibercept: A KINGFISHER Trial Analysis

Alhelaly M, Abbasgholizadeh R, Chung YC et al. · 2026 Jul 23
Study Type: Post hoc analysis of a multicentre randomised clinical trial (KINGFISHER)
Key Question: Does monthly brolucizumab versus aflibercept differentially affect longitudinal retinal nonperfusion in DME, as measured by ultrawidefield fluorescein angiography (UWF-FA)?
Key Findings:
  • In 91 evaluable eyes (59 brolucizumab, 32 aflibercept), nonperfusion index (NPI) showed no statistically significant change from baseline to week 52 in posterior, peripheral, or panretinal zones (p>0.05) with either agent.
  • Slight numerical increases in NPI were observed at week 52, but linear mixed-effects modelling found no significant difference in longitudinal NPI trajectories between treatment groups (p>0.05).
Clinical Relevance: Suggests anti-VEGF therapy, regardless of agent, may not reverse established retinal nonperfusion in DME, informing counselling on treatment goals and monitoring expectations in UK diabetic eye services.
Limitations: Small subgroup with baseline UWF-FA (91/517 participants), with substantial exclusions for laser scars and poor image quality, limiting generalisability and statistical power.

American journal of ophthalmology

Greater exposed neural canal elongation of the myopic optic nerve head is associated with papillomacular bundle defects in glaucoma

Lee EJ, Han JC, Park DY · 2026 Jul 24
Study Type: Retrospective cross-sectional study
Key Question: Does the degree of exposed neural canal (ENC) elongation at the optic nerve head, as measured by OCT, correlate with papillomacular bundle (PMB) defects in myopic eyes with normal-tension glaucoma?
Key Findings:
  • PMB defects occurred in 36.6% of eyes (37/101); longer externally oblique border tissue/choroidal border tissue length ratios and lower BMO-ASCO/BMO-SFO conjugate area ratios were independently associated with PMB defects after adjusting for age and axial length (all P<.05).
  • PMB defects were present in 75.0% of eyes with EOBT ratio ≥0.5 and 66.7% of eyes with BMO-SFO area ratio <0.5, indicating a dose-dependent relationship.
  • ENC elongation was most pronounced temporally/inferotemporally, matching the PMB trajectory.
Clinical Relevance: These OCT-derived structural markers may help UK clinicians identify myopic glaucoma patients at higher risk of PMB damage, which threatens central vision and is easily missed with standard perimetric strategies.
Limitations: Retrospective, cross-sectional design precludes causal inference regarding ENC elongation preceding PMB defect development.

American journal of ophthalmology

Factors Impacting Ophthalmology Interest and Perception Among U.S. Medical Students

Xu C, Ponzini M, Hou MY et al. · 2026 Jul 24
Study Type: Retrospective cross-sectional survey study (AAMC MSQ and GQ data, 2013–2022)
Key Question: What personal values, experiences, and resources are associated with U.S. medical students' decision to pursue ophthalmology?
Key Findings:
  • Matriculating students interested in ophthalmology valued high income (OR 1.46) and work-life balance (OR 1.44) more, but social change (OR 0.76), leadership (OR 0.94), and creativity (OR 0.94) less than peers.
  • Graduating students pursuing ophthalmology prioritised salary (OR 1.73), family considerations (OR 1.31), and work-life balance (OR 2.40); they were far more likely to have engaged in research (OR 4.75).
  • Ophthalmology-bound students relied more on web resources, group panels, and electives for specialty exposure, but not mentorship or formal AAMC/school workshops.
Clinical Relevance: UK ophthalmology training programmes and widening-participation initiatives may need to rethink outreach strategies—emphasising creativity, innovation, and mentorship—to attract a broader, more motivated applicant pool amid similar workforce diversity concerns within NHS specialty recruitment.
Limitations: Self-reported, U.S.-centric survey data with associative (not causal) design limits direct applicability to UK medical education and training pathways.

American journal of ophthalmology

Cultured Human Corneal Endothelial Cell Injection Therapy in Phakic Eyes with Bullous Keratopathy

Ogino R, Kobayashi A, Shirane M et al. · 2026 Jul 24
Study Type: Retrospective interventional case series (multicentre)
Key Question: Is cultured human corneal endothelial cell (cHCEC) injection therapy safe and effective for phakic eyes with bullous keratopathy, as a lens-sparing alternative to endothelial keratoplasty?
Key Findings:
  • Mean logMAR BCVA improved from 1.06 to 0.22 at 6 months (change -0.84; 95% CI -1.32 to -0.36; P=.008); CCT decreased from 782 to 578 µm (P=.005).
  • Postoperative ECD ranged 693–1622 cells/mm² (unmeasurable preoperatively due to oedema); corneal clarity improved in all 5 eyes and remained stable.
  • No cataract progression, cystoid macular oedema, or significant IOP change (P=.511) over 6 months.
Clinical Relevance: Offers a potential lens-preserving alternative to endothelial keratoplasty for phakic BK patients, relevant given NHS interest in minimising cataract surgery burden and preserving accommodation in younger patients.
Limitations: Very small sample (n=5) with short follow-up (6 months) limits generalisability and assessment of long-term durability/safety.

Cost-Effectiveness of Photobiomodulation for Intermediate Dry Age-Related Macular Degeneration: A Model-Based Analysis Using the LIGHTSITE III 24-Month Trial

Watane A, Witkin A, Heier J et al. · 2026 Jul 25
Study Type: Model-based cost-effectiveness analysis (Markov model using trial-derived data)
Key Question: Is photobiomodulation (PBM) cost-effective for intermediate dry AMD from a US healthcare payer perspective?
Key Findings:
  • Base-case ICER of $73,910/QALY over 10 years (5.244 discounted QALYs, incremental cost $29,556), below US $100,000–150,000 willingness-to-pay thresholds.
  • Sensitivity analyses gave ICERs from $36,700 to $117,694/QALY; only a $5,000/course scenario exceeded thresholds.
  • Benefit driven by a 71.7% relative reduction in incident geographic atrophy at 24 months; projected 5-year US national budget impact was $25.0 billion at 20% uptake.
Clinical Relevance: While US-focused, this economic modelling could inform future NHS/NICE cost-effectiveness appraisals of PBM as a novel, non-invasive intervention for intermediate dry AMD, a condition with no currently approved UK treatment to prevent progression to geographic atrophy.
Limitations: The analysis relies on a single trial's 24-month GA outcomes and modelled utility mapping (rather than prospective EQ-5D data), and uses US cost structures not directly transferable to the NHS.
