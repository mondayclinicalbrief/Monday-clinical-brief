Annals of Intensive Care

Outcomes of patients with amyotrophic lateral sclerosis admitted to the ICU for acute respiratory failure

Celier A, Lanore A, Hesters A et al. · 2026
Study Type: Retrospective cohort study (two ICUs, single tertiary centre, 2016–2022; n=139)
Key Question: What are the outcomes of patients with ALS admitted to ICU with acute respiratory failure, and which admission factors predict mortality?
Key Findings:
  • Mortality was 48.2% at 3 months, rising to 61.2% at 6 months and 74.1% at 12 months. Most patients (79.9%) received non-invasive ventilation; 13.7% were intubated and 2.2% underwent tracheostomy.
  • Hypoxaemia at admission (PaO₂/FiO₂ <300) independently predicted 3-month mortality (HR 2.27, 95% CI 1.28–4.01); older age was also associated (HR 1.03 per year).
  • Among 3-month survivors, 79.2% were living at home.
Clinical Relevance: Admission hypoxaemia may help inform early prognostic discussions and shared decision-making when ALS patients present to UK critical care, though ICU admission is not futile for all.
Limitations: Single-centre retrospective design with no ALS-specific disease severity or functional baseline data reported in the abstract, limiting generalisability and confounder adjustment.

Annals of Intensive Care

Innate immune response in COVID-19 pneumonia during the pandemic: a prospective cohort study

Chachali SP, Foutadakis S, Antonakos N et al. · 2026
Study Type: Prospective cohort study (two tertiary hospitals, Athens, Greece; n=130)
Key Question: How do ex vivo innate immune responses differ between COVID-19 pneumonia and non-COVID-19 CAP, and do they vary across pandemic periods or relate to severe respiratory failure (SRF) and mortality?
Key Findings:
  • PBMCs from COVID-19 patients (n=102) produced more TNFα and IL-6, but less IL-1RA, than those from non-COVID-19 CAP (n=28) on Day 1.
  • Omicron-infected patients showed higher TNFα and lower IL-6 production than earlier variants.
  • On multivariate analysis, a fall in PBMC IL-6 production over time was associated with progression to SRF and 28-day mortality (effect sizes not reported in abstract). Serum microRNA findings were exploratory and from a small subset.
Clinical Relevance: Findings suggest declining functional IL-6 responsiveness may flag deterioration, but this is a research observation with no bedside application or UK treatment implications yet.
Limitations: Small non-COVID comparator group, single-country setting and ex vivo assays limit generalisability, and the abstract lacks effect sizes and confidence intervals.

Annals of Intensive Care

Early sitting within 72 hours in the intensive care unit and post-intensive care outcomes: a multicenter cohort study

Watanabe S, Iida Y, Yanagita Y et al. · 2026
Study Type: Multicentre observational cohort study (Japan), with overlap propensity score weighting.
Key Question: Is sitting at the bedside within 72 hours of ICU admission associated with death or functional decline at 3 months post-discharge in mechanically ventilated survivors receiving rehabilitation?
Key Findings:
  • Of 423 patients, 173 sat early. No association with the primary outcome of death or functional decline (aRR 0.94, 95% CI 0.80–1.09).
  • Exploratory outcomes favoured early sitting for readmission or death (aRR 0.53, 95% CI 0.32–0.88; BH-adjusted p = 0.027), but this attenuated after adjusting for Day-3 clinical status. The cognitive impairment signal did not survive multiplicity adjustment or a stricter definition.
Clinical Relevance: A 72-hour sitting milestone should not be treated as a validated target for UK early mobilisation pathways, although sitting appears feasible in clinically stable patients.
Limitations: Residual confounding by clinical stability is likely, and the exploratory findings probably reflect patient selection rather than any effect of sitting.

Annals of Intensive Care

Phenotypic characterization of patients with ANCA-associated vasculitis and diffuse alveolar hemorrhage: a data-driven analysis of a multicentre, retrospective, nationwide cohort

Gendreau S, Sanna AC, Desnos C et al. · 2026
Study Type: Retrospective multicentre cohort study (ancillary unsupervised machine-learning analysis of the national PLEXHIA cohort, France)
Key Question: Can distinct clinical phenotypes be identified among ICU patients with severe ANCA-associated vasculitis (AAV) and diffuse alveolar haemorrhage (DAH), and do they differ in mortality?
Key Findings:
  • Among 197 patients (2012-2024), Kohonen self-organising maps identified three clusters using admission variables only: MPO pulmonary-renal (MPO-PR), mild-respiratory (Mild-R), and PR3 multi-organ (PR3-MO; younger, severe respiratory failure, multiorgan failure).
  • Organ support differed: RRT was most frequent in MPO-PR (73%); invasive ventilation in MPO-PR (71%) and PR3-MO (68%).
  • 30-day mortality differed significantly: 23% (MPO-PR), 14% (PR3-MO), 4% (Mild-R).
Clinical Relevance: Early phenotyping at ICU admission may help UK intensivists and rheumatology/nephrology teams anticipate organ support needs and prognosis, and could inform future phenotype-targeted immunosuppression trials.
Limitations: Retrospective design with unsupervised clustering and no external validation, so phenotypes are hypothesis-generating and cannot guide treatment (90-day outcomes and cluster sizes not reported in the abstract).

Annals of Intensive Care

Dynamics of ventriculo-arterial coupling and peripheral tissue perfusion during early resuscitation of septic shock

Caicedo Ruiz JD, García Gallardo G, Aldana JL et al. · 2026
Study Type: Prospective observational cohort study (single-cohort, n=84).
Key Question: Do dynamic changes in left ventriculo-arterial coupling (LVAC) correlate with changes in capillary refill time (CRT) during the first 6 hours of septic shock resuscitation?
Key Findings:
  • CRT change differed across LVAC trajectory groups (p=0.013): improved with uncoupled-to-coupled transition (−33.8%) and worsened with coupled-to-uncoupled transition (+17.5%); persistently coupled/uncoupled patients showed heterogeneous CRT responses.
  • In uncoupled patients, CRT fell after dobutamine (−26.2%, p=0.007) but not after fluids (−9.5%, p=0.656), although both raised stroke volume by ~14%. CRT was unchanged in already-coupled patients.
  • The authors conclude the LVAC-CRT relationship is weak, with peripheral perfusion often evolving independently of cardiac-arterial energy transmission.
Clinical Relevance: Supports a multimodal approach to resuscitation: improving stroke volume or LVAC alone does not reliably restore peripheral perfusion, so CRT-guided assessment (already familiar from ANDROMEDA-SHOCK) should not be replaced by macrohaemodynamic targets.
Limitations: Small observational sample with few intervention events (31 fluid, 32 inotrope), limiting causal inference and subgroup power.

Annals of Intensive Care

Permissive versus conventional weaning from venovenous ECMO in patients with ARDS: a retrospective cohort study

Moyon Q, Benitha Y, Harba C et al. · 2026
Study Type: Retrospective single-centre cohort study (landmark Cox and competing-risk analyses)
Key Question: Is decannulating from V-V ECMO despite persistently impaired respiratory mechanics on sweep gas-off trial ("permissive weaning") associated with worse outcomes than conventional weaning in ARDS?
Key Findings:
  • Of 280 successfully decannulated patients, 117 (42%) had permissive weaning. This group had longer ECMO duration (median 19 vs 12 days).
  • 90-day post-decannulation mortality was higher with permissive weaning (adjusted HR 4.45, 95% CI 1.98-9.97).
  • No difference in liberation from mechanical ventilation by day 90 (adjusted subdistribution HR 0.90, 95% CI 0.70-1.17).
Clinical Relevance: For UK ECMO centres, the findings caution against decannulating on oxygenation and CO₂ clearance alone while plateau/driving pressures, PEEP or tidal volumes remain abnormal, though they cannot establish that delaying decannulation improves outcomes.
Limitations: Retrospective, single-centre design with likely confounding by indication (permissive group had higher BMI, more COVID-19 ARDS and longer support), and only patients successfully decannulated were analysed.

Annals of Intensive Care

The effect of time-varying left ventricular ejection fraction on clinical outcome in critically ill COVID-19 patients

Chew MS, Jansson S, Jozwiak M et al. · 2026
Study Type: Retrospective observational cohort study (pooled data from three ICU repositories; 808 patients, 1614 echocardiograms).
Key Question: Is time-varying left ventricular systolic function (LVEF) independently associated with ICU mortality in critically ill COVID-19 patients?
Key Findings:
  • At baseline, 27% of patients had LVEF ≤50% and 17% had hyperdynamic LV function (LVEF >70%). Abnormal LVEF was most common at the first examination (27%) and least common at the fifth (17%), while hyperdynamic function peaked at the fourth examination.
  • After adjustment (including acute cor pulmonale, LVOT VTI and clinical covariates), neither abnormal LVEF (aHR 1.17, 95% CI 0.90–1.53) nor hyperdynamic LVEF (aHR 1.04, 95% CI 0.79–1.38) was independently associated with ICU mortality.
Clinical Relevance: LVEF phenotype alone appears to add little prognostic information in severe COVID-19 once RV function and forward flow are considered, suggesting echocardiographic assessment should focus on RV function and flow rather than LVEF.
Limitations: Retrospective design with echocardiograms performed at clinician discretion, and LVEF may be too insensitive to detect subtle myocardial injury.

Annals of Intensive Care

The hidden severity of new psychoactive substances: clinical evidence from emergency and intensive care admissions in Taiwan

Liao PH, Lin CC, Chang CM et al. · 2026
Study Type: Multicentre retrospective cohort study (10 hospitals, Taiwan; March 2019–January 2020).
Key Question: Do patients with new psychoactive substance (NPS) poisoning have greater clinical severity than those with traditional illicit drug (TID) poisoning?
Key Findings:
  • Of 1,378 suspected intoxications, 362 (26.3%) were LC-MS/MS-positive: 147 NPS, 130 TID, 85 mixed. Synthetic cathinones and ketamine analogues predominated among NPS.
  • Overall critical outcome (ICU admission or ED death) rate was 18.3%. Adjusted OR for critical illness, NPS vs TID: 0.94 (95% CI 0.51–1.74).
  • No difference in short-term survival across NPS, TID and mixed groups (log-rank p = 0.665).
Clinical Relevance: NPS poisoning carries comparable risk of critical illness to conventional drugs, supporting equivalent vigilance and escalation thresholds in UK EDs and ICUs, where NPS exposure is increasingly encountered.
Limitations: Retrospective design with a wide confidence interval (possibly underpowered), and a composite outcome that does not capture ICU-specific interventions or longer-term mortality; Taiwanese drug patterns may not generalise to the UK.
