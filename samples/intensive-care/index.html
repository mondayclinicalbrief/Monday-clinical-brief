American journal of respiratory and critical care medicine

An Update to the Classification, Evaluation, and Management of Childhood Interstitial Lung Disease in Infancy: An Official American Thoracic Society Clinical Practice Guideline

McGraw MD, DeBoer E, Weinman JP et al. · 2026 Jul 24
Study Type: Clinical practice guideline (GRADE methodology, multidisciplinary panel, ATS-endorsed)
Key Question: How should genetic testing, chest imaging, lung biopsy, and lung transplant referral be used to diagnose and manage childhood interstitial lung disease (chILD) in infants under 2 years?
Key Findings:
  • Rapid, broad genetic testing is strongly recommended for infants with chILD and respiratory failure.
  • Surgical lung biopsy is strongly recommended when genetic testing is unfeasible, delayed, or inconclusive, or to identify treatable histopathological traits or clarify prognosis.
  • Lung transplant referral is strongly recommended for chILD subtypes with predictably poor outcomes; chest CT (initial and repeat) is conditionally recommended to guide diagnosis, prognosis, and treatment changes.
Clinical Relevance: Although chILD is rare, these updated recommendations directly inform neonatal/paediatric intensive care pathways for infants with unexplained respiratory failure, supporting earlier genetic diagnosis and timely transplant referral within UK tertiary paediatric ILD/transplant networks.
Limitations: Recommendations are largely conditional and based on low-certainty evidence given the rarity and heterogeneity of chILD, limiting strength of applicability across all subtypes.

American journal of respiratory and critical care medicine

Macrophage membrane-coated nanoparticles control pathological inflammation and enhance survival in models of severe bacterial pneumonia

Gage H, Hannah SM, Zhou Z et al. · 2026 Jul 24
Study Type: Preclinical laboratory study (in vitro human cell models and in vivo murine pneumonia models)
Key Question: Can macrophage membrane-coated nanoparticles (MΦ-NPs) reduce pathological inflammation and improve outcomes in severe bacterial pneumonia?
Key Findings:
  • MΦ-NPs were cytoprotective and anti-inflammatory in vitro (lung endothelial/epithelial cells, neutrophils) without impairing neutrophil antimicrobial function.
  • In murine Pseudomonas aeruginosa and MRSA pneumonia models, MΦ-NP treatment significantly improved survival, reduced bacterial burden, lowered proinflammatory cytokines, and preserved lung architecture.
  • Proteomics showed suppression of inflammatory, coagulation, and fibrotic pathways linked to poor outcomes in human pneumonia/ARDS.
Clinical Relevance: Offers a potential host-directed adjunct therapy for severe bacterial pneumonia and ARDS, addressing persistent high mortality despite antibiotics—relevant to future NHS critical care sepsis/ARDS management pathways.
Limitations: Findings are limited to in vitro and murine models; human safety, pharmacokinetics, and efficacy data are not yet available.

American journal of respiratory and critical care medicine

ADAMTS14 is a Novel Modulator of Fibroblast Mechanoactivation in Pulmonary Fibrosis

Ganzleben I, Jaiswal A, Yang Y et al. · 2026 Jul 28
Study Type: Basic/translational science study (in vitro human lung fibroblast experiments with patient transcriptomic correlation).
Key Question: Does ADAMTS14 regulate YAP-mediated fibroblast mechanoactivation and matrix remodelling in idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis (IPF)?
Key Findings:
  • siRNA screening identified ADAMTS14 as a driver of YAP nuclear translocation and pro-fibrotic fibroblast activation; ADAMTS14-expressing fibroblasts localised to fibroblastic foci in IPF lung tissue with high collagen synthesis.
  • ADAMTS14 knockdown in IPF-derived fibroblasts reduced pro-fibrotic gene expression and blunted TGFβ responsiveness.
  • Collagen V identified as a key ADAMTS14 substrate; its loss destabilised extracellular matrix, disrupted focal adhesions, and impaired FAK-AKT mechanosignalling.
Clinical Relevance: Identifies a novel ADAMTS14-collagen V-focal adhesion pathway as a potential therapeutic target for IPF, a disease with limited treatment options and high mortality relevant to UK respiratory and ICU practice.
Limitations: Findings are based on in vitro/ex vivo human cell and tissue models without in vivo animal or clinical intervention data to confirm therapeutic relevance.

American journal of respiratory and critical care medicine

MMP12 induces fibrogenic macrophage differentiation to drive granuloma-associated cardiac fibrosis

Mazić M, Lim CX, Saha P et al. · 2026 Jul 29
Study Type: Preclinical mechanistic study (murine model with human tissue/dataset correlation)
Key Question: What macrophage-intrinsic mechanism converts granulomatous inflammation into myocardial fibrosis in cardiac sarcoidosis?
Key Findings:
  • mTORC1 activation in myeloid cells drives TGF-β-dependent differentiation of monocytes into a fibrogenic macrophage (FibMac) population (Cd63+, Spp1+, Gpnmb+, Fabp5+) within cardiac granulomas.
  • MMP12 is both necessary and sufficient for FibMac differentiation and epithelioid clustering in mouse and human macrophages; selective MMP12 inhibition disrupted granuloma structure, reduced fibroblast activation, attenuated fibrosis, and improved cardiac conduction in vivo.
  • MMP12 colocalised with FibMac markers in human cardiac sarcoidosis tissue, and MMP12-expressing fibrogenic macrophages were identified in independent human fibrosis datasets.
Clinical Relevance: Identifies MMP12 as a novel, targetable downstream effector distinct from mTORC1 inhibition, offering a potential therapeutic avenue for cardiac sarcoidosis—a condition managed in UK specialist cardiac/ICU settings where conduction abnormalities and fibrosis carry high mortality risk.
Limitations: Findings derive largely from a genetically engineered mouse model and in vitro/human correlative data, with no clinical trial evidence yet supporting MMP12 inhibition in patients.

American journal of respiratory and critical care medicine

Assessing tissue perfusion during septic shock resuscitation through the vascular waterfall lens

Pinsky MR, Gómez H, Hernandez G · 2026 Jul 29
Study Type: Commentary/conceptual review (physiological reasoning, not original data)
Key Question: How does the "vascular waterfall" concept (critical closing pressure vs mean systemic filling pressure) explain failure of tissue perfusion despite adequate MAP/cardiac output in septic shock, and how might this guide resuscitation?
Key Findings:
  • In vasoplegic septic shock, loss of vasomotor tone can reduce arteriolar critical closing pressure toward mean systemic filling pressure, collapsing the perfusion-driving pressure gradient and abolishing autoregulation independent of MAP or cardiac output.
  • Capillary refill time (CRT) is proposed as the only readily available real-time surrogate for tissue perfusion; a vasopressor test raising MAP >75 mmHg is suggested if CRT remains >3 seconds despite standard resuscitation, continuing only if CRT improves.
  • The paper is conceptual, raising unanswered questions rather than presenting empirical outcome data.
Clinical Relevance: Offers UK intensivists a physiological framework and potential CRT-guided vasopressor titration strategy to avoid both under- and over-resuscitation in septic shock, relevant to individualised haemodynamic management in NHS critical care.
Limitations: This is theoretical/opinion-based reasoning without clinical trial data supporting the proposed vasopressor-test approach.

American journal of respiratory and critical care medicine

Inflammatory Phenotypes In Severe Pneumonia: Clinical Evidence To Mouse Models For Precision Therapeutics

Taenaka H, Evrard B, Maishan M et al. · 2026 Jul 31
Study Type: Translational study combining a human cohort analysis (latent class analysis) with a corresponding mouse model of pneumococcal pneumonia.
Key Question: Do biomarker-defined hyperinflammatory/hypoinflammatory phenotypes seen in human pulmonary sepsis have a reproducible biological correlate in an animal model, and does this predict differential response to anti-inflammatory therapy?
Key Findings:
  • In 548 ICU patients with pulmonary sepsis, LCA identified hyperinflammatory (worse lung injury, higher mortality) and hypoinflammatory (more favourable outcome) phenotypes.
  • Mice with identical pathogen exposure and baseline conditions still segregated into analogous phenotypes with divergent lung injury and mortality trajectories.
  • Dexamethasone and IL-6 receptor blockade improved outcomes only in the hyperinflammatory mouse subgroup, not in hypoinflammatory animals.
Clinical Relevance: Supports a precision-medicine approach to sepsis/ARDS in UK ICUs, suggesting future biomarker-guided trials could restrict anti-inflammatory therapies (e.g., corticosteroids, IL-6 blockade) to hyperinflammatory phenotypes rather than treating all pneumonia patients uniformly.
Limitations: Findings are hypothesis-generating from an animal model and observational human cohort; prospective clinical validation of phenotype-targeted therapy is still required.

Critical care (London, England)

Twenty-five years of septic shock hemodynamic resuscitation trials: a conceptual perspective

Hernandez G, Hunsicker O, de Backer D et al. · 2026 Jul 21
Study Type: Perspective/narrative review (not original research)
Key Question: How has the conceptual and methodological approach to septic shock hemodynamic resuscitation evolved over 25 years of clinical trials?
Key Findings:
  • Early goal-directed therapy targeting fixed global oxygen-delivery endpoints failed to reproduce benefit in later multicentre trials, exposing the limits of rigid, single-target protocols.
  • Subsequent trials incorporated peripheral perfusion assessment, fluid responsiveness testing, echocardiography, and phenotyping; ANDROMEDA-SHOCK demonstrated capillary refill time-guided resuscitation improved outcomes and reduced over-resuscitation compared with lactate-guided care.
  • ANDROMEDA-SHOCK-2 advances this further with a personalised, phenotype-driven strategy using reversible hemodynamic tests and serial reassessment within short decision cycles.
Clinical Relevance: UK intensivists should recognise a paradigm shift away from protocolised, one-size-fits-all resuscitation targets toward individualised, perfusion-guided, dynamically reassessed strategies—relevant to updating local sepsis pathways and NICE/Surviving Sepsis-aligned practice.
Limitations: As a conceptual perspective rather than primary data synthesis, it lacks systematic quality appraisal and quantitative pooling of trial results.

Pressure-targeted neurocritical care at the bedside: the emerging role of transcranial doppler

Melo RH, Robba C · 2026 Jul 22
Study Type: Commentary/viewpoint (not original research)
Key Question: Can transcranial Doppler (TCD) parameters help individualise blood pressure targets in acute brain injury, moving beyond fixed cerebral perfusion pressure thresholds?
Key Findings:
  • Doppler-derived diastolic flow velocity (FVd) and pulsatility index (PI) may reflect changes in cerebrovascular resistance and autoregulatory status during controlled mean arterial pressure manipulation.
  • Tracking these parameters across MAP adjustments may help identify patient-specific pressure ranges associated with more favourable cerebral perfusion.
  • TCD should be integrated with systemic haemodynamic data (cardiac output, fluid responsiveness, cause of hypotension) rather than used in isolation.
Clinical Relevance: Offers UK neurocritical care teams a rationale for incorporating noninvasive, repeatable TCD assessment into individualised blood pressure management for ABI patients, potentially refining practice where invasive multimodal monitoring is unavailable or as an adjunct where it is.
Limitations: This is an expert viewpoint without original clinical data, so proposed TCD-guided targeting requires prospective validation before practice change.
