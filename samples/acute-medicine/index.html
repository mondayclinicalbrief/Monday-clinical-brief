BMJ (Clinical research ed.)

Risk of hair loss associated with glucagon-like peptide-1 receptor agonists in adults with type 2 diabetes: target trial emulation

Tang H, Zhang B, Lu Y et al. · 2026 Jul 22
Study Type: Retrospective cohort study using target trial emulation methodology
Key Question: Is GLP-1 receptor agonist use associated with increased risk of alopecia compared with SGLT-2 inhibitors or DPP-4 inhibitors in adults with type 2 diabetes?
Key Findings:
  • GLP-1 receptor agonist initiation was associated with higher alopecia risk versus SGLT-2 inhibitors (HR 1.37, 95% CI 1.08–1.73) and versus DPP-4 inhibitors (HR 1.68, 95% CI 1.28–2.20).
  • Association was specific to non-scarring alopecia (HR 1.53 and 1.72 respectively), consistent across subgroup/sensitivity analyses, though attenuated after negative control outcome calibration.
Clinical Relevance: GLP-1 receptor agonists are increasingly prescribed in UK primary and secondary care for type 2 diabetes and obesity; clinicians should be aware of hair loss as a possible, low-frequency adverse effect when counselling patients and evaluating new alopecia presentations.
Limitations: Observational design using single-centre US EHR data risks residual confounding and limited generalisability to UK populations, despite negative control calibration.

BMJ (Clinical research ed.)

Minimal detectable change of the Patient Health Questionnaire-9, Patient Health Questionnaire-8, and Patient Health Questionnaire-2: individual patient data meta-analysis

Wang Y, Wu Y, González-Domínguez NP et al. · 2026 Jul 23
Study Type: Individual patient data meta-analysis
Key Question: What score change on the PHQ-9, PHQ-8, and PHQ-2 represents a real clinical change beyond measurement error, and does this vary by patient or setting characteristics?
Key Findings:
  • MDC95 (95% confidence threshold) was 5.72 points for PHQ-9, 5.51 for PHQ-8, and 2.26 for PHQ-2, based on >42,000 participants across 94–98 studies.
  • MDC95 for PHQ-9 was higher in inpatient settings (6.48 points) and increased by 0.40 points per 10% rise in prevalence of major depression in the sample; age and sex had negligible influence.
  • A ~6-point PHQ-9 change is proposed as a pragmatic MDC threshold for general practice; higher thresholds may be needed in specialist mental health settings.
Clinical Relevance: Provides UK acute and general practice clinicians an evidence-based benchmark (~6-point PHQ-9 change) to distinguish genuine clinical improvement/deterioration from measurement noise when monitoring depression, relevant to NHS IAPT and primary care pathways.
Limitations: Included studies were largely non-psychiatric and pre-2018, so generalisability to acute psychiatric or crisis settings may be limited.

BMJ (Clinical research ed.)

Early treatment with rasburicase and risk of kidney replacement therapy and death in adults with tumour lysis syndrome: emulated target trial

Shenoy T, Sanchez-Almanzar D, Dias JA et al. · 2026 Jul 23
Study Type: Retrospective cohort study using emulated target trial methodology (inverse probability of treatment weighting)
Key Question: Does rasburicase given within 12 hours of tumour lysis syndrome (TLS) onset reduce the risk of kidney replacement therapy or death compared with delayed/no treatment?
Key Findings:
  • Early rasburicase (median 5.0 hours post-onset) was associated with lower risk of the composite outcome (kidney replacement therapy or death): 32.7% vs 42.0% (adjusted OR 0.67, 95% CI 0.52–0.88, P<0.001).
  • 90-day mortality was also reduced (adjusted OR 0.71, 95% CI 0.54–0.94).
  • Findings were consistent across sensitivity analyses despite treated patients having higher baseline uric acid.
Clinical Relevance: Supports prompt rasburicase administration in TLS across UK acute medicine and haematology-oncology pathways, reinforcing early recognition and treatment protocols to reduce renal replacement therapy need and mortality.
Limitations: Observational design across US hospitals introduces residual confounding and limits direct causal inference and UK generalisability despite robust statistical adjustment.

European journal of internal medicine

Subclinical hypotension in acute decompensated heart failure: prognosis and treatment implementation

Savonitto G, Stolfo D, Biber ME et al. · 2026 Jul 21
Study Type: Retrospective cohort study with propensity score matching (registry-based)
Key Question: Does subclinical hypotension (SBP ≤100 mmHg) at admission for acute decompensated heart failure predict worse outcomes or limit guideline-directed medical therapy (GDMT)?
Key Findings:
  • Admission hypotension was independently associated with higher 1-year risk of death/HF hospitalisation (HR 2.16, 95% CI 1.47–3.17), persisting at 5 years (HR 1.80, 95% CI 1.32–2.46).
  • Risk association was stronger in women (p-interaction=0.032), with a non-linear increase in risk below ~100 mmHg.
  • Hypotension did not independently reduce GDMT prescription or uptitration in HFrEF; renal dysfunction, treatment-naïve status, and older age were stronger determinants of suboptimal therapy.
Clinical Relevance: In UK acute medicine settings, low but "subclinical" admission SBP should be recognised as a marker of high-risk ADHF phenotype warranting close monitoring, but should not itself be used to justify withholding GDMT optimisation.
Limitations: Retrospective, single-registry design with propensity matching cannot fully exclude residual confounding or account for dynamic in-hospital haemodynamic changes.

European journal of internal medicine

Weight-adjusted fluid balance and its impact on days alive without life support in critical illness - an observational cohort study

Hofer DM, Pitteloud M, Faltys M et al. · 2026 Jul 24
Study Type: Retrospective observational cohort study
Key Question: At what weight-adjusted fluid balance (wFB) threshold does fluid accumulation or depletion in ICU patients begin to impair outcomes, measured as days alive without life support (DAWOLS)?
Key Findings:
  • In 17,956 ICU patients, wFB thresholds of ≥+7% (fluid accumulation) and ≤-4% (fluid depletion) at day 3 were significantly associated with reduced DAWOLS (p<0.001).
  • Fluid accumulation had a stronger negative association with DAWOLS (β -3.92, 95% CI -4.37 to -3.46) than fluid depletion (β -0.95, 95% CI -1.35 to -0.54).
  • Both thresholds were also linked to higher mortality, increased organ support needs, longer ICU/hospital stay, and delirium.
Clinical Relevance: Provides pragmatic, quantifiable wFB targets that could inform fluid stewardship protocols and trigger earlier de-resuscitation discussions in UK critical care units.
Limitations: Single-centre retrospective design limits generalisability and cannot establish causality between fluid balance and outcomes.

European journal of internal medicine

The cardio-hepatic syndrome and right ventricular dysfunction in heart failure with mildly reduced ejection fraction

Schupp T, Johann Steffen H, Abel N et al. · 2026 Jul 24
Study Type: Retrospective cohort study (single-centre, 2016–2022)
Key Question: Do cardio-hepatic syndrome (CHS) and right ventricular dysfunction (RVD) predict adverse outcomes in patients with heart failure with mildly reduced ejection fraction (HFmrEF)?
Key Findings:
  • Among 794 HFmrEF patients, CHS occurred in 20.3% and RVD in up to 20.9%.
  • CHS was not significantly associated with 30-month all-cause mortality (aHR 1.316, 95% CI 0.990–1.751, p=0.059).
  • RVD, defined by TAPSE <17mm (aHR 1.482, 95% CI 1.126–1.951) or TAPSE/PASP <0.36 (aHR 1.566, 95% CI 1.092–2.245), independently predicted mortality; neither CHS nor RVD predicted HF-related rehospitalisation.
Clinical Relevance: Simple echocardiographic RV indices (TAPSE, TAPSE/PASP) may offer more robust prognostic stratification than biochemical cholestasis markers in HFmrEF patients managed in UK acute medicine and cardiology services.
Limitations: Retrospective, single-centre design limits generalisability and causal inference.

European journal of internal medicine

Alcohol withdrawal syndrome in hospitalized patients: a practical review

Caputo F, Lungaro L, Costanzini A et al. · 2026 Jul 25
Study Type: Narrative/practical review (non-systematic)
Key Question: How should internists recognise, risk-stratify, and manage alcohol withdrawal syndrome (AWS) in hospitalised patients, and link this to longer-term alcohol use disorder (AUD) care?
Key Findings:
  • Benzodiazepines remain first-line for moderate-severe AWS; protocol choice (symptom-triggered, fixed-dose, or front-loading) should be tailored to risk profile, comorbidity, and ability to engage with symptom scales.
  • Phenobarbital is gaining use in standardised, closely monitored protocols; clomethiazole and sodium oxybate remain regionally used alternatives in parts of Europe.
  • Early identification of high-risk features, appropriate monitoring level, thiamine/electrolyte correction, and vigilance for delirium tremens/refractory withdrawal are key to safe management.
Clinical Relevance: AWS is a common, high-risk presentation in UK acute medical units, and this review offers practical guidance on monitoring choice, drug selection, and using admission as an opportunity to initiate AUD treatment to reduce readmission and mortality.
Limitations: As a narrative review, it lacks systematic methodology and formal evidence grading, limiting the strength of its recommendations.

Prevalence of opioid-induced constipation in the emergency department: a retrospective study

Kistemaker KRJ, Rakers JFA, Schober P et al. · 2026 Jul 21
Study Type: Retrospective single-centre cohort study
Key Question: What is the prevalence and clinical burden of opioid-induced constipation (OIC) among long-acting opioid users presenting to the ED?
Key Findings:
  • OIC was diagnosed in 6.4% (95% CI 5.4–7.6%) of 2017 long-acting opioid users; nearly half required enemas and 16% needed PAMORAs.
  • Cancer patients had markedly higher odds of OIC (OR 8.81; 95% CI 5.89–13.17); oxycodone use increased risk (OR 1.95; 95% CI 1.30–2.91), while tramadol was associated with lower risk.
  • 23 patients had a 30-day OIC-related ED revisit, and three deaths were attributed to OIC.
Clinical Relevance: OIC represents a measurable and sometimes fatal cause of ED attendance in opioid users, supporting proactive laxative prescribing and heightened vigilance in cancer patients and those on oxycodone within UK acute care pathways.
Limitations: Single-centre retrospective design limits generalisability and may underestimate true OIC prevalence due to reliance on documented diagnoses.
