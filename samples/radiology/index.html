AJR. American journal of roentgenology

Vision Language Models for Ultrasound Assessment of Suspicious Axillary Lymph Nodes in Breast Cancer

He P, Chen C, Dong HD et al. · 2026 Jul 22
Study Type: Retrospective diagnostic accuracy study
Key Question: Can general-purpose vision-language models (VLMs) match radiologists in classifying suspicious axillary lymph nodes as metastatic or nonmetastatic on ultrasound images, using biopsy as reference standard?
Key Findings:
  • Experienced radiologists had the highest accuracy (83%) and specificity (89%), outperforming inexperienced radiologists and all three VLMs.
  • Best-performing VLM (GPT-5.2: 78% accuracy, 76% sensitivity, 80% specificity) was statistically similar in accuracy to inexperienced radiologists but had lower sensitivity and higher specificity; Claude-Opus-4.5 performed worst (67% accuracy).
  • Inexperienced radiologists had highest sensitivity (92%) but poor specificity (64%), reflecting over-calling of malignancy.
Clinical Relevance: As NHS breast units increasingly rely on ultrasound-based axillary nodal assessment to guide individualised surgical management, VLMs could serve as adjunctive tools to reduce false-positive calls by less experienced practitioners, though they cannot yet replace expert radiologist judgement.
Limitations: Single-centre retrospective design using single static images (not real-time scanning), limiting generalisability to routine clinical practice.

Differentiating scirrhous hepatocellular carcinoma from intrahepatic mass-forming cholangiocarcinoma based on Gd-EOB-DTPA-enhanced MRI and serum biomarkers in an HBV-positive high-risk cohort: a multi-center study

He H, Qiu L, Song M et al. · 2026 Jul 20
Study Type: Retrospective multicentre diagnostic accuracy study (training + external validation cohorts)
Key Question: Can Gd-EOB-DTPA-enhanced MRI features combined with serum biomarkers reliably differentiate scirrhous HCC (SHCC) from intrahepatic mass-forming cholangiocarcinoma (IMCC) in HBV-positive patients?
Key Findings:
  • SHCC showed significantly less necrosis than IMCC (training p=0.03; validation p=0.002).
  • A combined model (non-thin rim arterial enhancement, enhancing capsule, opposed-phase T1 signal loss, plus serum biomarkers) achieved AUC 0.951 (sensitivity 85.9%, specificity 88.4%) in training, and AUC 0.939 (sensitivity 85.0%, specificity 88.9%) on external validation.
  • Decision curve analysis confirmed positive net clinical benefit across threshold probabilities.
Clinical Relevance: In HBV-endemic and high-risk surveillance populations, this model could improve preoperative differentiation of two prognostically distinct liver malignancies, supporting more accurate NHS MDT decision-making and treatment planning.
Limitations: Retrospective design with pathological confirmation bias and relatively small SHCC sample size may limit generalisability.
