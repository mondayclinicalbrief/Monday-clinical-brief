British Journal of Radiology

Integrating DWI Parameters with RECIST 1.1 for Early Assessment of Systemic Therapy Response in Lung Cancer Pelvic Bone Metastases

Park H, Lee S, Lee SY et al. · 2026 Oct 19
Study Type: Retrospective single-centre pilot study (78 lesions, 27 patients).
Key Question: Does combining ADC-based DWI response criteria with RECIST 1.1 improve early assessment of systemic therapy response in lung cancer pelvic bone metastases?
Key Findings:
  • ADC thresholds (>1300 µm²/s or ΔADC >0.14) were derived from ROC analysis against later lesion size change, then combined with RECIST as "DWI-RECIST".
  • DWI-RECIST gave modest net reclassification gains (7.4% and 11.1% for the two readers) against RECIST at TP2 as reference. AUC improvements were not significant (0.696 vs 0.618, p=0.472; 0.642 vs 0.618, p=0.781).
  • Interobserver agreement for ADC was good (ICC 0.83–0.86).
Clinical Relevance: Bone metastases are non-measurable under RECIST 1.1 in most NHS practice, so functional DWI markers could aid early response assessment, particularly in lesions classed as stable disease, but this remains hypothesis-generating.
Limitations: Thresholds were derived and tested in the same small cohort without external validation, and RECIST at TP2 is an imperfect reference standard for bone disease.

British Journal of Radiology

Retrospective multi-reader evaluation of a Deep-Learning-based cancer detection model in Contrast-Enhanced Mammography

Moustafa AFI, Jailin C, Vancamberg L et al. · 2026 Oct 19
Study Type: Retrospective, single-centre, multi-reader study with sequential unaided/AI-assisted reads (5 radiologists, 150 CEM examinations, ≥4-week washout)
Key Question: Does a deep-learning CAD tool improve radiologists' diagnostic accuracy and reading efficiency in contrast-enhanced mammography (CEM)?
Key Findings:
  • Lesion-level performance improved for most readers: mean AFROC AUC increase of 0.030 ± 0.018.
  • At 89% sensitivity, false positives fell by a mean of 11.4% ± 24%, with wide inter-reader variability.
  • No improvement at breast level; reading time fell 31% in one radiologist (exploratory, single reader).
Clinical Relevance: CEM is increasingly used in UK practice as an alternative to MRI, and AI support that lowers false positives and reading time could aid workload, though evidence is still early.
Limitations: Small, single-centre retrospective dataset with a single-view tool, an enriched case mix and an efficiency finding from one reader limit generalisability to real-world prospective workflows.

British Journal of Radiology

Why do authors publish radiological multivariable models: Questionnaire survey of motivations, evaluation, and usage in clinical practice

Hameed M, Taylor SA, Mallett S et al. · 2026 Oct 19
Study Type: Cross-sectional online questionnaire survey of authors (small sample, n = 34).
Key Question: Why do radiology authors develop and publish multivariable regression models, and are these models evaluated externally or used clinically?
Key Findings:
  • 49% (17/35) had no intention of external evaluation; main barriers were lack of time (27%), funding (22%) and interest in development alone (19%).
  • 67% (23/34) reported no or little statistical support; 29% (10/34) cited career progression as a motivation for publication.
  • 82% (28/34) did not use their own model clinically, 56% (19/34) believed others would not, and 79% (27/34) did not use models in general.
Clinical Relevance: For UK radiologists appraising or considering adopting published prediction models (including AI-adjacent tools), this suggests many lack external validation or real-world uptake, so critical appraisal of validation status is warranted before clinical use.
Limitations: Very small sample with an unreported response rate, so non-response bias is likely and findings may not generalise to all authors; the authors' conclusion on clinical utility is inferred from self-report rather than directly measured.

British Journal of Radiology

Cytogenetic biodosimetry for radiotherapy: anthropomorphic phantom-based dose-response calibration and in vivo evaluation

Vinnikov V, Zastko L, Klačková A et al. · 2026 Oct 19
Study Type: Proof-of-concept experimental study (in vitro phantom calibration with small prospective patient evaluation, n=12).
Key Question: Can a cytogenetic dose-response curve built in an anthropomorphic phantom predict chromosomal aberration yields in radiotherapy patients after a single fraction?
Key Findings:
  • Phantom-irradiated blood (6 MV photons, single-fraction glioblastoma and breast plans, 2.0 and 2.67 Gy PTV doses) gave a Dic+CR dose-response curve that fitted the linear-quadratic model well.
  • In 12 patients (5 glioblastoma, 7 breast), predicted yields from individual dose-volume data were the same order of magnitude as observed yields, but underestimated them in 10 of 12.
  • Correlation between expected and observed yields was positive but non-significant (r = 0.39, p = 0.21).
Clinical Relevance: Early-stage work toward biological verification of delivered dose in radiotherapy; not applicable to routine NHS practice, but of potential interest for future dose-verification and radiobiological monitoring research.
Limitations: Calibration used blood from a single healthy donor and the patient sample was very small, so in vivo biological variability could not be assessed and the study was underpowered to show correlation.
