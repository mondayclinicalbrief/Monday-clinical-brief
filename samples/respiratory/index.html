American journal of respiratory and critical care medicine

Clinical Utility of Next Generation Sequencing in Concurrent EBUS-TBNA and Liquid Biopsies in NSCLC

Pastis NJ, Fox AH, Ferguson T et al. · 2026 Jul 20
Study Type: Prospective observational cohort study
Key Question: Does concurrent EBUS-TBNA tissue sampling versus liquid biopsy yield different rates of clinically relevant mutation detection by NGS in NSCLC?
Key Findings:
  • Clinically relevant mutations detected more often via EBUS-TBNA (39.7%) than blood (29.6%); yield difference 15.1% (95% CI 5.5–24.2%, p=0.001).
  • Blood-based NGS had moderate sensitivity (0.53) but high specificity (0.95) versus tissue as gold standard; kappa 0.51 indicating moderate agreement.
  • 18.6% of patients had mutations detected only in tissue, but 3.5% had mutations detected only in blood, supporting complementary use.
Clinical Relevance: For UK MDTs managing NSCLC molecular testing pathways, tissue sampling via EBUS-TBNA remains the higher-yield first-line approach, but liquid biopsy can identify additional actionable mutations, supporting a dual-testing strategy where feasible within NHS resource constraints.
Limitations: Single-arm observational design without randomisation limits causal inference, and results may not generalise across differing NGS platforms or turnaround-time/QNS definitions.

American journal of respiratory and critical care medicine

Comparative effectiveness of oral appliances and continuous positive airway pressure: a prospective non-randomized study using a non-inferiority framework

Dieltjens M, Engelen S, Azarbarzin A et al. · 2026 Jul 20
Study Type: Prospective non-randomised crossover study (non-inferiority framework)
Key Question: Is mandibular advancement device (MAD) therapy an effective first-line alternative to CPAP in moderate-to-severe OSA, measured by combined efficacy and adherence?
Key Findings:
  • Mean disease alleviation was similar between MAD (49.9±26.1%) and CPAP (49.1±34.5%), meeting non-inferiority criteria (p=0.4), despite CPAP achieving greater AHI reduction (4.1/h vs 8.4/h, both p<0.001).
  • Nightly adherence was significantly higher with MAD (6.7h vs 5.4h; p<0.05).
  • Patient preference favoured MAD (51%) over CPAP (42%).
Clinical Relevance: Supports considering MAD as a genuine first-line option rather than reserving it for CPAP-intolerant patients, relevant to UK sleep services facing CPAP adherence challenges and capacity pressures.
Limitations: Non-randomised sequential design precludes separating true treatment effect from order/period effects, and MDA is not validated against patient-important clinical outcomes.

American journal of respiratory and critical care medicine

An Update to the Classification, Evaluation, and Management of Childhood Interstitial Lung Disease in Infancy: An Official American Thoracic Society Clinical Practice Guideline

McGraw MD, DeBoer E, Weinman JP et al. · 2026 Jul 24
Study Type: Clinical practice guideline (GRADE methodology, multidisciplinary ATS panel)
Key Question: How should genetic testing, imaging, biopsy, and transplant referral be used in the diagnostic evaluation and management of chILD in infants under 2 years?
Key Findings:
  • Rapid, broad genetic testing is strongly recommended for infants with chILD and respiratory failure.
  • Surgical lung biopsy is strongly recommended when genetic testing is infeasible, delayed, or inconclusive, to detect treatable histopathologic traits or guide prognosis.
  • Chest CT remains conditionally recommended for initial evaluation, with conditional support for repeat CT to inform prognosis/therapy changes; lung transplant referral is strongly recommended for chILD subtypes with predictably poor outcomes.
Clinical Relevance: Provides UK respiratory paediatric and MDT teams with updated, evidence-based pathways to standardise genetic testing, imaging, and biopsy decisions, potentially expediting diagnosis and appropriate transplant referral for this rare, high-mortality-risk population.
Limitations: Recommendations are largely conditional, reflecting low-to-moderate certainty evidence given the rarity and heterogeneity of chILD disorders.

American journal of respiratory and critical care medicine

Macrophage membrane-coated nanoparticles control pathological inflammation and enhance survival in models of severe bacterial pneumonia

Gage H, Hannah SM, Zhou Z et al. · 2026 Jul 24
Study Type: Preclinical laboratory study (in vitro cell models and murine in vivo models)
Key Question: Can macrophage membrane-coated nanoparticles (MΦ-NPs) reduce pathological inflammation and improve outcomes in severe bacterial pneumonia as a host-directed adjunct therapy?
Key Findings:
  • MΦ-NPs protected lung endothelial/epithelial cells and reduced inflammation in vitro without impairing neutrophil antimicrobial function.
  • In murine *Pseudomonas aeruginosa* and MRSA pneumonia models, MΦ-NP treatment significantly improved survival, reduced bacterial burden, lowered proinflammatory cytokines, and preserved lung architecture.
  • Proteomic analysis showed suppression of inflammatory, coagulation, and fibrotic pathways linked to poor outcomes in human pneumonia/ARDS.
Clinical Relevance: This offers early proof-of-concept for a novel host-directed nanotherapeutic that could complement antibiotics in managing severe pneumonia and ARDS, conditions with persistently high mortality in UK ICUs despite optimal antimicrobial care.
Limitations: Findings are based solely on in vitro and murine models, with no human safety, pharmacokinetic, or efficacy data yet available.

Obstructive Sleep Apnea (OSA) in Pregnancy - An American College of Chest Physicians Clinical Practice Guideline

D'Ambrosio CM, Dust C, Dominguez JE et al. · 2026 Jul 21
Study Type: Clinical practice guideline (systematic evidence review with expert panel consensus recommendations)
Key Question: How should obstructive sleep apnoea be screened for, diagnosed, treated, and followed up in pregnant patients?
Key Findings:
  • Nine conditional recommendations issued, all based on low-certainty evidence.
  • Screening recommended via pregnancy-specific questionnaire or standard OSA tool; diagnosis via HST or in-lab PSG if at-risk.
  • Treatment recommended for AHI 5–15 with symptoms/comorbidities, or AHI ≥15 regardless of symptoms, preferentially with APAP; scheduled naps suggested adjunctively; postpartum reassessment advised in select patients.
Clinical Relevance: Provides the first structured, evidence-based framework for managing OSA in pregnancy, relevant to UK obstetric and sleep services where no formal pathway currently exists and antenatal OSA screening is inconsistent.
Limitations: All recommendations are conditional and based on low-certainty evidence, reflecting a paucity of high-quality trials in this population.

Management of Adult Bronchiectasis: An American College of Chest Physicians Clinical Practice Guideline

Thomson R, Thornton C, Aksamit T et al. · 2026 Jul 21
Study Type: Clinical practice guideline (systematic review with GRADE methodology and modified Delphi consensus process)
Key Question: How should adults with bronchiectasis be managed across key domains including antibiotic therapy, airway clearance, anti-inflammatory treatment, hemoptysis, and surgical resection?
Key Findings:
  • Based on 47 studies, the panel issued 13 recommendations covering exacerbation antibiotics (duration/route), long-term suppressive antibiotics, anti-inflammatory therapy, airway clearance techniques, hemoptysis management, and selective surgical resection.
  • All 13 recommendations are conditional and based predominantly on low-certainty evidence, reflecting substantial gaps in the bronchiectasis evidence base.
  • The panel emphasises phenotyping/endotyping to guide individualised therapy and stratify risk.
Clinical Relevance: Provides UK respiratory clinicians with a structured, evidence-graded framework to standardise bronchiectasis management amid variable practice, complementing existing BTS guidance while highlighting research priorities relevant to NHS service planning.
Limitations: Recommendations are conditional and based on low-certainty evidence, limiting the strength of practice change they can support.

Key Question: How should acute pulmonary embolism be diagnosed, classified, and reported on CT and MR angiography to standardise communication and guide management?
Key Findings:
  • Introduces PE-RADS v2026, a standardised lexicon and hierarchical reporting framework for acute PE on CT/MR angiography.
  • Incorporates anatomic clot location and right heart imaging features to support cardiovascular risk stratification.
  • Aims to improve consistency of interpretation and inter-team communication to expedite appropriate treatment triage.
Clinical Relevance: Adoption of PE-RADS could standardise PE reporting across NHS radiology and respiratory/PE response teams, improving consistency in risk stratification and multidisciplinary decision-making for reperfusion versus anticoagulation strategies.

Effectiveness of the adjuvanted RSVPreF3 vaccine in preventing major adverse cardiovascular events and severe exacerbations of chronic obstructive pulmonary disease and asthma among US adults aged ≥60 years

Singer D, Steffens A, La EM et al. · 2026 Jul 23
Study Type: Retrospective cohort study (US claims database, propensity-matched)
Key Question: Does adjuvanted RSVPreF3 vaccination reduce RSV-related major adverse cardiovascular events (MACE) and severe COPD/asthma exacerbations in adults ≥60 years with pre-existing CVD, COPD, or asthma?
Key Findings:
  • In patients with CVD, VE against RSV-related MACE was 65.0% (95% CI 45.4–77.6%) including mortality, and 63.1% (41.8–76.6%) excluding mortality.
  • In patients with COPD, VE against RSV-related severe exacerbations was 74.4% (59.3–83.9%).
  • In patients with asthma, VE against RSV-related severe exacerbations was 61.6% (9.1–83.7%), with a wide confidence interval reflecting smaller event numbers.
Clinical Relevance: Supports UK adoption/prioritisation of RSV vaccination in older adults with cardiorespiratory comorbidities, aligning with JCVI guidance and potential to reduce COPD/asthma exacerbation burden and cardiovascular events in NHS-managed high-risk populations.
Limitations: Observational design using claims data is subject to residual confounding, misclassification of RSV attribution, and limited generalisability beyond the US healthcare/insurance context.
