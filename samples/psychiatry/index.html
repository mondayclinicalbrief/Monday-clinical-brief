JAMA Psychiatry

Mobile Units for Integrated Addiction and HIV Prevention Services for People Who Inject Drugs: The HPTN 094 Randomized Clinical Trial

Shoptaw S, El-Bassel N, Hanscom B et al. · 2026 Nov 04
Study Type: Open-label, two-arm randomised clinical trial (n = 409; five US cities).
Key Question: Does integrated PrEP and MOUD delivery via a mobile unit plus peer navigation improve PrEP and MOUD use in people who inject drugs, compared with peer navigation to existing community services?
Key Findings:
  • No significant benefit on co-primary outcomes at 26 weeks: confirmed PrEP use 5.4% vs 2.9%; MOUD use 14.8% vs 10.7% (intervention vs control). Uptake was low in both arms and remained similar at 52 weeks.
  • HIV acquisition was rare (1 intervention, 2 control).
  • All-cause mortality was lower in the intervention arm (4 vs 13 deaths; HR 0.29, 95% CI 0.1–0.9, P = .03), a secondary outcome the authors say needs replication.
Clinical Relevance: Mobile integrated delivery did not increase MOUD uptake over peer navigation alone, so this model is not clearly supported for UK addiction services, though the mortality signal merits attention given high drug-related death rates.
Limitations: The mortality finding derives from a secondary outcome with few events (17 deaths) and a wide confidence interval, so it is hypothesis-generating only.

American Journal of Psychiatry

Targeted Use of Kudzu for Binge Drinking Among Adults With Alcohol Use Disorder: A Phase 2b Randomized Trial

Santos GM, Coffin P, Dunham A et al. · 2026 Nov 04
Study Type: Phase 2b double-blind, placebo-controlled RCT (n=120, 1:1 randomisation, 12 weeks plus 3-month follow-up).
Key Question: Does as-needed kudzu extract (2 g on days when binge drinking is anticipated), alongside weekly counselling, reduce binge drinking in adults with alcohol use disorder?
Key Findings:
  • ITT analysis: kudzu reduced weekly binge-drinking days versus placebo (IRR 0.73, 95% CI 0.54–0.99); the lower CI bound sits close to 1.
  • As-treated analysis (≥2.7 days/week medication use): stronger effect (IRR 0.58, 95% CI 0.38–0.89), with significant PEth positivity reduction (RR 0.82, 95% CI 0.67–0.99). ITT biomarker results are not reported in the abstract.
  • Reduction in binge-drinking days persisted at 3 months post-treatment (RR 0.68, 95% CI 0.47–0.97). Retention was 88%, with similar adverse event rates between groups and no serious kudzu-related events.
Clinical Relevance: A low-risk, as-needed option for non-abstinent AUD patients could widen treatment choice, but kudzu is not a licensed medicine in the UK and has no NICE recommendation.
Limitations: Modest sample and borderline ITT significance, with primary outcome self-reported; the larger as-treated effect is vulnerable to selection bias.

American Journal of Psychiatry

Changing Patterns of Alcohol and Cannabis Acute Care Admissions by Age in the United States, 2016-2023

Steinle JT, Grucza RA, Buss JL et al. · 2026 Nov 04
Study Type: Retrospective cohort study (US MarketScan Commercial and Multi-State Medicaid claims data, 2016-2023).
Key Question: How have age-specific proportions of alcohol- and cannabis-related emergency department and inpatient admissions changed among 16-64 year olds?
Key Findings:
  • Substance involvement diverged by age: alcohol featured in 19-26% of adolescent encounters versus 73-76% at ages 55-64; cannabis featured in 70-78% of adolescent encounters versus 10-11% at ages 55-64.
  • Alcohol-related encounter proportions rose annually in ages 25-34 (+1.80 percentage points, 95% CI 1.59-2.00) and 35-44 (+1.19, 95% CI 0.99-1.38). Cannabis-related proportions rose most in ages 19-24 (+1.81, 95% CI 1.57-2.04), with increases in psychosis and hyperemesis presentations.
  • For primary diagnoses only, cannabis trends were attenuated, with adolescent declines (-1.46, 95% CI -1.98 to -0.94).
Clinical Relevance: Suggests growing cannabis-related psychiatric and hyperemesis presentations in young people and rising alcohol morbidity in early-to-mid adulthood, which may inform liaison psychiatry and addiction service planning, though US data may not map directly onto the UK.
Limitations: Findings come from insured US populations and are expressed as proportions of substance-related encounters, so changes in one substance's share cannot be read as absolute changes in incidence.

American Journal of Psychiatry

Dose-Response Patterns and Pathways Linking Migration-Related Stigma and Mental Health

Ayón C, Link BG · 2026 Nov 04
Study Type: Cross-sectional survey study (national quota sample, N=908) with multivariable OLS regression.
Key Question: How do macro, interpersonal and intrapersonal migration-related stigma relate to psychological distress and PTSD symptoms in Asian, Black and Latinx immigrant-origin adults in the US?
Key Findings:
  • Racial-ethnic differences in distress and PTSD symptoms were attenuated after adjusting for immigration-related factors and stigma exposure.
  • Interpersonal and intrapersonal stigma (discrimination, social exclusion, shame, perceived devaluation) were the strongest predictors of both outcomes.
  • A cumulative stigma index showed a dose-response pattern: each additional stigma exposure was associated with significantly higher symptom levels. (Effect sizes not reported in abstract.)
Clinical Relevance: Supports routinely asking about discrimination, exclusion and shame in migrant, refugee and asylum-seeker assessments, and trauma-informed, culturally responsive care; findings are US-based and may not transfer directly to UK settings.
Limitations: Cross-sectional design precludes causal or temporal inference, and the quota (non-probability) sample limits generalisability.

American Journal of Psychiatry

Mapping Individual Brain Deviations in ICD-11 Catatonia: Normative Modeling of Structural and Functional MRI Across Two Independent Cohorts

Volkmer S, Kubera KM, Fritze S et al. · 2026 Nov 04
Study Type: Cross-sectional neuroimaging study using hierarchical Bayesian normative modelling, with two independent cohorts (Mannheim, Bern; total N=280).
Key Question: Does ICD-11 catatonia show reproducible individual-level structural and functional brain deviations across mood and schizophrenia spectrum disorders?
Key Findings:
  • Catatonia was associated with enlargement of the third and bilateral lateral ventricles and cortical thinning in the left dorsal posterior cingulate and insula (all q<0.05), replicated across both cohorts.
  • Exploratory analysis suggested a left orbitofrontal difference between mood disorder and schizophrenia-spectrum catatonia; in the overall catatonia comparison this survived correction only in demographically adjusted models.
  • Reduced connectivity between sensorimotor, salience, limbic and executive networks was nominally significant but did not survive multiple-comparison correction.
Clinical Relevance: Findings support catatonia as a transdiagnostic syndrome involving motor-salience circuitry, which may inform future neuromodulation targets, though there is no immediate impact on UK clinical practice.
Limitations: Functional findings did not survive correction, and the cross-sectional design cannot establish whether the structural changes are causes, consequences or markers of catatonia.
