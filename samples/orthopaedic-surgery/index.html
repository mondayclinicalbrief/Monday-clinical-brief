Do Personalized Recovery Trajectories Enhance the Utility of Patient-reported Outcome Measures After Orthopaedic Surgery?

Graber J, Jin X, Juarez-Colunga E et al. · 2026 Aug 01
Study Type: Retrospective cohort study (prognostic model development), Level III evidence
Key Question: Can a "people-like-me" predictive modelling approach generate personalised DASH recovery trajectories after total shoulder arthroplasty (TSA) that outperform conventional PROM interpretation methods (MDC, MCID, normative values)?
Key Findings:
  • Among 1392 patients (28 US outpatient physiotherapy clinics, 2017–2022), the model predicted 120-day postoperative DASH scores with mean error -2 points and mean absolute error 11 points (DASH range 0–100).
  • Prediction intervals were well-calibrated (e.g., 49% of observed scores fell within the 50% interval) and notably narrower (±9 to ±22 points across 50–90% intervals) than the full range of observed scores, indicating clinically useful precision.
  • Case studies demonstrated personalised trajectories add contextual value beyond static MDC (9 points), MCID (12 points), and normative benchmarks by accounting for patient-specific factors (age, sex, BMI) and allowing continuous monitoring against expected recovery.
Clinical Relevance: As NHS orthopaedic services increasingly adopt PROMs for post-arthroplasty follow-up and shared decision-making, individualised trajectory tools could improve interpretation of early recovery, support patient expectation-setting, and enhance multidisciplinary communication, though validation in a UK TSA population is needed.
Limitations: The model was derived from a single US integrated health system with 40% of eligible patients excluded due to missing data, limiting external validity and generalisability to NHS populations.

For Love, Money, or Country: What Is the Return on Investment of Orthopaedic Fellowship, Academic, and Military Pathways?

Jhand AS, Elnaggar A, Almaat A et al. · 2026 Jul 24
Study Type: Cross-sectional economic/decision analysis (Level III evidence)
Key Question: What is the lifetime financial return of orthopaedic fellowship, academic, and military-funded training pathways compared with nonacademic general orthopaedic practice?
Key Findings:
  • At a 5% discount rate, only spine surgery yielded a positive lifetime net present value (NPV), breaking even 6 years post-fellowship; joint replacement (IRR 2.6%) and trauma (IRR 1.2%) fell below the discount rate, making them financially unfavourable despite higher absolute earnings; all other subspecialties had negative NPVs.
  • After adjusting for longer subspecialist work hours (54 vs 48 hours/week), no fellowship pathway—including spine—outperformed general practice per hour worked.
  • Military-funded education reduced lifetime NPV by ~$1.7M (HPSP) and ~$3.0M (USUHS); academic practice reduced NPV across all subspecialties, most markedly in trauma (~$2.4M).
Clinical Relevance: UK trainees/advisors considering subspecialisation or academic careers (and any cross-Atlantic career comparisons) should recognise that fellowship and academic pathways may carry substantial long-term opportunity costs, reinforcing that career choice should weigh clinical interest and lifestyle alongside financial modelling.
Limitations: The model relies on national mean compensation figures rather than individual-level data, precluding confidence intervals and limiting generalisability, particularly given its US-specific compensation and military pathway assumptions.

What Is the Proportion of Late-developing DDH After a Normal Ultrasound in Newborns With Breech Presentation?

Beber SA, Scher EM, Math D et al. · 2026 Jul 28
Study Type: Retrospective cohort study (Level III diagnostic study)
Key Question: In breech infants with a normal 6-week screening hip ultrasound, what proportion subsequently develop DDH by 6 months, and are there identifiable risk factors?
Key Findings:
  • Of 139 infants with normal initial ultrasound who underwent 6-month follow-up radiographs, 9% (13/139) had late-developing DDH (11 acetabular dysplasia, 2 dislocations); most (8/13) required abduction orthosis, 2 needed closed reduction/spica casting.
  • Female sex was associated with significantly higher odds of late-developing DDH (OR 5, 95% CI 1.2–48.0; p=0.03); concomitant congenital muscular torticollis showed a non-significant trend (OR 3, 95% CI 0.9–10.0; p=0.06).
  • Only 57% of eligible infants completed recommended 6-month follow-up imaging, highlighting surveillance attrition.
Clinical Relevance: Supports considering routine 6-month follow-up imaging (ultrasound or radiograph) in breech infants even after a normal early screening ultrasound, particularly in females, which may inform UK DDH surveillance pathways given current lack of consensus on repeat imaging timing.
Limitations: Modest sample size, single-institution retrospective design, and substantial loss to follow-up limit generalisability beyond the breech population studied.

Minimum 5-Year Outcomes After Arthroscopic Subspine Decompression in Patients With Femoroacetabular Impingement: A Propensity-Matched Study

Kahana-Rojkind AH, Rana K, Barda SR et al. · 2026 Jul 23
Study Type: Retrospective cohort study, propensity-matched (Level of evidence 3)
Key Question: Does concurrent arthroscopic subspine decompression (SSD) in FAI surgery provide durable, comparable outcomes to FAI correction alone at minimum 5-year follow-up?
Key Findings:
  • SSD patients (n=94) showed significant improvement across all PROs at ≥5 years (P<.0001), with MCID/PASS achievement rates statistically similar to matched controls without SSI (n=188, P>.05).
  • Conversion to THA/resurfacing was low (5.3%) and comparable between groups (P=.61); time to arthroplasty conversion also similar (P=.71).
  • Time to revision arthroscopy was longer in the SSD group (52.0 vs 32.8 months, P=.04), suggesting delayed rather than reduced failure risk.
Clinical Relevance: Supports routine consideration of concurrent SSD during hip arthroscopy for FAI in patients with confirmed subspine impingement, reassuring UK hip preservation surgeons of durable midterm outcomes without compromising survivorship.
Limitations: Retrospective design with propensity matching cannot fully eliminate selection bias or unmeasured confounders affecting surgical decision-making for SSD.

Revision Hip Arthroscopy: Propensity Score-Matched Comparison of Labral Reconstruction Versus Repair With Minimum 5-Year Outcomes

Quesada-Jimenez R, Cohen MF, Rana K et al. · 2026 Jul 23
Study Type: Retrospective cohort study, propensity score-matched (Level of evidence, 3)
Key Question: In revision hip arthroscopy, do labral reconstruction and labral repair yield comparable midterm (≥5-year) patient-reported outcomes?
Key Findings:
  • 34 reconstruction patients matched 1:1 with 34 repair patients (matched for age, BMI, sex, acetabular Outerbridge grade, capsular treatment); reconstruction group had more severe labral tears (Seldes type 1/2: 61.8% vs 32.4%, P<.05).
  • Both groups showed significant improvement across all PRO measures from baseline (P<.01), with similar rates of achieving MCID and PASS.
  • No significant difference in revision arthroscopy or conversion to arthroplasty rates between groups.
Clinical Relevance: Supports labral reconstruction as a viable option in revision hip arthroscopy for UK surgeons managing complex, tissue-deficient labral tears where repair is not feasible, offering outcomes comparable to repair despite more severe baseline pathology.
Limitations: Small matched sample (n=68) with retrospective design limits generalisability and power to detect differences in secondary surgery rates.

Nerve Blocks for Shoulder Arthroscopy: A Systematic Review of Randomized Controlled Trials With a Network Meta-analysis

Hurley ET, Goltz DE, Frank RM et al. · 2026 Jul 24
Study Type: Systematic review and network meta-analysis of RCTs (Level 1 evidence)
Key Question: Which regional anaesthetic technique (interscalene, suprascapular, supraclavicular, combined blocks, or GA alone) provides optimal analgesia and opioid-sparing after shoulder arthroscopy?
Key Findings:
  • 63 RCTs (4235 patients) analysed; interscalene block (ISB) was most effective for reducing 24-hour opioid consumption (P-score 0.775, SUCRA 77.5%).
  • Combined techniques outperformed single blocks at specific timepoints: supraclavicular+axillary block ranked highest at 2 hours (P-score 0.735) and 24 hours (0.638); suprascapular+costoclavicular block best at 6 hours (0.728); suprascapular block alone best at 12 hours (0.851).
  • Heterogeneity was substantial for opioid outcomes (τ² = 28.16), moderate for pain scores (τ² 0.85–7.93).
Clinical Relevance: For UK units performing high-volume shoulder arthroscopy under enhanced recovery pathways, ISB remains the preferred default for opioid reduction, but combined peripheral nerve blocks may offer phrenic-sparing alternatives with comparable time-specific analgesia, relevant given increasing scrutiny of ISB-associated respiratory complications.
Limitations: High between-study heterogeneity and indirect comparisons inherent to network meta-analysis limit confidence in ranking precision.

Platelet-Derived Growth Factor Synergistic Enhancement of Bone Marrow Stimulation for Enhanced Labral Repair and Functional Recovery in a Rat Bankart Model

Mendiaz F, Vaish B, Rifat S et al. · 2026 Jul 26
Study Type: Controlled laboratory study (animal model, rat)
Key Question: Does combining bone marrow stimulation (BMS) with localised PDGF delivery improve labral healing and functional recovery over BMS or suture repair alone in a rat Bankart model?
Key Findings:
  • BMS alone did not increase progenitor cell recruitment to the tear site versus control.
  • BMS + PDGF produced a 7-fold increase in progenitor cell accumulation versus BMS alone (27.46 ± 11.51 vs 3.5 ± 3.26 cells/0.01 mm²; P < .001), with greater chondrogenic differentiation, glycosaminoglycan content, and lower Pauli scores.
  • Combined treatment achieved the best functional recovery on weekly gait analysis.
Clinical Relevance: Suggests a potential biological augmentation strategy (BMS + PDGF) that could improve outcomes of Bankart repair, relevant to future NHS practice as adjunctive biologics gain traction in shoulder instability surgery.
Limitations: Small animal model with limited group sizes (n = 3–5 per timepoint), requiring validation in larger animal or human studies before clinical translation.

Selective Epiphysiodesis of the Anterior Distal Femoral Physis Alters Trochlear Morphology in a Rabbit Model

Wills DJ, Dan MJ, Oliver R et al. · 2026 Jul 29
Study Type: Controlled laboratory (animal) study
Key Question: Does selective regional epiphysiodesis of the anterior distal femoral physis predictably alter trochlear morphology, supporting a growth-modulation approach to trochlear dysplasia?
Key Findings:
  • Lateral epiphysiodesis reduced proximal sulcus angle (P=.005) and increased lateral trochlear depth at all levels (P=.021–.036).
  • Central epiphysiodesis increased medial trochlear depth (P=.009), medial inclination (P=.007), facet symmetry (P=.016), trochlear width, and medial ridge height (P=.043).
  • Medial epiphysiodesis decreased lateral trochlear depth/inclination (P=.030–.031) and increased medial facet length (P=.003); no gross/histological cartilage changes seen.
Clinical Relevance: These preclinical findings suggest region-specific physeal manipulation could theoretically guide future growth-modulation techniques for children at risk of trochlear dysplasia and patellofemoral instability, relevant to paediatric/adolescent knee services in the NHS as such strategies are explored.
Limitations: Small animal model (rabbit, n=12) with short follow-up limits direct extrapolation to human physeal biology and long-term trochlear development.
