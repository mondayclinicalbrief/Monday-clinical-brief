Grading colorectal adenocarcinoma: interobserver agreement between American Joint Committee on Cancer and World Health Organization criteria and revisiting diagnostic challenges

Karamchandani DM, Loughrey MB, Pai RK et al. · 2026 Oct 21
Study Type: Interobserver agreement study (five gastrointestinal pathologists, 100 scanned CRC slides)
Key Question: How reproducibly do pathologists grade colorectal adenocarcinoma using AJCC8, WHO5 and WHO6 criteria, and where do the systems diverge?
Key Findings:
  • Fleiss kappa showed substantial agreement for all systems: WHO6 0.730, two-tiered AJCC8 0.731, WHO5 0.655 (all P < 0.001), so WHO6 improved on WHO5.
  • WHO grading classified far more tumours as high-grade (mean 34.4 cases with WHO6, 33.6 with WHO5) than AJCC8 (11.8 cases) (P < 0.01).
  • Reviewers agreed on some scenarios (e.g. discounting invasive front tumour buds under WHO) but diverged on others, notably stroma-rich areas.
Clinical Relevance: WHO-based grading, as used in UK reporting, may upgrade roughly three times as many tumours as AJCC8, with potential consequences for risk stratification and adjuvant treatment decisions.
Limitations: Only five reviewers and 100 cases were assessed, with no outcome data to show which system better predicts prognosis.

Identification and relevance of inter-tumour heterogeneity in hepatocellular adenoma according to subtypes and the context

Bioulac-Sage P, Quaglia A, Schirmacher P et al. · 2026 Oct 21
Study Type: Multicentre retrospective case series (26 patients; most from Bordeaux, plus international contributors).
Key Question: What is the spectrum and clinical significance of inter-tumour heterogeneity (multiple molecular subtypes within one patient) in hepatocellular adenoma?
Key Findings:
  • ITH-HCA was identified in 26 patients (17 women). All women lacked underlying liver disease, whereas 8/9 men had it.
  • Inflammatory HCA combined with another subtype was the commonest pattern (22/26), most often with b-catenin exon 3-mutated IHCA (6) or HNF1A-HCA (5). Combinations spanned the full HCA classification.
  • Two patients had HNF1A adenomatosis, one with malignant transformation; another patient with b-IHCA exon 3 progressed to HCC. The largest nodule was a 13 cm IHCA, and one sh-HCA presented with haemorrhage.
Clinical Relevance: Sampling a single nodule may miss a high-risk subtype (b-catenin exon 3, sh-HCA), so multinodular HCA warrants subtyping of several lesions and MDT or tertiary referral.
Limitations: Small, selected retrospective series without follow-up data or denominator, so prevalence and malignant transformation risk cannot be estimated.

Modern Pathology

Bone Marrow Cell Depletion in Myelofibrosis Is Associated With High-Risk Features and Shortened Survival: A Bone Marrow Pathology Group Study

Geyer JT, Abu-Zeinah G, Sy C et al. · 2026 Oct 26
Study Type: Multicentre retrospective cohort study (11 institutions)
Key Question: Does bone marrow cell depletion in advanced fibrotic myelofibrosis (MF-3 or osteosclerosis) identify patients with distinct features and worse outcomes compared with cellular MF?
Key Findings:
  • 141 cell-depleted vs 100 cellular MF cases. Depleted MF showed lower haemoglobin and platelets, higher peripheral blasts and basophils, and more osteosclerosis (p<0.001).
  • Depleted MF was enriched for ASXL1 (p=0.007), U2AF1 (p=0.01), EZH2 (p=0.026) and MPL (p=0.029) mutations.
  • Overall survival was significantly shorter in depleted MF (p=0.001), although the abstract states cellularity was not independently prognostic.
Clinical Relevance: Reporting marrow cellularity alongside fibrosis grade and osteosclerosis may refine risk stratification in advanced MF, supporting more informative trephine reports and MDT discussions.
Limitations: Retrospective design with non-independent prognostic value suggests depletion may mark advanced disease rather than drive outcome; effect sizes and hazard ratios are not reported in the abstract.

The American Journal of Surgical Pathology

Light Microscopy-Based Techniques to Identify TFEB-Altered Renal Cell Carcinoma: A Comparison Study With TFE3-Rearranged Renal Cell Carcinoma and Common Renal Cell Tumors

Caliò A, Marletta S, Giacometti C et al. · 2026 Oct 28
Study Type: Retrospective diagnostic accuracy study (single-cohort comparison against FISH/molecular reference standard)
Key Question: Which light microscopy-based ancillary tests most accurately identify TFEB-altered renal cell carcinoma and distinguish it from TFE3-rearranged RCC and common renal tumours?
Key Findings:
  • TFEB RNA-ISH was positive in all 16 TFEB-altered RCCs (12 rearranged, 4 amplified) and negative in all controls, including TFE3-rearranged tumours.
  • Cathepsin K (3F9) and GPNMB were highly sensitive but non-specific, labelling 40% and 50% of TFE3-rearranged tumours respectively, plus subsets of common renal neoplasms. TRIM63 RNA-ISH was positive in all TFEB-altered cases but also in TFE3-rearranged tumours.
  • TFEB IHC sensitivity varied by clone; the Bethyl assay was most reliable.
Clinical Relevance: TFEB RNA-ISH could offer UK laboratories without ready FISH access a practical alternative for confirming this rare entity, which often mimics other RCC subtypes.
Limitations: Small TFEB-altered cohort (n=16) and very few TFE3-rearranged (n=5) and FLCN/mTOR-mutated (n=4) controls limit confidence in the reported specificity, which has not been validated prospectively.

The American Journal of Surgical Pathology

Acquired Cystic Disease-Associated Renal Cell Carcinoma: Recurrent Copy-Number Patterns and Overlap With Papillary Renal Cell Carcinoma

Pacheco RR, Sangoi AR, Acosta AM et al. · 2026 Oct 23
Study Type: Retrospective case series with molecular profiling (targeted NGS and copy-number analysis); 14 tumours from 13 patients.
Key Question: What are the molecular features of acquired cystic disease-associated renal cell carcinoma (ACD-RCC), and how do they relate to papillary RCC (PRCC)?
Key Findings:
  • No recurrent single-gene alterations were identified; occasional variants (KMT2C, SETD2, FANCA, FAT1) have been reported in other series.
  • Three copy-number patterns emerged: gain-predominant (n=7; chromosomes 3, 7, 16, 17, 20), loss-predominant (n=4; chromosomes 6, 9, 14, 19, 21), and near-euploid (n=3).
  • Gain-predominant tumours overlapped with TCGA-KIRP PRCC (gains of 7, 16, 17, 20). One synchronous ACD-RCC/PRCC pair had near-identical arm-level profiles, differing only by 3q gain in the ACD-RCC.
Clinical Relevance: Copy-number profiling may support classification and diagnostic discrimination of ACD-RCC versus PRCC in end-stage renal disease nephrectomies, though it is not yet a validated diagnostic tool.
Limitations: Very small cohort with no outcome data, so the clinical or prognostic significance of the copy-number subgroups is unknown.

Journal of Pathology

Beyond a single ratio: an adaptable deep learning approach to analyze the prognostic value of TSP distribution in ovarian cancer

Firmbach D, Kuritcyn P, Benz M et al. · 2026 Oct 27
Study Type: Retrospective biomarker development/prognostic study (single cohort, n = 98) using a deep learning pipeline on digitised whole-slide images.
Key Question: Does automated quantification of the whole-tumour tumour-stroma percentage (TSP) distribution, rather than a single regional value, predict recurrence-free survival in ovarian cancer?
Key Findings:
  • A few-shot fine-tuned deep learning pipeline quantified TSP across the entire tumour area, capturing spatial heterogeneity with minimal new annotation.
  • Mean-median difference of the TSP distribution (an asymmetry measure) was significantly associated with recurrence-free survival (HR 0.60, 95% CI 0.46–0.79, p < 0.001).
  • Mean TSP was also associated (HR 0.65, 95% CI 0.43–0.97, p = 0.03).
Clinical Relevance: Supports digital pathology approaches that reduce inter-observer variability and region-selection bias in stromal assessment, relevant to NHS digital pathology adoption, though not yet ready for routine use.
Limitations: Small single cohort with no reported external validation, and the claim of superiority over manual assessment is not supported by comparative data in the abstract.

The American Journal of Surgical Pathology

The Largest Cohort Study of Intravenous Leiomyomatosis Reveals HMGA2 and Collagen Gene Alterations as Cooperative Drivers

Zheng W, Liu X, Sun Y et al. · 2026 Oct 22
Study Type: Retrospective molecular cohort study (single-centre series, n=89 IVL, with comparator groups of 106 conventional leiomyomas and 83 cellular leiomyomas).
Key Question: What are the molecular drivers of intravenous leiomyomatosis, and how do they differ from conventional and cellular uterine leiomyomas?
Key Findings:
  • HMGA2 overexpression by IHC in 87.5% (77/88) of IVLs (strong in 40.9%); about half harboured HMGA2 rearrangements, mainly HMGA2/RAD51B fusion.
  • WES (n=10) showed COL gene family mutations in 70% (7/10), all co-occurring with HMGA2 upregulation.
  • MED12 exon 2 mutations (2.5%, 2/79) and FH deficiency (2.4%, 2/82) were rare.
Clinical Relevance: HMGA2 IHC may support diagnosis of IVL in difficult cases, and the low FH deficiency rate suggests FH IHC is unlikely to be useful for routine IVL work-up, though this is not a validated diagnostic recommendation.
Limitations: WES was performed in only 10 cases, so the proposed HMGA2-COL cooperation is hypothesis-generating and lacks functional validation.

The American Journal of Surgical Pathology

Endometrioid Carcinoma With Malignant Squamous Differentiation: Clinicopathologic, Immunohistochemical, and Molecular Analysis

Lu L, Ma X, Bao L et al. · 2026 Oct 28
Study Type: Retrospective case series (single-centre, n=9 identified from 3,696 endometrioid carcinomas, 2020–2025)
Key Question: What are the clinicopathologic, immunophenotypic and molecular features of endometrioid carcinoma with malignant squamous differentiation (SqD), and does it carry prognostic significance?
Key Findings:
  • Malignant SqD was rare (9/3,696; approximately 0.2%) but associated with deep myometrial invasion, extensive lymphovascular space invasion and frequent nodal metastasis.
  • The malignant squamous component localised mainly to invasive fronts and metastatic deposits, with consistent hormone receptor loss. Preoperative biopsy/curettage often underestimated or misclassified it.
  • Cases spanned all four TCGA/ProMisE molecular subtypes, with recurrent PI3K/AKT pathway alterations.
Clinical Relevance: Molecular classification alone may not capture this aggressive phenotype, so careful morphological assessment of invasive fronts and nodal disease remains essential in UK endometrial cancer reporting.
Limitations: Very small sample (n=9) with no survival or recurrence outcomes reported in the abstract, limiting conclusions on prognosis and risk stratification.
