Molecular dichotomy of mucinous carcinoma defined by the presence of solid papillary carcinoma components: an integrative transcriptomic analysis

Hara Y, Yamaguchi R, Oshi M et al. · 2026 Jul 31
Study Type: Retrospective transcriptomic analysis (integrative bioinformatics study using TCGA and METABRIC cohorts).
Key Question: Does the presence of solid papillary carcinoma (SPC) components within breast mucinous carcinoma (MC) define a molecularly distinct subtype?
Key Findings:
  • MC-SPC tumours showed enrichment for neurotransmission/synaptic signalling pathways, while MC-OTHER showed upregulation of extracellular matrix organisation and cell adhesion genes, replicated across both cohorts.
  • After adjusting for immune confounders, MC-OTHER displayed increased immune signalling, whereas MC-SPC showed enhanced mitochondrial and biosynthetic activity.
  • Findings support at least two biologically distinct MC subtypes stratified by SPC-associated features.
Clinical Relevance: Refined molecular subtyping of MC could inform future histopathological classification criteria and biomarker-driven treatment stratification within UK breast cancer diagnostic pathways.
Limitations: The study is based on retrospective transcriptomic data with histological classification subjectivity and lacks prospective clinical or outcome validation.

Monoclonal immunoglobulin-associated kidney lesions with coexistent pathological patterns: clinicopathological features and outcomes

Wang Y, Lin Y, Li J et al. · 2026 Jul 31
Study Type: Retrospective case series (n=23), single-institution kidney biopsy cohort.
Key Question: What are the clinicopathological features and prognostic implications of coexistent monoclonal immunoglobulin-associated kidney lesion patterns within the same biopsy?
Key Findings:
  • Commonest combinations: light chain cast nephropathy (LCCN) + light chain deposition disease (34.8%), LCCN + AL amyloidosis (21.7%), and LCCN + light chain proximal tubulopathy (21.7%); multiple myeloma underlay 87% of cases.
  • LCCN+AL showed significantly worse acute tubular injury/chronic damage than isolated AL, and carried the poorest prognosis (median survival 32 months, higher mortality vs isolated LCCN, isolated AL, and LCCN+LCPT, all P<0.05).
  • LCCN+LCPT had the most favourable outcome among combined-pattern groups.
Clinical Relevance: Highlights the need for meticulous, comprehensive histological assessment (light microscopy, immunofluorescence, EM) in UK renal pathology practice, as overlapping lesions—particularly LCCN+AL—identify a high-risk subgroup requiring closer haematology-nephrology co-management.
Limitations: Small, single-centre retrospective sample limits generalisability and statistical power for subgroup comparisons.

From Information Extraction to Clinical Reasoning: A Systematic Scoping Review of Large Language Models in Cancer Pathology Reports

Seifaddini M, Beheshti M, Richberg S et al. · 2026 Jul 21
Study Type: Systematic scoping review (PRISMA-ScR methodology; 41 studies).
Key Question: How are large language models (LLMs) currently being used to extract information and support clinical reasoning from cancer pathology reports?
Key Findings:
  • Studies were mapped to a 4-level capability framework; most (majority of 41 studies) addressed level 2 (information extraction), with far fewer tackling level 3 (guideline-based reasoning, e.g., TNM staging) or level 4 (interpretive synthesis/decision support).
  • Encoder models (e.g., BioBERT) dominated structured extraction tasks, while generative models (GPT, LLaMA, Mistral) were increasingly applied to prompting-based extraction, staging, and summarisation, often with high reported accuracy on well-defined tasks.
  • External validation was uncommon and evaluation metrics inconsistent across studies; strong extraction performance did not reliably translate to higher-level reasoning, particularly with inconsistent reports or missing staging data.
Clinical Relevance: Highlights that LLM tools are not yet ready for autonomous cancer staging or synthesis tasks in NHS pathology workflows and underscores the need for multi-site validation before adoption in cancer registries or MDT reporting pipelines.
Limitations: Heterogeneous study designs, metrics, and datasets limit cross-study comparison and generalisability, with minimal external/multi-site validation evidence.

Recurrent TNC::PDGFD Fusions in Ossifying Plexiform Tumors of the Skin

Ronen S, Meurgey A, Michal M et al. · 2026 Jul 21
Study Type: Case series (molecular/pathological characterisation)
Key Question: Is TNC::PDGFD a recurrent, defining oncogenic fusion in ossifying plexiform tumours of the skin, and how does this entity relate to dermatofibrosarcoma protuberans (DFSP)?
Key Findings:
  • 7 cases identified, all harbouring a TNC::PDGFD fusion transcript by RNA sequencing; median tumour size 6 mm (range 4–9 mm), most on nose/hand.
  • All lesions showed lobulated, biphasic architecture (bland spindle cells with central ossification); SATB2 positive in all tested (4/4).
  • Transcriptomic profiling showed a distinct signature from DFSP and other soft tissue tumours, despite shared PDGFD involvement.
Clinical Relevance: Recognising TNC::PDGFD-driven ossifying plexiform tumour as a distinct entity from DFSP has diagnostic implications for UK histopathologists using molecular panels, avoiding misclassification and unnecessary DFSP-directed management (e.g., imatinib consideration, wide excision protocols).
Limitations: Small case series (n=7) limits generalisability and definitive assessment of biological behaviour/prognosis.

USP6 RNA Chromogenic In Situ Hybridization Is Useful in the Diagnosis of Nodular Fasciitis and Aneurysmal Bone Cyst

Dinh TA, Hrycaj S, Brown NA et al. · 2026 Jul 27
Study Type: Retrospective diagnostic validation study
Key Question: Can USP6 RNA chromogenic in situ hybridization (CISH) serve as a reliable, faster alternative to FISH for confirming USP6 rearrangement in nodular fasciitis (NF) and aneurysmal bone cyst (ABC)?
Key Findings:
  • USP6 RNA CISH was positive in 89% (16/18) of NF and 58% (7/12) of ABC cases, and negative in all histologic mimics tested.
  • Concordance with USP6 rearrangement status (FISH/NGS) was high: 92% (22/24) of rearrangement-positive cases were CISH-positive, and 100% (19/19) of rearrangement-negative cases (including mimics) were CISH-negative.
  • One FISH-inconclusive case was resolved as positive by CISH.
Clinical Relevance: USP6 RNA CISH offers a rapid, in-house alternative to FISH for confirming NF/ABC diagnosis, potentially shortening turnaround times and reducing diagnostic uncertainty in NHS histopathology laboratories lacking ready FISH access.
Limitations: Sensitivity for ABC was moderate (58%), limiting its use as a standalone rule-out test in that entity.

GJA4-Associated Cavernous Venous Malformation: A Distinct Morphologic and Molecular Subtype of Venous Malformation

Chen KH, Huang HY, Chuang HC et al. · 2026 Jul 28
Study Type: Retrospective molecular-pathological case series (n=54)
Key Question: Can molecular profiling clarify the classification of superficial cavernous-pattern venous malformations, particularly lesions historically labelled "cavernous hemangioma"?
Key Findings:
  • GJA4 p.Gly41Cys mutation identified in 94% (30/32) of classical cavernous VM (CL-CVM) cases, but absent in all classic VM and non-classical CVM cases—highly sensitive/specific marker.
  • Classic VMs frequently harboured TEK or PIK3CA mutations (71%, 5/7) instead.
  • 75% (24/32) of CL-CVM cases required diagnostic revision from original entries; all CL-CVM/nCL-CVM patients had no evidence of disease at follow-up versus 29% recurrence/residual disease in classic VM.
Clinical Relevance: Offers UK histopathologists a reproducible molecular marker (GJA4) to reclassify a subset of vascular lesions currently subject to diagnostic ambiguity, with direct implications for reporting terminology and prognostic counselling.
Limitations: Single-cohort retrospective design with modest subgroup sizes limits generalisability and external validation of the proposed reclassification.

Follicular-Patterned Lesions of the Thyroid Gland

Xu B, Ghossein RA · 2026 Jul 28
Study Type: Narrative review
Key Question: How should pathologists classify and differentiate the spectrum of follicular-patterned thyroid lesions using morphological, invasion, and molecular criteria?
Key Findings:
  • Classification hinges on three core features: capsular/vascular invasion status, presence of PTC-type nuclear atypia, and oncocytic cell content (>75% defining oncocytic tumours).
  • The review covers a full diagnostic spectrum—from non-neoplastic follicular nodular disease through follicular/oncocytic adenoma and carcinoma, NIFTP, tumours of uncertain malignant potential, to infiltrative follicular and invasive encapsulated follicular variant PTC.
  • Diagnostic criteria, differential diagnosis, and molecular correlates are outlined for each entity, reflecting evolving nomenclature aimed at reducing overtreatment of indolent lesions.
Clinical Relevance: Accurate subclassification directly affects malignancy risk stratification, extent of surgery, and follow-up decisions in UK thyroid MDTs, particularly given NIFTP's impact on avoiding overdiagnosis of "carcinoma."
Limitations: As a narrative review, it synthesises existing literature without original data or systematic methodology, so recommendations reflect expert consensus rather than new evidence.

An Explainable Cryobiopsy AI Model, CRAI, to Predict Progression in Interstitial Pneumonia

Uegami W, Okoshi EN, Lami K et al. · 2026 Jul 29
Study Type: Retrospective cohort study (AI model development and validation)
Key Question: Can an explainable AI model (CRAI) analysing transbronchial lung cryobiopsy specimens better predict disease progression in interstitial lung disease than conventional pathologist-based UIP diagnosis?
Key Findings:
  • In 135 cross-validation cases, CRAI classified 105 as non-progressive and 30 as progressive, with significantly different annual FVC decline (-1.293% vs -5.198%).
  • CRAI significantly differentiated progression; only 5 of 19 pathologists achieved significant differentiation using standard UIP diagnosis.
  • Progressive-group patients had significantly shorter survival (p = 0.038).
Clinical Relevance: For UK pathology services reporting cryobiopsies in ILD MDTs, CRAI offers a standardised, interpretable adjunct that may reduce inter-observer variability and improve prognostic stratification beyond current UIP-pattern diagnosis.
Limitations: Single/limited-centre retrospective design with modest sample size requires prospective external validation before clinical adoption.
