American journal of obstetrics and gynecology

Depressive Symptoms and Associated Factors Among Obstetrics and Gynecology First-Year Residents

Frank E, Rossi J, Zhao Z et al. · 2026 Jul 21
Study Type: Prospective longitudinal cohort study
Key Question: What is the prevalence, predictors, and treatment-seeking behaviour for depressive symptoms among first-year OBGYN residents?
Key Findings:
  • 35.6% of 1,271 OBGYN interns screened positive for depression (PHQ-9) at least once during internship, but fewer than one-third sought mental health treatment.
  • Baseline predictors: prior depression history, higher baseline PHQ-9 scores, neuroticism, difficult early family environment, and not being in a committed relationship.
  • Internship-related predictors of worsening symptoms: fewer sleep hours, longer work hours, and self-reported medical errors.
Clinical Relevance: With OBGYN facing a projected UK/US workforce shortage and being the surgical specialty with the highest proportion of women trainees, these findings highlight an urgent need for proactive, opt-out mental health support and structural changes to working hours/sleep provision within NHS training programmes to protect trainee wellbeing and retention.
Limitations: Reliance on self-reported screening (PHQ-9) rather than clinical diagnosis, plus potential residual selection/attrition bias despite weighting, limits causal inference and generalisability beyond the US training context.

American journal of obstetrics and gynecology

Liquid Biopsy Cell-free RNA-based Machine Learning Enables Preoperative Risk-Stratification of Uterine Leiomyosarcoma

Stahlschmidt SR, Lago V, Machado-López A et al. · 2026 Jul 23
Study Type: Prospective multicentre cohort study (diagnostic biomarker development)
Key Question: Can a plasma cell-free RNA (cfRNA) signature preoperatively distinguish uterine leiomyosarcoma (UMS) from benign leiomyoma (UM)?
Key Findings:
  • A 100-gene cfRNA classifier (regularized logistic regression) achieved AUROC 0.868, sensitivity 0.732, specificity 0.813 (Monte Carlo cross-validation, n=102: 78 UM, 24 UMS).
  • Performance was stable across age groups (AUROC 0.879 under 55 years; 0.882 over 55 years).
  • External validation in an independent tissue cohort showed retained discriminatory power (AUROC 0.892), supporting biological robustness.
Clinical Relevance: A liquid biopsy test could reduce inadvertent morcellation of occult sarcoma and unnecessary radical surgery for benign fibroids, addressing a persistent diagnostic gap relevant to UK gynaecological cancer pathways and MDT decision-making.
Limitations: Small sample size (24 UMS cases) and lack of prospective external clinical validation mean predictive value (especially PPV) in real-world, lower-prevalence populations remains unestablished.

Human reproduction update

Clinical outcomes of Day-7 blastocysts: a systematic review and meta-analysis

Firmin J, Ducreux B, Saidessalam H et al. · 2026 Jul 22
Study Type: Systematic review and meta-analysis (17 retrospective studies)
Key Question: Do frozen Day-7 blastocyst transfers achieve comparable clinical outcomes to Day-5/Day-6 blastocysts, including among euploid embryos?
Key Findings:
  • Day-7 blastocysts had significantly lower clinical pregnancy and live birth rates than Day-5 (LBR OR 0.23, 95% CI 0.16–0.33) and Day-6 (LBR OR 0.37, 95% CI 0.28–0.49) transfers, with higher miscarriage rates vs Day-5 (OR 1.54, 95% CI 1.04–2.30).
  • These differences persisted after restricting analysis to PGT-A euploid embryos (LBR OR 0.21 vs Day-5; OR 0.36 vs Day-6), alongside lower euploidy rates for Day-7 blastocysts overall.
  • Perinatal outcome data were limited but reassuring; substantial inter-study heterogeneity was noted.
Clinical Relevance: For UK fertility units offering extended culture/PGT-A, Day-7 blastocysts remain transferable and may add to cumulative live birth rates for poor-prognosis patients, but counselling should reflect markedly reduced per-transfer success even when euploid.
Limitations: All included studies were retrospective, with high heterogeneity and sparse perinatal/long-term outcome data limiting definitive conclusions.

Human reproduction update

Post-translational modification of proteins in the human testis development pathway

Idris FP, Ferreira LGA, Houston B et al. · 2026 Jul 23
Study Type: Narrative literature review
Key Question: What role do post-translational modifications (PTMs) of proteins play in human foetal testis development, and how might their disruption contribute to differences of sex development (DSD)?
Key Findings:
  • Proteins critical to testis development undergo extensive PTM (phosphorylation, SUMOylation, acetylation, ubiquitination), identified via PhosphoSitePlus, ClinVar, and HGMD cross-referencing.
  • PTM-dependent pathways, notably MAPK signalling, are disrupted in patients with DSD or related reproductive conditions.
  • Proteomic study of human foetal gonads remains limited by tissue access, ethical constraints, and lack of robust in vitro models, though emerging low-input proteomics and stem cell-based systems show promise.
Clinical Relevance: Understanding PTM disruption in testis development pathways could improve genetic diagnosis and mechanistic understanding of DSD, an area directly relevant to NHS paediatric endocrinology, genetics, and reproductive medicine services managing patients with atypical sex development.
Limitations: As a narrative review reliant on existing animal/in vitro data and variant databases, it cannot establish causal PTM–phenotype relationships in human foetal tissue directly.

Human reproduction update

Assessing the reproductive lifespan of female cancer survivors using AMH: a meta-synthesis of longitudinal studies

Balachandren N, Kastora SL, Anderson RA et al. · 2026 Jul 24
Study Type: Systematic review with meta-synthesis of longitudinal studies
Key Question: How does AMH change after cancer treatment across different malignancies/regimens, and how do long-term AMH trajectories in survivors compare with age-matched healthy controls?
Key Findings:
  • At 12 months post-treatment, AMH fell by 76.4% (breast cancer, 95% CI -93.5 to -55.7), 90.1% (non-ABVD lymphoma regimens, CI -96.1 to -68.3), 57.9% (thyroid/radioiodine, CI -65.2 to -36.4, high-certainty), and 63.1% (paediatric/AYA mixed cancers, very low-certainty); ABVD regimens showed a smaller, non-significant reduction (-21.1%, CI -81.6 to +14.8).
  • AMH improved consistently between 6 and 12–24 months post-treatment across all cohorts.
  • In survivors retaining ovarian function, subsequent AMH trajectories paralleled age-matched controls, suggesting no accelerated reproductive ageing once function is preserved.
Clinical Relevance: Provides UK fertility and oncology teams with regimen-specific, quantified AMH recovery data to better counsel patients on post-treatment fertility potential and timing of fertility preservation/family planning discussions.
Limitations: Certainty of evidence varied widely (from high to very low) and heterogeneity in treatment regimens, follow-up timing, and paediatric data limits precision and generalisability.

Obstetrics and gynecology

Repeat Diagnostic and Therapeutic Procedures After Excisional Procedures for High-Grade Cervical Intraepithelial Neoplasia

Onyirimba B, Chen L, Ferris J et al. · 2026 Jul 24
Study Type: Retrospective cohort study (propensity-matched, US commercial claims database)
Key Question: Do rates of repeat diagnostic/therapeutic procedures and cancer diagnosis differ between CKC and LEEP for treatment of high-grade CIN?
Key Findings:
  • Repeat diagnostic evaluation: 19.7% after CKC vs 21.2% after LEEP (P=.02); repeat excision: 2.8% vs 4.3% (P<.001), favouring CKC.
  • Hysterectomy rates were higher after CKC (4.9% vs 4.1%, P=.01), as was cervical cancer diagnosis by 36 months (1.2% vs 0.7%, P<.001).
  • Overall re-intervention burden after excisional treatment was substantial regardless of modality.
Clinical Relevance: These findings, though from a US insured population, are relevant to UK colposcopy services as they highlight trade-offs between excision method choice (LEEP predominant in NHS practice) and downstream surveillance/re-treatment burden, informing counselling and follow-up pathways.
Limitations: Retrospective claims-based design lacks histopathological margin status, lesion severity detail, and confirms association rather than causation.

Obstetrics and gynecology

Influence of Cardiometabolic Status on Cardiovascular Effects of Oral Menopausal Hormone Therapy

Rossouw JE, Aragaki AK, Manson JE et al. · 2026 Jul 24
Study Type: Secondary analysis of two double-blind placebo-controlled RCTs (WHI CEE-alone and CEE+MPA trials).
Key Question: Does baseline cardiometabolic status, particularly lipid profile, modify cardiovascular risk associated with oral menopausal hormone therapy (MHT)?
Key Findings:
  • In the CEE+MPA trial, untreated women with elevated LDL (≥190 mg/dL) had markedly increased CHD risk (HR 2.77, 95% CI 1.42–5.40) versus those with normal LDL (HR 0.59, 95% CI 0.31–1.10), with risk rising across LDL/HDL ratio categories (P-trend=.008).
  • Higher HDL (≥60 mg/dL) was modestly protective (HR 0.68 vs 1.24 for HDL <50; P-trend=.02).
  • Prior treated hyperlipidaemia, blood pressure, glucose, triglycerides, and metabolic syndrome did not modify CHD risk; patterns were consistent across age groups (50–79 years).
Clinical Relevance: For UK clinicians prescribing MHT, untreated dyslipidaemia—particularly elevated LDL or low HDL—should prompt lipid assessment and possibly lipid-lowering treatment or cautious risk-benefit discussion before initiating oral CEE-based regimens, supporting individualised NICE-aligned menopause care.
Limitations: Post hoc subgroup analysis of older trials using oral CEE/MPA formulations, limiting generalisability to transdermal or newer MHT regimens used in current UK practice.

Obstetrics and gynecology

Thyroid Function Throughout Pregnancy in Women With Subclinical Hypothyroidism or Hypothyroxinemia

Theilen LH, Mele L, Peaceman AM et al. · 2026 Jul 27
Study Type: Secondary analysis of two parallel multicentre placebo-controlled RCTs (placebo-arm participants only)
Key Question: Does untreated subclinical hypothyroidism or hypothyroxinemia diagnosed before 20 weeks' gestation persist throughout pregnancy?
Key Findings:
  • 88.7% of women with subclinical hypothyroidism and 61.4% with hypothyroxinemia had at least one subsequent normal thyroid function test during pregnancy (P<.001).
  • Progression to overt hypothyroidism was rare (2.5% and 2.0% respectively).
  • Thyroid peroxidase antibody status did not predict subsequent abnormal results in either group (Ptrend=.18 and 1.0).
Clinical Relevance: These findings challenge the rationale for universal early-pregnancy thyroid screening/treatment protocols, as many women identified with mild thyroid dysfunction would normalise spontaneously without levothyroxine—relevant to UK debates on antenatal thyroid screening policy.
Limitations: Secondary analysis limited to placebo-arm participants from trials not designed to assess natural history, restricting causal inference about screening strategy outcomes.
