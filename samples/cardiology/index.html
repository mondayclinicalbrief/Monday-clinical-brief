Circulation

ICare-FASTER (Improving Care by Faster Risk Stratification Through Use of High-Sensitivity Point-of-Care Troponin in Patients Presenting With Possible Acute Coronary Syndrome in the Emergency Department): A Stepped-Wedge Cluster-Randomized Trial

Than M, Pickering JW, Joyce LR et al. · 2026 Oct 19
Study Type: Pragmatic stepped-wedge cluster-randomised trial (six EDs, 59,980 presentations).
Key Question: Does adding point-of-care hs-cTnI testing to an accelerated diagnostic pathway reduce ED length of stay, without compromising safety, versus central laboratory hs-cTn testing?
Key Findings:
  • Adjusted ED length of stay fell by 13% (95% CI 9-16%; P<0.001), equating to 47 minutes (95% CI 33-61) against a control mean of 376 minutes.
  • 30-day MI or cardiac death was identical in both arms (0.39% vs 0.39%; P=0.54).
  • Whole-blood point-of-care testing was integrated into a single-sample rule-out pathway.
Clinical Relevance: Supports point-of-care hs-cTn as a means of improving ED flow and reducing crowding in NHS departments, though local laboratory turnaround times will influence the benefit.
Limitations: Safety was assessed through 30-day event rates with no formal non-inferiority margin stated in the abstract, and the effect size may not generalise to settings with faster central laboratory turnaround.

Circulation

Multimodal KCNQ1 Variant-Effect Maps Define Ion Channel Mechanisms and Disease Risk

Harvey ML, Osborn E, Lancaster MC et al. · 2026 Oct 20
Study Type: Functional genomics study (high-throughput deep mutational scanning) with supporting case-control and biobank analyses.
Key Question: Can large-scale functional assays classify KCNQ1 variants of uncertain significance (VUS) and define mechanisms linking variants to long QT syndrome (LQTS) and arrhythmia risk?
Key Findings:
  • 13,403 coding variants were tested across 4 assays (49,305 measurements); 1,757 showed strong pathogenic evidence and 6,660 moderate benign evidence.
  • Loss-of-function variants were significantly associated with QT prolongation and LQTS in case-control and biobank analyses (effect sizes not given in abstract).
  • Most pathogenic variants were dominant-negative, and these carried higher arrhythmia diagnosis risk than haploinsufficient variants. Deleterious variants clustered in distinct structural domains by functional class.
Clinical Relevance: This offers a functional evidence resource that could support reclassification of KCNQ1 VUS in UK inherited cardiac conditions services and help refine arrhythmia risk stratification.
Limitations: Findings derive from in vitro cellular assays, and the abstract does not report validation of reclassified variants against clinical outcomes or effect sizes for the risk associations.

Circulation

Radiation Vasculopathy of the Coronary Arteries: State-of-the Art Review

Xia J, Kim J, Sharma A et al. · 2026 Oct 20
Study Type: Narrative state-of-the-art review (no systematic methodology described in the abstract).
Key Question: What is the current understanding of the pathophysiology, presentation, management and prevention of radiation-associated coronary artery disease (RACAD), and where do evidence gaps remain?
Key Findings:
  • Risk is determined by radiotherapy factors (cardiac dose, proximity of coronaries to the field) and patient factors (younger age, higher baseline cardiovascular risk, concurrent cardiotoxic systemic therapy).
  • Presentation is early (1–2 years) after high cardiac doses (e.g. lung, oesophageal cancer) or delayed (5–20 years) after lower doses (e.g. left-sided breast cancer); symptoms mimic conventional CAD but may be under-recognised in cancer survivors.
  • Management follows standard CAD guidelines, adapted for oncological surgery, bleeding risk and post-radiotherapy complications such as atretic internal mammary arteries.
Clinical Relevance: Cardiology teams in the UK should maintain a low threshold for ischaemia assessment in prior thoracic radiotherapy patients, and contribute to cardio-oncology multidisciplinary planning given growing numbers of survivors.
Limitations: As a narrative review, it provides no quantitative effect estimates, and the authors acknowledge major gaps, including the lack of validated dosimetry-based risk models, surveillance protocols, and evidence comparing percutaneous with surgical revascularisation.

Multidisciplinary Collaboration in the Performance of Aortic Surgery: A Scientific Statement From the American Heart Association

DeAnda A, Steppan J, Boskovski MT et al. · 2026 Oct 22
Study Type: Scientific statement (expert consensus document); not original research and no systematic evidence synthesis described.
Key Question: How do the surgeon, anaesthetist and perfusionist each approach intraoperative management in aortic surgery, and where do their actions affect one another?
Key Findings:
  • No quantitative data, effect sizes or graded recommendations are reported in the abstract.
  • The statement presents three professional perspectives (surgeon, anaesthesiologist, perfusionist), emphasising that one team member's actions have immediate consequences for the others.
  • Aortic-specific complexities highlighted include single-lung ventilation, systemic cooling and rewarming, and myocardial, cerebral and visceral protection.
Clinical Relevance: Aortic centres in the UK (including aortic teams and surgical MDTs) may find it useful as a framework for intraoperative communication and team briefing, though it reflects US practice and professional structures.
Limitations: The authors state it is deliberately non-exhaustive, so it offers illustrative concerns rather than evidence-graded, actionable recommendations.

European Heart Journal

Familial hypercholesterolaemia and thymic ageing in a Chinese cohort

Li F, Zhang J, Deng J et al. · 2026 Oct 20
Study Type: Cross-sectional observational cohort study (single-country, case-control comparison).
Key Question: Does lifelong LDL-cholesterol exposure in heterozygous familial hypercholesterolaemia (FH) impair thymic output, and is this modified by lipid-lowering therapy?
Key Findings:
  • Treatment-naïve FH patients (n=236) had reduced CD4+ recent thymic emigrants versus healthy controls (n=295), most marked in those aged 18–30 or with highly pathogenic *LDLR* variants; TREC analysis showed reduced output in naïve CD4+ and CD8+ cells.
  • FH patients reached the control median CD4+ RTE percentage ~6 years earlier (approx. 19 vs 25.5 years), with TCR-β sequencing showing skewing towards high-frequency clones.
  • Statin-treated FH patients (n=326), especially females, had higher CD4+ RTE levels than statin-naïve patients.
Clinical Relevance: Suggests FH may carry immunological consequences beyond atherosclerosis, which could strengthen the case for early diagnosis and treatment in UK FH pathways, though clinical implications remain unproven.
Limitations: Cross-sectional, non-randomised design cannot establish causality or a true statin treatment effect (confounding by indication), and findings in a Chinese cohort may not generalise; no clinical immune outcomes were reported.

European Heart Journal

Acute heart failure in emergency settings

Miró Ò, Masip J, Chioncel O et al. · 2026 Oct 22
Study Type: Consensus document (expert review; no original data or systematic methodology described in the abstract)
Key Question: What is the current evidence and recommended practice for diagnosing, treating and determining disposition of acute heart failure (AHF) in prehospital and emergency department settings?
Key Findings:
  • Recommends early phenotypic classification (acute decompensated HF, acute pulmonary oedema, isolated RV failure, cardiogenic shock) to guide initial management and triage.
  • Reviews evidence on biomarkers, imaging, ventilatory support, diuretics, vasodilators, and early initiation or continuation of guideline-directed medical therapy.
  • Supports validated risk stratification tools for disposition decisions; identifies research gaps in prehospital interventions, biomarker-guided therapy, and early disease-modifying treatment.
Clinical Relevance: Offers a framework that could inform ED pathways and ambulance service protocols for AHF in the NHS, though recommendations will need mapping to NICE guidance and local services.
Limitations: The abstract does not state the evidence-grading method or strength of individual recommendations, so their evidential basis cannot be judged without the full text.

European Heart Journal

Sex differences in cardiovascular disease burden, risk factors, and disease spectrum in Europe: a global burden of disease 2023 analysis within the ESC Atlas framework

Conrad N, Torbica A, Wood AM et al. · 2026 Oct 23
Study Type: Secondary analysis of Global Burden of Disease 2023 data (population-level descriptive epidemiology, 55 ESC member countries, 1990-2023).
Key Question: How do CVD burden, risk factor exposure and disease composition differ by sex across the ESC region?
Key Findings:
  • Overall burden was similar by sex (women: 55% of cases, 6.0 million; 50% of deaths, 1.5 million), but composition differed: ischaemic heart disease accounted for 21% of CVD in women vs 38% in men; peripheral arterial disease for 23% vs 19%.
  • Tobacco and alcohol exposure was 10-20 percentage points higher in men; high BMI and low physical activity were 5-15 points higher in women. Differences in glucose, cholesterol and air pollution were modest.
  • Maternal hypertensive disorders comprised 25% of incident CVD in women, with up to 18-fold between-country variation. Cross-country disparity was greater in women (about 3-fold in incidence; up to 25-fold in premature mortality) than in men (about 2-fold in incidence).
Clinical Relevance: Supports sex-informed, life-course prevention in UK practice, including recognising pregnancy-related hypertension as a major contributor to female CVD burden.
Limitations: Modelled GBD estimates depend on variable data quality across countries, and the abstract does not report uncertainty intervals.

JAMA Cardiology

Vascular Function and Cardiorespiratory Fitness in Adults Who Use Nicotine Vapes

Boidin M, Degens H, Junejo RT et al. · 2026 Oct 21
Study Type: Cross-sectional, single-centre, laboratory-based physiological study (n=58)
Key Question: Is regular daily nicotine vape use associated with impaired vascular function and cardiorespiratory fitness in young adults without cardiovascular risk factors?
Key Findings:
  • FMD was lower in vape users (5.5%) and cigarette smokers (4.4%) than in naive controls (8.3%); CAR was similarly reduced (0.6% and -1.4% vs 4.7%). Vape and cigarette groups appeared broadly comparable.
  • Peak VO₂ was lower in vape users than in naive controls but not significantly different between smokers and controls; minute ventilation and cardiac output were lower in both nicotine groups.
  • Spirometry and physical activity did not differ across groups. Between-group P values and confidence intervals are not reported in the abstract.
Clinical Relevance: Suggests vaping may cause early endothelial dysfunction and reduced fitness in otherwise low-risk young people, which is pertinent to UK smoking-cessation advice that positions vapes as a lower-harm alternative.
Limitations: Small, cross-sectional design (17 vape users) precludes causal inference and leaves residual confounding possible.
