Circulation

Caffeine and Cardiovascular Disease: A Scientific Statement From the American Heart Association

Marcus GM, Hu FB, van Dam RM et al. · 2026 Jul 20
Study Type: Scientific statement / expert narrative review (AHA)
Key Question: What is the current evidence on caffeine's effects on cardiovascular risk factors and disease outcomes?
Key Findings:
  • Naturally occurring caffeine (mainly coffee) shows an inverse J-shaped relationship with blood pressure; habitual coffee intake is associated with lower incident type 2 diabetes risk, though data are inconsistent.
  • No consistent link between caffeine and lipids, except unfiltered coffee raising LDL cholesterol; coffee consumption generally shows neutral-to-protective associations with coronary artery disease, heart failure, and stroke.
  • RCT evidence in regular coffee drinkers shows caffeinated coffee reduces atrial fibrillation risk but increases premature ventricular contraction frequency; high-dose caffeine (e.g., energy drinks) appears cardiovascularly harmful.
Clinical Relevance: Provides UK cardiologists with reassurance that moderate coffee/caffeine intake is broadly cardio-neutral or protective, informing patient counselling on lifestyle advice and arrhythmia risk, while flagging energy drinks as a concern, particularly in younger patients.
Limitations: Most underlying evidence is observational, limiting causal inference, with RCT data confined to specific populations and endpoints.

Association of Pulmonary Vascular Remodeling With Cardiac Structure and Function, Pulmonary Pressure, and Heart Failure in Late Life: The Atherosclerosis Risk in Communities (ARIC) Study

Dewanjee A, Lamberson V, Yang Y et al. · 2026 Jul 21
Study Type: Cross-sectional cohort analysis with prospective follow-up (ARIC study, community-based)
Key Question: Does CT-quantified pulmonary vascular remodeling (distal pruning and proximal dilation) relate to cardiac structure/function, pulmonary pressure, and heart failure risk in older adults?
Key Findings:
  • Greater distal pruning (lower aBV5/aTBV) and proximal dilation (higher aBVg10/aTBV) were both associated with worse LV remodeling, diastolic/systolic dysfunction, higher NT-proBNP, and higher odds of moderate-severe dyspnea.
  • Both metrics showed nonlinear associations with higher pulmonary artery systolic pressure.
  • Proximal dilation predicted incident heart failure over 4 years (HR 1.25 per SD, 95% CI 1.03–1.51).
Clinical Relevance: These CT-derived pulmonary vascular metrics may offer an early, pre-symptomatic marker of cardiac dysfunction and heart failure risk in an ageing UK population, relevant to incidental CT findings and HFpEF risk stratification pathways.
Limitations: Observational design with 4-year follow-up limits causal inference and generalisability beyond an older, predominantly community-dwelling US cohort.

Circulation

Effects of a "Food Is Medicine" Intervention on Glucose Control Among Medicaid-Insured Patients With Type 2 Diabetes: A Randomized Controlled Trial

Nau C, Wu JHY, Han B et al. · 2026 Jul 21
Study Type: RCT (3-arm, parallel-group)
Key Question: Does providing medically tailored groceries (MTG) improve glycaemic control in Medicaid-insured adults with poorly controlled type 2 diabetes?
Key Findings:
  • MTG reduced HbA1c by 0.66 points vs 0.25 points with usual care; adjusted treatment difference −0.40 (95% CI −0.73 to −0.08; P=0.016).
  • No significant dose-response: higher- and lower-dose MTG produced similar benefit.
  • Food security and nutrition security odds increased (OR 2.12 and 3.65 respectively); no significant change in BMI or hypertension.
Clinical Relevance: Supports "food is medicine" social prescribing models as an adjunct to pharmacological diabetes management, relevant to NHS efforts addressing food insecurity and health inequalities in deprived populations.
Limitations: Modest telenutrition uptake (21.5%) and a US Medicaid population with high baseline food insecurity limit generalisability to UK healthcare and welfare contexts.

Diagnostic Approach to Cardiac Sarcoidosis

Lehtonen J, Birnie DH · 2026 Jul 21
Study Type: Commentary/clinical primer (expert narrative review, not original research).
Key Question: How should clinicians approach the diagnosis of cardiac sarcoidosis given the lack of a single accurate test and discordance among existing consensus guidelines?
Key Findings:
  • The four major consensus documents on cardiac sarcoidosis use differing diagnostic thresholds and can produce discordant diagnoses in the same patient.
  • Concordant CMR and FDG-PET abnormalities are proposed as sufficient for diagnosis in nonurgent cases; endomyocardial biopsy is reserved for inconclusive imaging or to exclude giant cell myocarditis.
  • A meaningful proportion of presumed isolated cardiac sarcoidosis cases are actually genetic cardiomyopathies, supporting a proposed 5-step pathway incorporating imaging, selective biopsy, and genetic testing.
Clinical Relevance: UK cardiologists managing unexplained heart block, ventricular arrhythmia, or heart failure—especially before device implantation—should apply a structured, imaging-led diagnostic pathway rather than relying on a single guideline, given diagnostic ambiguity and overlap with genetic cardiomyopathies.
Limitations: This is expert opinion; the proposed diagnostic pathway has not been prospectively validated.

Does Characterization of Phenotypic Heterogeneity Provide Mechanistic Insights or Influence the Response to Treatment? Experience in Positive and Neutral Trials in Patients With Heart Failure and a Preserved Ejection Fraction

Packer M, Schiattarella GG, Petrie MC et al. · 2026 Jul 21
Study Type: Commentary/perspective piece (not original research)
Key Question: Has efforts to characterise phenotypic heterogeneity in HFpEF meaningfully advanced mechanistic understanding or guided treatment selection?
Key Findings:
  • Subgroup analyses, proteomics, and unsupervised cluster analyses in HFpEF have failed to reproducibly identify treatment responders or reveal novel therapeutic pathways.
  • Large HFpEF trials using broad eligibility criteria demonstrated benefit from SGLT2 inhibitors and MRAs without significant subgroup interactions.
  • Most trial participants had central obesity, and obese patients showed particularly large treatment benefits, supporting visceral adiposity/adipokine dysregulation as a unifying pathogenic mechanism rather than distinct phenotypic clusters.
Clinical Relevance: For UK cardiologists managing HFpEF, this argues against resource-intensive phenotyping strategies and supports continued broad application of SGLT2 inhibitors and MRAs across the HFpEF population, including obese patients who may derive greatest benefit.
Limitations: As an expert opinion/commentary rather than primary data analysis, conclusions are interpretive and not derived from new systematic evidence synthesis.

Circulation

Loss of ATP-Dependent Citrate Lyase Drives Left Ventricular Dysfunction by Metabolic Remodeling

Liu S, Gammon ST, Tan L et al. · 2026 Jul 21
Study Type: Translational/basic science study (human tissue analysis combined with murine genetic knockout models and multi-omics/computational modeling).
Key Question: Does loss of ACL (ATP-citrate lyase)-mediated acetyl-CoA production contribute mechanistically to left ventricular dysfunction via metabolic remodeling in heart failure?
Key Findings:
  • ACL abundance/activity was reduced in human nonischemic cardiomyopathy hearts, correlating with depleted Krebs cycle intermediates.
  • Cardiomyocyte-specific ACL knockout in mice caused increased glucose oxidation, impaired energy flux, elevated AMP:ATP ratio, reduced histone acetylation, and LV dysfunction via AMPK/PKA-driven YAP phosphorylation.
  • AAV9-mediated IDH1 deletion (targeting a compensatory citrate pathway) improved energy provision and cardiac function by restoring YAP signaling.
Clinical Relevance: Identifies ACL/citrate–IDH1–YAP metabolic signaling as a novel, druggable pathway underlying heart failure pathophysiology, relevant to future metabolic-targeted therapeutics in UK heart failure services.
Limitations: Findings are derived primarily from murine genetic models and limited human tissue samples, requiring validation in larger clinical cohorts before therapeutic translation.

European heart journal

Standard clinical and stress electrocardiogram variables to exclude left main and left main-equivalent disease: the MASTER study

De Carlo M, Malanima MA, Baglietto L et al. · 2026 Jul 23
Study Type: Multicentre case-control study (risk model derivation with internal and external validation)
Key Question: Can clinical and exercise stress test variables reliably exclude left main/left main-equivalent coronary disease in chronic coronary syndrome patients, avoiding unnecessary invasive angiography?
Key Findings:
  • Model combining clinical and EST parameters achieved AUC 0.78 (335 cases, 797 matched controls).
  • At assumed 5% LMCAD prevalence, negative predictive value was 98.2%, allowing angiography to be safely avoided in 41% of patients.
  • Trade-off: one missed LMCAD/equivalent diagnosis per 58 angiograms avoided.
Clinical Relevance: This offers a low-cost, non-invasive triage tool for CCS patients able to exercise, potentially reducing invasive angiography workload where CT coronary angiography access is constrained, as is common in some UK services.
Limitations: Case-control design with matching and assumed prevalence/cost ratios may not generalise directly to real-world unselected CCS populations or NHS-specific risk thresholds.

JAMA cardiology

Atrial Dyssynchrony and Bachmann Bundle Pacing: A Review

Kim JU, Kaza N, Zuhair M et al. · 2026 Jul 22
Study Type: Narrative review
Key Question: Can Bachmann bundle pacing correct interatrial dyssynchrony and improve outcomes where conventional atrial pacing may worsen atrial activation?
Key Findings:
  • Interatrial dyssynchrony, often due to impaired Bachmann bundle conduction or nonphysiological pacing, is linked to reduced ventricular filling and increased atrial arrhythmia risk.
  • Conventional right atrial appendage pacing can exacerbate abnormal activation patterns rather than restore synchrony.
  • Bachmann bundle pacing, by engaging the dominant interatrial conduction pathway, shows early promise for improving atrial synchrony, optimising AV timing, and reducing arrhythmia burden.
Clinical Relevance: As physiological pacing (e.g., conduction system pacing) becomes more established in UK practice, Bachmann bundle pacing may offer a complementary strategy to address the underrecognised problem of atrial dyssynchrony and its arrhythmic consequences.
Limitations: This is a narrative review summarising emerging, largely observational data rather than randomised evidence, so implantation technique and clinical benefit remain undefined.
