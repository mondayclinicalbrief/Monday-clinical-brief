Calcium Hydroxylapatite-Carboxymethylcellulose Gel Embolization in a Simulated Microvascular Environment: Occlusion, Retrograde Flow Dynamics, and Enzymatic Recanalization

Soares DJ, Wu J, McCarthy AD · 2026 Jul 22
Study Type: In vitro simulation study (bench-top microvascular flow model)
Key Question: How does CaHA-CMC filler behave during simulated arterial embolization, and can enzymatic degradation reverse resulting vascular occlusion?
Key Findings:
  • Undiluted CaHA-CMC caused total microtubular occlusion; dilution reduced occlusive frequency, but all concentrations reached distal 200–300 µm branches.
  • Larger bolus volume (1 ml vs 0.2 ml) significantly increased occlusion (57.1% vs 16.0%, p<0.0001) and retrograde flow (100% vs 10%, p=0.005).
  • Hyaluronidase had minimal effect; systemic cellulase produced rapid, progressive recanalization with near-complete patency restored by 60 minutes.
Clinical Relevance: Supports investigation of cellulase as a targeted reversal agent for CaHA-CMC-related vascular occlusion, an increasingly relevant complication as CMC-based fillers gain use in UK aesthetic practice where hyaluronidase is currently the only licensed rescue agent.
Limitations: Findings derive from a synthetic flow model that may not fully replicate in vivo vascular biology, enzyme pharmacokinetics, or clinical embolic behaviour.

Rapid Results With TrenibotulinumtoxinE For the Treatment of Glabellar Lines in Toxin-naïve Adults: Safety, Efficacy, and Satisfaction Findings From a Pivotal Phase 3 Study

Lain E, George R, Kavali C et al. · 2026 Jul 24
Study Type: Randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled phase 3 trial
Key Question: Is trenibotulinumtoxinE (trenibotE), a first-in-class serotype E botulinum toxin, safe and effective for rapid correction of moderate-to-severe glabellar lines in toxin-naïve adults?
Key Findings:
  • 65.7% of trenibotE-treated participants achieved ≥2-grade FWS improvement to none/mild at day 7 vs 1.2% placebo (P<.0001); onset from hour 8, peak at day 7, reverting to moderate/severe by day 21.
  • Treatment satisfaction was markedly higher with trenibotE at 24 hours (69.3% vs 13.7%) and day 7 (84.9% vs 9.8%); 84.7% reported satisfaction with natural appearance.
  • Most treatment-emergent adverse events were mild and unrelated; no serious treatment-related events or signs of distant toxin spread.
Clinical Relevance: Offers UK clinicians a novel, fast-onset, short-duration BoNT/E option that could suit patients wanting rapid, temporary correction (e.g., pre-event use) distinct from existing BoNT/A products, pending MHRA approval and NICE/UK aesthetics practice guidance.
Limitations: Short 12-week follow-up and rapid loss of effect by day 21 limit assessment of longer-term efficacy and repeat-dosing safety.

Annals of plastic surgery

Breast Cancer Patient Awareness and Reasons for Foregoing Breast Reconstruction. Early Findings From the ROSE (Breast Cancer and Reconstruction Outcome Survey) Study

Yacob S, Weller M, Eden K et al. · 2026 Jul 22
Study Type: Prospective cohort study with survey and thematic analysis
Key Question: Why do breast cancer patients decline breast reconstruction (BR) after mastectomy/lumpectomy, and how informed is this decision-making process?
Key Findings:
  • 57% of 349 participants did not undergo BR; the no-BR group was older (mean 62.6 vs 49.0 years, P<0.0001) and more often received adjuvant radiation (76% vs 36%, P<0.0001).
  • Only 32% of no-BR patients recalled discussing reconstruction with their breast surgeon, 13% were offered plastic surgery referral, and just 3% attended a consultation.
  • Main reasons for declining BR: appearance acceptance (48%), belief BR was not essential (34%), and feeling sufficiently treated (16%); thematic analysis highlighted information gaps and perceived ineligibility post-lumpectomy.
Clinical Relevance: Highlights persistent gaps in equitable access to plastic surgery referral and shared decision-making within UK breast cancer pathways, reinforcing the need for standardised early discussion of reconstructive options as per NHS best-practice guidance.
Limitations: Single academic-centre design limits generalisability, and recall bias may affect reported referral and discussion rates.

Immediate Soft-Tissue Reconstruction in Pediatric Limb-Salvage Extremity Tumor Surgery: Patterns and Outcomes From NSQIP-Pediatric, 2012-2023

Amrami MB, Dagi AF, Yang LD et al. · 2026 Jul 22
Study Type: Retrospective cohort study (national database analysis)
Key Question: How often is immediate soft-tissue reconstruction used in paediatric limb-salvage extremity tumour surgery, and what are its associated short-term outcomes?
Key Findings:
  • Immediate reconstruction was performed in only 3.5% of 4500 cases (3.1% lower extremity vs 12.2% upper extremity).
  • Reconstructed cases had markedly higher complication rates than non-reconstructed cases (27.7% vs 2.4%, P<0.001).
  • In lower-extremity multivariable analysis, immediate reconstruction (OR 3.48, 95% CI 1.56–7.75) and ASA III–IV status (OR 2.17, 95% CI 1.18–3.98) were independently associated with 30-day complications.
Clinical Relevance: These benchmark data can inform UK orthoplastic MDT case selection and perioperative counselling in paediatric oncologic limb-salvage surgery, highlighting the need for closer collaboration and outcome tracking in complex reconstructive cases.
Limitations: Retrospective, registry-based design cannot adequately control for case complexity, so higher morbidity likely reflects confounding by indication rather than a causal effect of reconstruction itself.

Diversity, Equity, and Inclusion in Plastic Surgery in 2026: An Updated Review and New Recommendations

Swanson E · 2026 Jul 22
Study Type: Narrative literature review (author commentary/opinion-based analysis)
Key Question: Has the 2023 US Supreme Court ruling against affirmative action influenced DEI-related publication trends and recommendations in the plastic surgery literature?
Key Findings:
  • 60 articles across six plastic surgery journals (Jan 2024–Dec 2025) uniformly endorsed DEI initiatives, with recurring themes of mentorship, "leaky pipeline," and underrepresentation.
  • The author argues these recommendations largely persist despite conflicting with the Supreme Court ruling, and disputes the "leaky pipeline" narrative, attributing disparities to fewer Black/Hispanic applicants entering training pathways rather than systemic barriers.
  • Author highlights that White representation in US medical schools is now below general population proportions, and argues racial concordance in patient-provider matching is not justifiable.
Clinical Relevance: UK plastic surgery departments engaging with DEI policy, recruitment, or training initiatives should be aware of shifting US legal and academic debate, as this may influence international discourse and journal editorial policy, though direct NHS applicability is limited given differing legal frameworks.
Limitations: This is an opinion-driven review reflecting one author's interpretation of legal and demographic data, not a systematic or empirical analysis, and lacks NHS/UK-specific context.

Circumflex Scapular Artery-Based Flaps for Primary Donor-Site Closure of Large Latissimus Dorsi Myocutaneous Flap

Yuan Y, Wang W, Wu L et al. · 2026 Jul 22
Study Type: Retrospective case series (n=25)
Key Question: Can circumflex scapular artery (CSA)-based flaps reliably achieve primary donor-site closure after harvesting large latissimus dorsi myocutaneous flaps (LDMF)?
Key Findings:
  • All 25 LDMF donor-site defects achieved primary closure using CSA-based flaps (ascending scapular flap 68%, scapular flap 32%).
  • Complications were low (2/25, 8%): one distal partial flap necrosis, one hypertrophic scar widening.
  • Pre-expansion (of LDMF and/or CSA flap, used in >50% of cases) did not significantly increase complication rates versus non-expanded flaps (14.3% vs 5.6%; P=0.490).
Clinical Relevance: Offers UK reconstructive surgeons a reproducible, low-morbidity technique to extend LDMF harvest size while avoiding skin grafts or tension-related donor-site complications, relevant to breast and complex trunk/limb reconstruction pathways.
Limitations: Single-centre retrospective design with small sample size and no comparator group (e.g., skin grafting or other closure methods), limiting generalisability and causal inference.

Review of Little Book of Bad Wounds By Yvette Godwin

Johnson J, Gergoudis F, Giannas E et al. · 2026 Jul 23
Study Type: Editorial (book review)
Key Question: Does *The Little Book of Bad Wounds* offer a practical, evidence-consistent framework for managing complex traumatic/wartime wounds in resource-limited settings?
Key Findings:
  • The text's "delayed acute wound" concept and "blueprint recipe" structure provide accessible, rapid bedside guidance for blast/ballistic injury management, emphasising serial debridement, antimicrobial stewardship, and biofilm awareness.
  • Several recommendations diverge from guideline-supported practice, including high-concentration povidone-iodine use, rejection of pulse lavage, omission of negative pressure wound therapy, and selective antibiotic/topical antimicrobial timing.
  • Strengths include pragmatic austere-resource solutions and balanced limb salvage vs amputation discussion, though guidance reflects field experience rather than validated standards.
Clinical Relevance: As UK civilian trauma and reconstructive teams increasingly manage conflict-related and mass-casualty wounds, this book offers a useful adjunct field reference, provided recommendations are cross-checked against established NHS/BAPRAS wound care guidelines.
Limitations: As an editorial/book review rather than primary research, conclusions are subjective and based on the reviewers' clinical judgement rather than systematic evidence appraisal.

Extended Forehead Flap With Lowered Pivot Point

Lee HH, Jung AG, Abarca MC et al. · 2026 Jul 24
Study Type: Retrospective single-surgeon cohort study
Key Question: Does lowering the pivot point of a paramedian forehead flap below the medial eyebrow reduce donor-site scar length without increasing complications compared with a conventional pivot position?
Key Findings:
  • Lowered-pivot cohort (n=14) achieved mean shortening fraction of 0.156±0.025, with proportional forehead-scar reduction of 0.432±0.106, consistent with a geometric model where pivot lowering reduces cephalic incision length by roughly twice the lowered distance.
  • No significant difference in complications between lowered-pivot and conventional (n=16) groups: hair-bearing transfer (28.6% vs 43.8%, P=0.466), partial distal necrosis (7.1% vs 6.3%, P=1.000), donor-site scarring (35.7% vs 62.5%, P=0.272).
  • Within the lowered-pivot group, shortening fraction was not associated with any complication (all P>0.35).
Clinical Relevance: This technique offers UK reconstructive surgeons a simple, low-cost modification to reduce forehead donor-site scarring in paramedian forehead flap nasal reconstruction, particularly relevant where tissue expansion is not planned or feasible within NHS resource constraints.
Limitations: Small, non-randomised, single-surgeon retrospective cohort limits generalisability and power to detect complication differences.
