A Retrospective Propensity-Matched Cohort Study of Intravenous Versus Oral Iron Formulations for Management of Iron Deficiency Anemia in Pregnancy

Choi UE, Nicholson RC, Economy KE et al. · 2026 Jul 17
Study Type: Retrospective propensity-matched cohort study (large multicentre database, TriNetX)
Key Question: Does IV iron improve maternal/obstetric outcomes compared with oral iron in pregnant patients with iron deficiency anaemia?
Key Findings:
  • IV iron associated with reduced preterm labour (7.7% vs 10.0%, RR 0.77, 95% CI 0.62–0.96) and caesarean section (18.7% vs 22.2%, RR 0.84, 95% CI 0.74–0.96).
  • Significantly higher post-treatment haemoglobin (10.6 vs 9.7 g/dL) and ferritin (138 vs 39 ng/mL), p=.0001.
  • Subgroup excluding iron sucrose showed stronger preterm labour reduction (RR 0.57, 95% CI 0.40–0.80); no significant differences in transfusion, mortality, preeclampsia, or adverse events.
Clinical Relevance: Supports wider adoption of IV iron for antenatal IDA in NHS obstetric anaesthesia pathways, potentially reducing preterm delivery and caesarean-related anaesthetic workload, though transfusion/preeclampsia rates were unaffected.
Limitations: Retrospective database design with residual confounding and reliance on coded diagnoses limits causal inference despite propensity matching.

Anesthesia and analgesia

Trends and Variations in Tracheal Intubation for Acute Respiration Failure in Critically Ill Patients in the United States

Karamchandani K, Iancau A, Rosero EB · 2026 Jul 20
Study Type: Retrospective observational cohort study (National Inpatient Sample, US, 2016–2019)
Key Question: How has the use of tracheal intubation for acute respiratory failure (ARF) in critically ill patients changed over time, and what factors and outcomes are associated with this practice?
Key Findings:
  • Intubation rate among ARF admissions fell from 23.8% (2016) to 19.8% (2019) despite rising ARF case volumes (P<.0001).
  • Mortality in intubated patients remained unchanged (~30.6% to 31.0%), while mortality in nonintubated patients declined (8.1% to 6.6%).
  • Sepsis was the strongest patient-level predictor of intubation; ~10.5% of variation in intubation rates was attributable to admitting hospital, with higher use in large urban teaching hospitals.
Clinical Relevance: These US trends highlight increasing reliance on non-invasive/alternative respiratory support strategies over invasive ventilation, prompting UK intensivists and anaesthetists to reflect on local intubation thresholds, escalation pathways, and hospital-level variation in ARF management.
Limitations: Administrative coding data lack granularity on disease severity, ventilation strategy, and clinical decision-making context, limiting causal interpretation.

Anesthesia and analgesia

Making the Surgical Safety Checklist Even Safer: A Team Timeout Checklist to Improve Information Exchange and Interprofessional Communication Before Skin Incision

Weiss M, Reimer C, Budnitskiy A et al. · 2026 Jul 20
Study Type: Prospective pre-post interventional study
Key Question: Does a nurse-led 16-item Team Timeout Checklist (TTOC), used alongside the WHO surgical safety checklist, improve information exchange and interprofessional communication before skin incision?
Key Findings:
  • Median verbal information exchange rose from 62.5% (IQR 50–68.8) pre-intervention to 100% (IQR 100–100) post-intervention (P<.001); 15/16 items showed significantly higher odds of exchange (ORs 10.8–1896).
  • Closed-loop communication increased from mean 16.4 (SD 7.24) to 20.4 (SD 10.7) sequences per case (P=.004).
  • Participation increased for circulating/scrub nurses, surgical residents, and anaesthesia (all P<.05), but not for lead surgeons.
Clinical Relevance: Offers a low-cost, nurse-led adjunct to the WHO checklist that could strengthen safety-critical communication and team engagement in UK theatres, addressing known gaps in nurse involvement during pre-incision timeouts.
Limitations: Single-centre, observational pre-post design without randomisation limits causal inference and generalisability, and surgeon participation remained unchanged.

Anesthesia and analgesia

Sonorheometry Quantra QPlus Characteristics in Adult Cardiac Surgical Patients: An Observational Cohort Study

Knio ZO, Marche CA, Khan J et al. · 2026 Jul 20
Study Type: Retrospective observational cohort study
Key Question: How accurately do Quantra QPlus sonorheometry parameters (CTH, FCS, PCS) detect abnormalities in standard coagulation tests during and after cardiac surgery, and do CPB or hypofibrinogenemia alter the PCS–platelet count relationship?
Key Findings:
  • CTH ≥153 s discriminated elevated INR post-CPB (AUC 0.78–0.84); FCS 1.5–2.3 hPa detected hypofibrinogenemia (AUC 0.88–0.97).
  • PCS thresholds (6.9–12.2 hPa off-CPB; 9.1–12.1 hPa on-CPB) accurately detected thrombocytopenia (AUC 0.90–0.98).
  • For a given PCS, platelet count was higher during CPB (β 1.51) and with hypofibrinogenemia (β 3.13–4.04), indicating context-dependent interpretation is needed.
Clinical Relevance: Supports use of Quantra QPlus as a rapid point-of-care alternative to laboratory coagulation testing in UK cardiac theatres, but clinicians must adjust PCS interpretation according to CPB status and fibrinogen level to avoid misestimating platelet function.
Limitations: Single-centre retrospective design limits generalisability and may introduce confounding not addressed by regression adjustment alone.

Anesthesiology

Anesthesia and surgery induce sex-dependent Tau phosphorylation and behavior changes in aged mice

Zhang J, Yu P, Xu M et al. · 2026 Jul 16
Study Type: Preclinical laboratory study (animal model, C57BL/6J mice)
Key Question: Does anesthesia/surgery-induced Tau phosphorylation (Tau-PT217) and delirium-like behaviour in aged mice differ by sex, and is this mediated by androgen signalling?
Key Findings:
  • Anesthesia/surgery increased lung, blood, and brain Tau-PT217 and produced delirium-like behaviour (composite Z score 3.74±1.46 vs 0.60±1.17, P<0.01) in aged female but not male mice.
  • Females had lower baseline testosterone and androgen receptor expression; orchiectomy or enzalutamide in males reproduced the female phenotype (raised Tau-PT217, behavioural changes), while testosterone inhalation in females attenuated these effects.
  • Elevated inflammatory markers and GSK3β activity accompanied Tau-PT217 rises in vulnerable animals, implicating androgen-dependent modulation of this pathway.
Clinical Relevance: Supports a biological basis for sex-related differences in postoperative delirium risk, potentially informing future risk stratification and hormonal-modulation strategies in perioperative care of elderly surgical patients within the NHS.
Limitations: Findings are from an animal model and require validation in human perioperative cohorts before clinical translation.

Anesthesiology

Cerebral Hemodynamic and Metabolic Responses to Anesthesia and Vasopressors in Adult Shoulder Surgery: a 2x2 Factorial Design Randomized Controlled Trial with Light-based Neuromonitoring (CHEM-FACT study)

Samaei S, Shoemaker L, Kamar F et al. · 2026 Jul 16
Study Type: RCT (2×2 factorial design)
Key Question: Do anaesthetic maintenance agent (propofol vs sevoflurane) and vasopressor choice (phenylephrine vs ephedrine) affect cerebral oxygenation and metabolism during beach chair shoulder surgery?
Key Findings:
  • No significant main effect of anaesthetic or vasopressor on average rStO2/rCMRO2 over 30 minutes.
  • Time-dependent effects: rStO2 declined (β=-0.005/min, p=0.001) and rCMRO2 rose (β=3.2/min, p<0.001) overall.
  • Significant Time×Vasopressor interaction: phenylephrine caused 9% greater cerebral desaturation and a 70% rise in rCMRO2 over 30 minutes versus ephedrine (p=0.004 and p=0.007 respectively).
Clinical Relevance: In UK practice, where phenylephrine is commonly first-line for beach chair hypotension, these findings support cautious reconsideration of vasopressor choice given progressive cerebral metabolic-perfusion mismatch, though this requires confirmation before altering guidelines.
Limitations: Small single-centre sample (n=39) with only 30 minutes of monitoring limits generalisability and assessment of clinically relevant outcomes (e.g., neurocognitive effects).

Anesthesiology

Association of Intraoperative Narrow Pulse Pressure during Normotension with Postoperative Acute Kidney Injury: A Retrospective Cohort Analysis

Yao S, Liu Q, Zeng R et al. · 2026 Jul 17
Study Type: Retrospective cohort study
Key Question: Does intraoperative narrow pulse pressure (<40 mmHg) predict postoperative AKI independently of mean arterial pressure (MAP), even when MAP is maintained ≥65 mmHg?
Key Findings:
  • Narrow pulse pressure was independently associated with AKI (OR 1.66, 95% CI 1.42–1.94) versus reference pulse pressure (40–70 mmHg).
  • Normotension (MAP ≥65) with narrow pulse pressure still raised AKI risk (OR 1.56, 95% CI 1.27–1.89), while hypotension with normal pulse pressure did not (OR 1.09, ns); combined hypotension + narrow pulse pressure carried the highest risk (OR 1.85, 95% CI 1.21–2.81).
  • Narrow pulse pressure correlated with markedly lower stroke volume index (27 vs 47 mL/m²), suggesting a low-flow mechanism; risk emerged after ~40 minutes of exposure.
Clinical Relevance: For UK anaesthetists relying on MAP-based haemodynamic targets, this suggests pulse pressure (and by extension stroke volume/flow) should be monitored alongside MAP to identify occult low-flow states risking renal injury, particularly in longer non-cardiac surgeries.
Limitations: Single-centre retrospective design from South Korea limits generalisability and cannot establish causation despite covariate adjustment.

British journal of anaesthesia

Finger-cuff and oscillometric versus intra-arterial blood pressure measurements: a prospective method comparison study in patients having noncardiac surgery

Bergholz A, Wohlert M, Krause L et al. · 2026 Jul 20
Study Type: Prospective method comparison study
Key Question: How do finger-cuff and oscillometric (upper-arm) noninvasive blood pressure measurements agree with intra-arterial blood pressure in patients undergoing low- to moderate-risk noncardiac surgery?
Key Findings:
  • 76 patients, 2577 simultaneous measurement triplets analysed.
  • Mean blood pressure: finger-cuff vs intra-arterial bias -2.8 mmHg (SD 6.9, limits of agreement -16.3 to 10.7 mmHg).
  • Oscillometric vs intra-arterial bias 2.1 mmHg (SD 8.9, limits of agreement -15.3 to 19.5 mmHg); finger-cuff agreement was comparable to, or narrower than, oscillometric agreement.
Clinical Relevance: Continuous finger-cuff monitoring may offer noninvasive blood pressure surveillance with accuracy comparable to standard intermittent oscillometric cuffs, potentially improving detection of hypotensive episodes in lower-risk NHS surgical patients without arterial line insertion.
Limitations: Single-centre design with a relatively small sample limits generalisability across patient populations and surgical settings.
