State-of-the-Art Review: Infections in Patients With Cerebrospinal Fluid Shunts

Kunnathu Puthanveedu ND, Pouya N, Tam VH et al. · 2026 Jul 23
Study Type: Narrative/state-of-the-art review
Key Question: How should clinicians diagnose and manage cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) shunt infections, given the current evidence base and areas of uncertainty?
Key Findings:
  • Diagnosis and management require multidisciplinary, individualised decision-making due to limited high-quality evidence.
  • Both intravenous and intraventricular antimicrobial strategies are discussed, but optimal regimens/duration remain poorly defined.
  • Neurosurgical management (shunt removal, external drainage) is central to treatment, alongside emerging concerns of fungal and multidrug-resistant Gram-negative shunt infections.
Clinical Relevance: UK ID and neurosurgical teams managing shunt-related CNS infections face similar diagnostic and antimicrobial dilemmas, particularly regarding intraventricular therapy and resistant organisms, making this review directly applicable to MDT-based practice and antimicrobial stewardship.
Limitations: As a narrative review rather than systematic synthesis, recommendations rely on heterogeneous, low-quality evidence and expert opinion rather than robust comparative data.

Bucris E, Zuckerman NS, Levin T et al. · 2026 Jul 24
Study Type: Outbreak investigation with genomic epidemiology (descriptive; no formal comparative design specified).
Key Question: What are the epidemiologic and genomic characteristics driving the ongoing 2025–2026 measles outbreak in Israel?
Key Findings:
  • Outbreak comprises ~3,200 cases and 16 deaths, jeopardising Israel's measles elimination status.
  • Sequencing data support a single importation event as the outbreak's origin.

Limitations: Abstract provides limited methodological detail (e.g., sequencing approach, case ascertainment), constraining assessment of study rigour.

Southern Tick-Associated Rash Illness in Pediatric Patient, New York, USA, 2025

Handel AS, Ahmed S, Rochlin I et al. · 2026 Jul 24
Study Type: Case report
Key Question: Can Southern tick-associated rash illness (STARI) occur in a paediatric patient in New York, and what pathogen is associated with the causative tick bite?
Key Findings:
  • A child developed an erythema migrans-like rash following a bite from an *Amblyomma americanum* (lone star tick), consistent with STARI.
  • *Borrelia lonestari*, a disputed causative agent of STARI, was detected in the tick removed from the patient.
Clinical Relevance: This highlights the northward geographic expansion of lone star ticks and STARI beyond traditional southern/mid-Atlantic US ranges, relevant to UK clinicians assessing travel-associated erythema migrans-like rashes that may be mistaken for Lyme disease and do not require standard Lyme antibiotic regimens.
Limitations: Single case report; causal role of *B. lonestari* in STARI remains unconfirmed and cannot be generalised.

Nature reviews. Microbiology

Host-microbiota-pathogen interactions drive Salmonella enterica serovar Typhimurium pathogenesis

Vázquez-Torres A · 2026 Jul 23
Study Type: Narrative review (basic/molecular microbiology and immunology).
Key Question: How do interactions between host defences, gut microbiota, and *Salmonella* Typhimurium virulence/metabolic adaptation determine the spectrum of infection outcomes, from acute gastroenteritis to chronic asymptomatic carriage?
Key Findings:
  • Inflammation is context-dependent, mediated by tissue- and cell-specific effectors (inflammasome activation, hypoxia, reactive oxygen/nitrogen species), which can either restrict or promote *Salmonella* persistence.
  • *Salmonella* dynamically reprograms virulence, metabolism and redox state depending on niche—favouring rapid replication in the gut lumen/enterocyte cytosol versus energy-conserving dormancy in systemic tissues.
  • Bistable type III secretion system expression and microbiota-derived cues jointly shape the balance between acute diarrhoeal disease and quiescent systemic carriage.
Clinical Relevance: Understanding these host-microbiota-pathogen dynamics informs strategies for managing non-typhoidal *Salmonella* carriage and relapse, relevant to UK infection control, particularly in immunocompromised or chronic carrier populations.
Limitations: As a narrative review, it synthesises largely preclinical/mechanistic data without new primary clinical evidence.

Eggerthella lenta: metabolism, pathogenesis and therapeutic implications

Rock R, Zhang S, Noecker C et al. · 2026 Jul 23
Study Type: Narrative review
Key Question: What is the metabolic and pathogenic role of *Eggerthella lenta* in the human gut microbiota, and what are its implications for host health and drug metabolism?
Key Findings:
  • *E. lenta* is an asaccharolytic obligate anaerobe that uniquely metabolises amino acids and short-chain fatty acids, with versatile anaerobic respiration.
  • It extensively biotransforms endogenous compounds, dietary molecules, and pharmaceuticals, implicating it in variable drug response and metabolism.
  • Associated with multiple chronic diseases in humans and shown to exacerbate disease phenotypes in preclinical models, suggesting genuine pathogenic potential rather than mere association.
Clinical Relevance: UK ID clinicians should note *E. lenta*'s capacity to alter drug metabolism and contribute to chronic disease, with future microbiome-editing approaches potentially informing personalised therapeutics and antimicrobial stewardship.
Limitations: As a review, conclusions rely on preclinical models and associative human data, with causal mechanisms in humans not yet firmly established.

The Journal of infectious diseases

Does the Evidence Support High-Dose Liposomal Amphotericin B in the Treatment of Mucormycosis?

Lewis RE, Kontoyiannis DP · 2026 Jul 21
Study Type: Narrative/critical review (evidence appraisal of 58 publications)
Key Question: Does high-dose liposomal amphotericin B (>6 mg/kg/day) improve outcomes in invasive mucormycosis compared with standard dosing (5–6 mg/kg/day)?
Key Findings:
  • The only prospective trial of high-dose L-AMB (Ambizygo, single-arm pilot) showed response rates comparable to historical 5 mg/kg/day cohorts, with no clear efficacy signal.
  • Retrospective studies consistently show no survival benefit with higher doses, but higher rates of nephrotoxicity.
  • Saturable pharmacokinetics and altered liposome tissue distribution above 10 mg/kg/day may explain the plateau in efficacy.
Clinical Relevance: UK clinicians managing mucormycosis (often in haemato-oncology, transplant, or diabetic populations) should prioritise early diagnosis, surgical debridement, and immunosuppression reversal over routine dose escalation, reserving higher L-AMB doses for individualised, high-risk scenarios—relevant given NHS antifungal stewardship and nephrotoxicity/cost considerations.
Limitations: Evidence base is dominated by retrospective and small/uncontrolled studies, limiting causal inference about dose-response relationships.

The Journal of infectious diseases

Early transcriptional signature in chikungunya predicts chronic arthralgia and reveals deficient antiviral response

Cerqueira-Silva T, de Moraes L, Pereira BJ et al. · 2026 Jul 21
Study Type: Prospective cohort study with transcriptomic analysis (bulk RNA-Seq, qRT-PCR, flow cytometry validation)
Key Question: Can early peripheral blood transcriptional signatures identify patients at risk of progressing to chronic chikungunya arthralgia, and does biological sex modify this immune response?
Key Findings:
  • Ten genes were differentially expressed within 7 days of onset between those who progressed to chronic disease (n=24) versus recovered (n=11); IKZF2 was upregulated (validated by qRT-PCR) and ACKR3 upregulated in recovered patients (validated by flow cytometry).
  • Chronic progressors had higher acute viral loads with blunted IFN-α and IFN-γ pathway activation.
  • Sex-stratified analysis showed IFN-α/γ and TNF signalling were upregulated in women who developed chronic disease but downregulated in men with chronic disease, relative to recovered counterparts.
Clinical Relevance: With chikungunya increasingly imported into the UK amid expanding global transmission, early biomarkers (ACKR3, IKZF2) could help ID clinicians risk-stratify patients for chronic arthralgia and consider sex-specific monitoring or intervention.
Limitations: Small sample size (n=44 total) limits generalisability and statistical power, particularly for sex-stratified subgroup analyses.
