International Myeloma Working Group Recommendations for the Diagnosis and Management of Solitary Plasmacytomas

Kastritis E, Kumar SK, Rajkumar VS et al. · 2026 Jul 22
Study Type: Expert consensus/guideline (International Myeloma Working Group recommendations)
Key Question: How should solitary plasmacytomas be diagnosed, staged, and managed given advances in diagnostic and monitoring technology?
Key Findings:
  • Roughly 50% of solitary plasmacytomas progress to symptomatic myeloma within 5 years of definitive local radiotherapy.
  • Accurate diagnosis requires rigorous exclusion of systemic disease using advanced imaging, sensitive bone marrow assessment, and blood/urine studies to distinguish true SP from occult myeloma.
  • Local radiotherapy remains standard treatment; the added benefit of systemic/novel MM therapies remains poorly defined and unsupported by robust evidence.
Clinical Relevance: These updated IMWG criteria will help UK MDTs standardise diagnostic work-up (including PET-CT and sensitive marrow techniques) and follow-up, reducing misclassification and guiding radiotherapy-focused management within NHS haemato-oncology pathways.
Limitations: This is a consensus-based guideline rather than original trial data, so recommendations reflect expert opinion and available evidence quality rather than new prospective outcomes.

Hypo- Versus Standard Fractionated Locoregional Radiotherapy of Patients With High-Risk Breast Cancer in the Randomized Phase III Trial: The Danish Breast Cancer Group Skagen Trial 1

Offersen BV, Alsner J, Høgsbjerg K et al. · 2026 Jul 23
Study Type: RCT (phase III, noninferiority trial)
Key Question: Does hypofractionated locoregional radiotherapy (40Gy/15fx) cause more arm lymphedema than standard fractionation (50Gy/25fx) in high-risk breast cancer, without compromising oncological outcomes?
Key Findings:
  • 3-year lymphedema rates: 8.0% (40Gy) vs 9.4% (50Gy); OR 0.84 (95% CI, 0.62–1.14), meeting the predefined noninferiority margin (+5%).
  • At 8 years, no significant differences by fractionation: locoregional recurrence HR 0.96 (0.62–1.51), distant recurrence HR 1.10 (0.89–1.37), breast cancer mortality HR 1.25 (0.93–1.66), all-cause mortality HR 1.08 (0.85–1.36).
  • Large cohort (n=2,908) across 17 Danish centres supports robustness of findings.
Clinical Relevance: Supports wider adoption of hypofractionated locoregional radiotherapy in high-risk breast cancer within NHS practice, reducing treatment burden without increasing lymphedema risk or compromising cancer control.
Limitations: Follow-up for cancer outcomes (median 5.25 years) may be insufficient to fully exclude late differences in recurrence or mortality between fractionation schedules.

Total Neoadjuvant Therapy With Long-Course Radiotherapy Versus Chemoradiotherapy in High-Risk Locally Advanced Rectal Cancer (TNTCRT): A Multicenter, Randomized, Phase III Trial

Wang X, Tang Y, Lu J et al. · 2026 Jul 23
Study Type: Multicentre, randomised, phase III trial
Key Question: Does total neoadjuvant therapy combining long-course radiotherapy with uninterrupted doublet chemotherapy improve outcomes compared with standard neoadjuvant chemoradiotherapy in high-risk locally advanced rectal cancer?
Key Findings:
  • 3-year DFS improved with doublet-LC TNT vs nCRT (74.8% vs 66.0%; HR 0.674, 95% CI 0.489–0.929, P=.016), with parallel gains in metastasis-free survival (77.7% vs 67.6%; HR 0.655).
  • Pathologic complete response rate nearly tripled (26.4% vs 9.8%; P<.001); locoregional failure remained low and similar between arms (~6%).
  • Neoadjuvant-phase grade ≥3 toxicity was higher with TNT (27.6% vs 8.6%), but overall treatment-course toxicity and postoperative complications were comparable.
Clinical Relevance: Supports adopting doublet-based, long-course TNT as a standard option for high-risk LARC in UK MDTs, offering meaningful DFS/MFS benefit without added surgical morbidity, relevant to ongoing NHS rectal cancer pathway redesign.
Limitations: Median follow-up of 51 months is relatively short for assessing long-term survival and late toxicity outcomes.

Izalontamab Brengitecan (Iza-Bren), a First-in-Class EGFR-HER3 Bispecific Antibody-Drug Conjugate in Extensive-Stage Small Cell Lung Cancer: Results From a Phase Ib Study

Zhao Y, Zhao H, Wang Q et al. · 2026 Jul 24
Study Type: Phase Ib, open-label, multicenter dose-expansion study
Key Question: Does izalontamab brengitecan (iza-bren), an EGFR-HER3 bispecific antibody-drug conjugate, show efficacy and acceptable safety in relapsed extensive-stage SCLC?
Key Findings:
  • Overall ORR 48.1% (95% CI, 34.0–62.4); median PFS 4.1 months, median OS 12.2 months (n=52).
  • Second-line subgroup (n=22): ORR 72.7%, median PFS 6.2 months, median OS 15.0 months.
  • Predominant toxicities were haematologic (neutropenia, thrombocytopenia, anaemia, leukopenia); HER3-positive tumours showed better response signal.
Clinical Relevance: These are notable response rates in a disease with few effective salvage options post-platinum, and a phase III trial versus topotecan (NCT06500026) is already underway, which may inform future UK second-line SCLC pathways.
Limitations: Single-arm design with modest sample size limits definitive efficacy and safety conclusions pending randomised phase III data.

AAML1831: A Phase III Trial Comparing Standard Induction Therapy With CPX-351 in De Novo Pediatric AML: A Report From the Children's Oncology Group

Pollard JA, Alonzo TA, Gerbing RB et al. · 2026 Jul 24
Study Type: Phase III randomised controlled trial
Key Question: Does substituting CPX-351 for standard daunorubicin/cytarabine (DA) induction improve outcomes in children and young adults with newly diagnosed FLT3-wild-type AML?
Key Findings:
  • Trial stopped early for futility: 2-year EFS was significantly worse with CPX-351 (51.2%) versus DA (62.2%), P=.011.
  • In low-risk patients, CPX-351 was associated with lower 2-year DFS (57.5% vs 73.8%, P=.001) and higher cumulative relapse incidence (39.9% vs 23.6%, P=.001).
  • Outcomes in high-risk patients undergoing HSCT consolidation were comparable between arms.
Clinical Relevance: These findings argue against adopting CPX-351 in paediatric AML induction, reinforcing standard DA-based protocols as used in current UK paediatric oncology practice (e.g., via NHS-affiliated Children's Cancer and Leukaemia Group trials).
Limitations: Early termination after interim futility analysis limits statistical power and long-term follow-up data.

Nature reviews. Clinical oncology

Towards liquid biopsy-based analysis of antitumour immunity

Pantel K, Masmoudi D, Alix-Panabières C · 2026 Jul 24
Study Type: Narrative review
Key Question: How can liquid biopsy-based analysis of blood-borne tumour and immune biomarkers complement tissue biopsy in characterising antitumour immune responses and guiding immunotherapy decisions?
Key Findings:
  • Peripheral blood permits repeated, minimally invasive multimodal profiling of circulating tumour cells, ctDNA, and immune cell subsets, enabling longitudinal tracking of systemic immune dynamics not achievable with serial tissue biopsy.
  • Blood-based markers may help anticipate immunotherapy response and detect emerging resistance, though tumour-derived material is often highly diluted and spatial tumour microenvironment information is lost.
  • The review positions liquid biopsy as complementary to, not a replacement for, tissue-based immune profiling.
Clinical Relevance: For UK oncologists, blood-based immune monitoring could reduce reliance on repeat invasive biopsies and support more dynamic, accessible treatment-response assessment within NHS immunotherapy pathways.
Limitations: As a narrative review, it synthesises existing evidence without new primary data or formal quality appraisal.
