A phase 2 trial of rilzabrutinib in IgG4-related disease

Stone JH, Carruthers M, Baker MC et al. · 2026 Jul 21
Study Type: Phase 2a, open-label, single-arm trial
Key Question: Is rilzabrutinib (an oral BTK inhibitor) safe and effective as a glucocorticoid-sparing treatment for IgG4-related disease, including in patients who failed or were intolerant to rituximab?
Key Findings:
  • 70.4% (19/27) of participants remained flare-free and off glucocorticoids/immunosuppressants by end of treatment.
  • Mean IgG4-RD Responder Index fell by 8.3 points (SD 5.8) at week 12, and by 10.7 points (SD 6.3) in those completing 52 weeks (14/17).
  • Serious TEAEs occurred in 2 patients (1 death), both deemed unrelated to treatment; most common AEs were diarrhoea (40.7%), COVID-19 (18.5%), and dizziness (18.5%).
Clinical Relevance: Offers a potential oral, non-B-cell-depleting alternative to rituximab for IgG4-RD, relevant given UK reliance on rituximab and the unmet need for steroid-sparing options in rituximab-refractory disease.
Limitations: Small, uncontrolled, open-label design limits causal inference and generalisability; placebo-controlled trials are needed.

2025 EULAR recommendations for the management of polymyalgia rheumatica and primary large vessel vasculitis

Mukhtyar CB, Nikiphorou E, Bartoletti A et al. · 2026 Jul 21
Study Type: Guideline (EULAR recommendation update, informed by systematic literature review and expert consensus)
Key Question: How should PMR, GCA, and Takayasu arteritis be diagnosed and managed, based on updated evidence since 2015/2018?
Key Findings:
  • 4 overarching principles, 12 recommendations, and 2 quality indicators agreed; suspected GCA requires specialist review within 24 hours, with GC started immediately on strong clinical suspicion.
  • Induction GC doses: 15–25 mg/day prednisone-equivalent for PMR; 40–60 mg/day for GCA/TAK, with defined tapering guidance.
  • Adjunctive therapy recommended: IL-6 receptor inhibitors for PMR and GCA, or upadacitinib for GCA (methotrexate as alternative); GC-sparing agents mandatory for all TAK patients from outset.
Clinical Relevance: These recommendations will directly shape NHS rheumatology fast-track GCA pathways, glucocorticoid-sparing prescribing (including biologics/JAK inhibitor access), and referral triage for PMR/TAK.
Limitations: As a consensus-based guideline, recommendations reflect synthesis of evidence and expert opinion rather than new primary trial data.

Annals of the rheumatic diseases

CNS immune remodelling in SLE associated with anti-CD19 CAR-T therapy

Sun J, Hu X, Li W et al. · 2026 Jul 23
Study Type: Exploratory translational cohort study (single-cell RNA/TCR sequencing and proteomics)
Key Question: How does anti-CD19 CAR-T therapy alter CNS immune cell composition and behaviour in patients with refractory SLE?
Key Findings:
  • CSF profiling (47,222 cells) showed CAR-T cells infiltrating the CNS, shifting from a proliferative/cytotoxic phenotype at day 9 to a memory/exhausted phenotype by day 60.
  • Endogenous CD8⁺ T cells showed diverse effector states (GZMB⁺, NK-like, type I IFN-responsive, HLA⁺GZMH⁺) early post-therapy, later dominated by memory/proliferating subsets; CD4⁺ T cells showed prominent type I IFN signalling at day 9.
  • Extensive myeloid–lymphocyte crosstalk and evolving TCR clonal diversity were observed post-treatment.
Clinical Relevance: As CAR-T therapy moves into UK trials for refractory SLE, understanding CNS immune dynamics is critical for anticipating and managing neurotoxicity risk in specialist rheumatology-neurology settings.
Limitations: Very small, heterogeneous sample sizes (n=2–5 per timepoint) preclude firm conclusions about causality or generalisability to neurotoxicity outcomes.

Spatial transcriptomics reveals mechanism of autoimmunity driven by internalised autoantibodies

Pinal-Fernandez I, Pak K, Casal-Dominguez M et al. · 2026 Jul 23
Study Type: Translational mechanistic study (bulk RNA sequencing, in vitro functional assays, immunofluorescence, and spatial transcriptomics), with external validation cohort.
Key Question: Does internalisation of autoantibodies into target cells drive tissue-specific transcriptional and inflammatory programmes across autoimmune diseases?
Key Findings:
  • Autoantibody-specific transcriptomic signatures (anti-Mi2 dermatomyositis, anti-PM/Scl scleromyositis) were reproducible across 814 muscle biopsies plus a 41-sample validation cohort, and were recapitulated in healthy cells electroporated with patient IgG.
  • Spatial transcriptomics showed distinct pathway activation (type I interferon/TGFβ in anti-Mi2; type II interferon in anti-PM/Scl), occurring in muscle fibres, macrophages, endothelium, and fibroblasts/FAPs.
  • Antibody internalisation extended beyond muscle to skin and other tissues in anti-U1RNP, anti-Ku, and anti-Scl70-associated diseases.
Clinical Relevance: This provides mechanistic evidence supporting autoantibody internalisation as a direct driver of tissue injury across myositis and connective tissue diseases, informing future biomarker and targeted therapy development relevant to UK myositis/CTD services.
Limitations: Findings are largely derived from in vitro and cross-sectional tissue data, limiting causal inference in vivo.

Annals of the rheumatic diseases

High-throughput plasma proteomics reveals circulating biomarkers of interstitial lung disease and progressive pulmonary fibrosis in systemic sclerosis

Gerges E, Cauvet A, Guedot M et al. · 2026 Jul 23
Study Type: Multicentre biomarker discovery and validation study (proteomics with ELISA replication across independent cohorts).
Key Question: Can circulating plasma proteins identify SSc-ILD and predict progressive pulmonary fibrosis (SSc-PPF) in systemic sclerosis?
Key Findings:
  • Of 1507 proteins profiled, SP-D and MMP-8 were validated by ELISA; SP-D was significantly elevated in SSc-ILD vs no ILD (17.36 vs 9.49 ng/mL, P<.0001).
  • MMP-8 was higher in SSc-PPF vs non-progressive disease (17.67 vs 10.77 ng/mL, P<.001), independently predicting progression and improving models beyond clinical variables alone.
  • MMP-8's prognostic value was replicated in a separate Tokyo/Milan cohort (n=113), supporting reproducibility.
Clinical Relevance: These biomarkers could support earlier, blood-based identification of SSc-ILD and stratification of progression risk, potentially informing referral and monitoring pathways within NHS specialist ILD/rheumatology services ahead of costly serial imaging.
Limitations: Despite multicentre replication, prospective validation with standardised assays and longer follow-up is needed before clinical implementation.

Rheumatoid arthritis susceptibility genes show pathotype-specific expression in synovial tissue of patients with early treatment-naïve RA

den Hond IC, Reinders MJT, Lewis MJ et al. · 2026 Jul 24
Study Type: Cross-sectional transcriptomic analysis (synovial RNA sequencing) of two early-RA cohorts (PEAC, N=87; Flinders, N=18)
Key Question: Does expression of RA (and related rheumatic disease) GWAS susceptibility genes differ across synovial histological pathotypes in treatment-naïve early RA?
Key Findings:
  • RA susceptibility gene expression significantly separated synovial pathotypes and correlated with disease activity markers.
  • RA risk genes were enriched in lympho-myeloid synovium, linked to lymphocyte activation/differentiation pathways; OA risk genes were enriched in diffuse-myeloid and fibroid synovium.
  • These patterns were most marked in ACPA-positive patients but directionally consistent in ACPA-negative patients.
Clinical Relevance: Highlights that genetic risk signals are pathotype-specific, supporting synovial biopsy-guided stratification (as used in PEAC/R4RA-style UK trials) to better target immune- versus degeneration-driven RA subtypes for stratified treatment approaches.
Limitations: Cross-sectional design with modest sample sizes limits causal inference and generalisability across disease stages.

Clinical outcomes of upadacitinib continuation vs withdrawal in giant cell arteritis through 2 years

Schmidt WA, Setty AR, Dejaco C et al. · 2026 Jul 28
Study Type: RCT (blinded re-randomised withdrawal extension study; SELECT-GCA period 2)
Key Question: In patients with GCA who achieve sustained remission on upadacitinib 15 mg, does continuing treatment beyond 1 year maintain benefit compared with withdrawal to placebo?
Key Findings:
  • Flare rate weeks 52–104: 7.4% with continued upadacitinib vs 59.5% with placebo switch (P ≤ .0001).
  • Glucocorticoid-free remission maintained in 71.7% vs 31.4% (P ≤ .0001); median glucocorticoid exposure 0 mg vs 1048 mg.
  • No new safety signals identified with continued upadacitinib through 2 years.
Clinical Relevance: Supports continuing upadacitinib beyond induction in UK GCA patients achieving remission, offering a strategy to sustain steroid-free control and reduce cumulative glucocorticoid toxicity relevant to NHS long-term management pathways.
Limitations: Selective re-randomisation of only those achieving sustained remission limits generalisability, and small subgroup numbers (68 vs 35) reduce precision of estimates.
