The Lancet Rheumatology

Long-term evaluation of the effectiveness and safety of autologous haematopoietic stem-cell transplantation for systemic sclerosis (NISSC1-LTE): a cohort study

Henes J, Oliveira MC, Labopin M et al. · 2026 Oct 29
Study Type: Multicentre prospective cohort study (long-term extension of the EBMT NISSC1 study), n=80.
Key Question: What are the long-term (up to 10 years) effectiveness and safety outcomes of autologous HSCT in severe progressive systemic sclerosis in real-world practice?
Key Findings:
  • Progression-free survival was 81% (95% CI 71–88) at 2 years, 72% (61–81) at 5 years, and 60% (47–71) at 10 years; median follow-up was 9.2 years.
  • 18/80 patients died: HSCT-related 5 (6%), disease progression 9 (11%), post-HSCT malignancy 2 (3%), late serious infection 2 (3%).
  • 34/80 (43%) remained in remission off immunosuppression at 10 years.
Clinical Relevance: Provides the longest real-world benchmark for HSCT in early severe SSc, informing patient selection, counselling on late risks, and comparison with emerging cellular therapies.
Limitations: No control arm, with 12 patients lost to follow-up and selected-centre, real-world data that limit comparative inference.

Rheumatology

Machine learning improves 10-year prediction of atherosclerotic plaque progression and cardiovascular events in systemic lupus erythematosus

Papazoglou N, Sfikakis PP, Tektonidou MG · 2026 Oct 29
Study Type: Prospective single-cohort study (10-year follow-up) with machine learning model development and internal validation.
Key Question: How well do generic and SLE-adapted CVD risk scores predict 10-year plaque progression and CVD events in SLE, and can a machine learning model improve on them?
Key Findings:
  • In 111 patients with SLE and no prior CVD, 47.7% developed new plaques and 7.2% had incident CVD events. All 11 scores performed poorly (sensitivity 0.00–0.44; AUC 0.58–0.79).
  • QRISK3 was the only score independently associated with the combined outcome (HR 1.87, p = 0.027).
  • A gradient boosting model adding disease-related factors (duration, cumulative glucocorticoid dose, DORIS attainment, antiphospholipid antibodies, hydroxychloroquine) and traditional factors (physical activity, waist circumference) to QRISK3 outperformed all scores. Specific performance figures were not reported in the abstract.
Clinical Relevance: QRISK3 is the standard UK tool and appears to underestimate risk in SLE, supporting a lower threshold for intervention and the case for SLE-specific risk stratification.
Limitations: Small single-centre cohort with few events and internal validation only, so the model is not ready for clinical use without external validation.

Rheumatology

Predictors of b/tsDMARDs discontinuation and treatment response in psoriatic arthritis: results from the British Society for Rheumatology Psoriatic Arthritis Register (BSR-PsA)

Rotariu O, Lembke S, Helliwell PS et al. · 2026 Oct 27
Study Type: Prospective observational registry cohort study (BSR-PsA), n=815.
Key Question: What baseline factors predict b/tsDMARD discontinuation and 12-month treatment response in real-world psoriatic arthritis?
Key Findings:
  • At censoring, 65% remained on their index drug (median exposure 31 months); discontinuation was mainly for inefficacy (55%) or intolerance (26%). PDE4 inhibitors carried over twice the discontinuation risk of TNF inhibitors (HR 2.25, 95% CI 1.45-3.50); other mechanisms of action did not differ materially.
  • At 12 months, response rates were low: 24% achieved MDA, 24% ACR50 and 49% PsARC (based on imputed outcomes).
  • Male sex, younger age and employment predicted response; worse baseline clinical and patient-reported measures predicted non-response.
Clinical Relevance: UK real-world data showing poor response rates despite high baseline disease activity (median DAPSA 33.5), and shorter persistence with apremilast than with TNF inhibitors, which is relevant to NICE-pathway sequencing and counselling.
Limitations: Observational design with substantial missing data handled by imputation, and the abstract reports no adjusted effect sizes for the response predictors.

Annals of the Rheumatic Diseases

Type I interferon activation in classical monocytes and GZMK+ CD8+ T cells characterises the ultrasound-negative phase of imminent rheumatoid arthritis

Tariq F, Martin P, Abacar K et al. · 2026 Oct 22
Study Type: Prospective observational cohort with single-cell RNA sequencing and plasma proteomic profiling (small sample; exploratory/mechanistic).
Key Question: What peripheral immune signatures distinguish ACPA+ at-risk individuals who imminently progress to RA, and do these differ by presence or absence of ultrasound-detectable subclinical synovitis?
Key Findings:
  • US-negative imminent progressors (n=11) showed type I interferon upregulation in CD14+ classical monocytes and GZMK+ CD8+ T cells.
  • US-positive progressors (n=11) showed a shift to IL-1β+ classical monocytes and clonal expansion of GZMB+ CD8+ T cells; GZMB+ and GZMK+ subsets were largely clonotypically distinct.
  • Plasma proteomics showed a shift from Toll-like receptor-associated innate pathways (USneg) to effector and tissue-remodelling signatures (USpos).
Clinical Relevance: Identifies candidate biomarkers and pathway targets (e.g. IFN-I signalling) for risk stratification and prevention strategies in ACPA+ at-risk individuals, relevant to UK clinics managing arthralgia.
Limitations: Very small subgroups (n=11 each) and a single-timepoint comparison limit generalisability and causal inference; no validation cohort is reported.

Annals of the Rheumatic Diseases

Emerging drugs in phase 2/3 and selected phase 1/2 cellular-therapy trials: a glimpse into the future of systemic sclerosis treatment

Naveed E, Lescoat A, Matucci-Cerinic M et al. · 2026 Oct 23
Study Type: Narrative review of ongoing phase 2/3 drug trials and selected phase 1/2 cellular-therapy trials in systemic sclerosis (SSc).
Key Question: Which emerging therapies are in clinical development for SSc, and which disease mechanisms do they target?
Key Findings:
  • Current approved options are organ-specific (e.g., tocilizumab and nintedanib for SSc-ILD); no therapy is approved for the disease as a whole.
  • Newer agents are being developed against defined molecular and cellular pathways identified through advances in SSc pathogenesis.
  • Patient stratification and personalised treatment are increasingly emphasised in trial design; cellular therapies, including CAR T-cell therapy, are highlighted as a significant innovation. The abstract gives no efficacy data, effect sizes or trial-level details.
Clinical Relevance: Gives UK rheumatologists a horizon-scan of the SSc pipeline, which may inform future trial referral and patient counselling, given limited disease-modifying options in routine NHS practice.
Limitations: Narrative review of ongoing trials without reported outcome data, so no conclusions on efficacy or safety can be drawn; the abstract does not describe the search method.

Annals of the Rheumatic Diseases

Ten rules to reduce noise and increase precision in imaging studies in rheumatology: a methodological viewpoint informed by narrative reviews

Landewé R, Ramiro S, de Hooge M et al. · 2026 Oct 24
Study Type: Methodological viewpoint (expert opinion informed by narrative reviews); not original empirical research.
Key Question: How can acquisition, measurement and analytical "noise" be reduced to improve signal-to-noise ratio and credibility in rheumatology imaging studies?
Key Findings:
  • Imaging research has prioritised sensitivity of techniques and scoring methods over constraining noise, despite regulatory emphasis on noise reduction for drug approval.
  • The authors propose 10 expert-based rules covering image scoring, database handling and data analysis. Strategies include repeated scoring of existing images and correcting prior scoring or analytical errors.
  • Credibility should rest on optimal signal-to-noise ratio, not on statistical model complexity or P values. Reader and analyst variability undermines the perceived objectivity of imaging endpoints.
Clinical Relevance: Clinicians appraising imaging endpoints in trials (e.g. radiographic progression, MRI scoring in axial SpA/RA) or designing local audits and research may find these rules a useful critical-appraisal framework.
Limitations: The rules are explicitly unvalidated methodological hypotheses requiring empirical testing; the abstract does not list them, so specifics cannot be assessed here.

Annals of the Rheumatic Diseases

A CCL20+ macrophage subpopulation is the major producer of TNF in axial spondyloarthritis synovium and is driven by CD40L and a myeloid autocrine circuit

Zhao J, Liu F, Li J et al. · 2026 Oct 26
Study Type: Translational laboratory study (scRNA-seq of synovial tissue and fluid, immunofluorescence, flow cytometry, and in vitro T-cell/macrophage coculture experiments).
Key Question: Which cells produce TNF in axSpA synovium, and what drives their phenotype?
Key Findings:
  • CCL20+ macrophages were the predominant TNF-producing population in axSpA synovium (5 patients), with TNF protein confirmed in tissue and synovial fluid.
  • Activated CD4+ T cells induced the CCL20+ phenotype via CD40L–CD40 signalling and an IL-1β/TNF-α autocrine loop, in both healthy control and axSpA-derived monocyte-derived macrophages.
  • Circulating axSpA monocytes showed a higher baseline CCL20+ signature and greater TNF, IL-6, IL-1β and IL-23 production than healthy controls; these differences largely disappeared after T-cell induction or IL-1/TNF priming.
Clinical Relevance: This offers a mechanistic link between Th17 and TNF biology in axSpA and proposes circulating CCL20+ monocytes as a candidate biomarker of IL-1β/TNF-driven inflammation, which could eventually help stratify response to TNF or IL-17 inhibitors.
Limitations: Very small synovial sample (n=5) and in vitro mechanistic data, with no clinical outcome or treatment-response correlation, limit generalisability.

Annals of the Rheumatic Diseases

Medicalising the Human Cell Atlas for musculoskeletal disease: towards a translational roadmap

Hourston GJM, To K, Teichmann SA et al. · 2026 Oct 26
Study Type: Narrative review / perspective article (no original data).
Key Question: How can the Human Cell Atlas be adapted for musculoskeletal disease so that tissue-level cellular maps inform diagnosis, stratification and therapy?
Key Findings:
  • Single-cell, spatial and multiomic studies are mapping MSK tissues (including bone marrow, muscle, osteoarthritis, rheumatoid arthritis, tendon disease and inflammatory fibrosis), suggesting that a single diagnostic label can hide distinct cellular mechanisms, while different diseases may share convergent stromal, immune, vascular or neuronal programmes.
  • The authors propose a translational roadmap: define healthy reference tissue, identify disease-enriched cell states, nominate biomarkers and targets, then feed clinical trial outcomes back into the atlas.
  • No quantitative outcomes are reported; the abstract presents a conceptual framework.
Clinical Relevance: It signals a possible shift towards classifying disease by pathogenic cell state, which could eventually inform mechanism-led stratification and trial design in UK rheumatology practice.
Limitations: As a conceptual review, it provides no prospective validation that atlas-derived cell states improve patient outcomes or can be implemented in routine care.
