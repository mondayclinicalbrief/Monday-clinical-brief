Childhood Antimicrobial Resistance With Global Forecasts

Hu YJ, Qiu H, Harwell JI et al. · 2026 Jul 20
Study Type: Multiregional surveillance study (retrospective analysis of laboratory database, 2004–2022)
Key Question: How has antimicrobial resistance in children changed globally over nearly two decades, and what are the projected trends to 2035?
Key Findings:
  • Watch-group antibiotic resistance in PICUs more than doubled (15%→33%, P<.001), with similar rises in infants aged 0–2 years, sepsis, and respiratory infections.
  • *Acinetobacter baumannii* had the highest resistance overall (>55% across all AWaRe categories); *Klebsiella* species showed the fastest rise in cephalosporin/carbapenem resistance, notably in Southeast Asia, Eastern Europe, and the Western Pacific.
  • By 2035, carbapenem resistance is projected to reach 35% (Klebsiella) and 82% (A. baumannii), while Access-group resistance appears to be stabilising.
Clinical Relevance: Rising Gram-negative resistance to Watch/Reserve antibiotics threatens empiric sepsis and pneumonia management in PICUs, reinforcing the need for robust UK antimicrobial stewardship, surveillance, and judicious Reserve-antibiotic use aligned with NHS AWaRe-based prescribing policies.
Limitations: Reliance on a convenience-sample surveillance database (ATLAS) may introduce selection bias and uneven regional representation, limiting generalisability of absolute resistance estimates.

Clesrovimab in Infants at Increased Risk For Severe Disease During 2 RSV Seasons: A Randomized Clinical Trial

Zar HJ, Bont LJ, Manzoni P et al. · 2026 Jul 20
Study Type: Randomised, partially masked, active-controlled phase 3 clinical trial
Key Question: Is clesrovimab safe and effective for RSV prevention across two consecutive seasons in infants at increased risk of severe RSV disease (including those with prematurity, chronic lung disease, or significant congenital heart disease)?
Key Findings:
  • Season 1: adverse event rates comparable between clesrovimab 105 mg (n=498) and palivizumab (n=499); RSV-associated MALRI incidence similar (3.2%, 95% CI 1.8–5.2% vs 3.4%, 95% CI 2.0–5.6%) through day 150.
  • Season 2: open-label clesrovimab 210 mg (n=276) was well tolerated, with RSV-associated MALRI incidence of 7.3% (95% CI 4.4–11.4%) through day 180.
Clinical Relevance: Supports extending single-dose clesrovimab use into a second RSV season for high-risk infants, potentially simplifying NHS RSV prophylaxis pathways currently reliant on monthly palivizumab dosing.
Limitations: Season 2 data were open-label without a concurrent control arm, limiting direct efficacy comparison.

Childhood Factors and Positive Functioning Among Adults Born Very Preterm or With Very Low Birth Weight

Gönen E, Twilhaar ES, Tsalacopoulos N et al. · 2026 Jul 20
Study Type: Longitudinal cohort study
Key Question: What childhood factors predict distinct profiles of positive adult functioning (risk avoidance, wealth, relationships) among adults born very preterm/very low birth weight (VP/VLBW) versus term-born controls?
Key Findings:
  • Three functioning profiles identified at age 34: optimal (63%), moderate with poor partner relationships (26%), and low economic/social functioning (12%); VP/VLBW individuals were overrepresented in the low-functioning group (18% vs 12% overall).
  • Optimal-functioning profile had substantially higher life satisfaction than the low-functioning profile (mean difference 7.71, 95% CI 5.17–10.24, d=1.46).
  • Higher childhood IQ (per 1-SD increase) was strongly associated with reduced odds of low functioning vs optimal functioning (OR 0.21, 95% CI 0.13–0.34), after adjustment.
Clinical Relevance: Identifies childhood cognitive functioning as a modifiable target for early intervention services (e.g., neonatal follow-up, educational support) to improve long-term psychosocial outcomes in VP/VLBW children within UK developmental follow-up pathways.
Limitations: Single geographic cohort (Bavaria, Germany) born in 1985–86, limiting generalisability to contemporary UK neonatal populations and current NICU practices.

A Brief Tool to Predict Agitation in Pediatric Emergency Psychiatric Patients

Babcock L, Klein L, Zhang Y et al. · 2026 Jul 21
Study Type: Prospective observational prognostic validation study
Key Question: Can a 5-item nurse-completed triage tool (BRACHA-S) predict which paediatric ED mental/behavioural health patients will require pharmacologic or physical intervention for agitation (ARI)?
Key Findings:
  • Among 472 encounters, 11.7% had ARI; BRACHA-S showed moderate discrimination (AUROC 0.72, 95% CI 0.64–0.79).
  • ARI risk rose with score: 3.3% at score 0 vs 36.0% at score 5; scores 4–5 carried a relative risk of 5.9 (95% CI 3.3–10.7) vs scores 0–2.
  • Low scores (0–1) had high negative predictive value (96.7%) but poor specificity (13.9%); high scores (4–5) had specificity ≥89% and PPV >33%.
Clinical Relevance: A quick triage-based score could help UK paediatric EDs stratify agitation risk early, supporting proactive safety planning and staffing/resource allocation for MBH presentations.
Limitations: Two-centre design and modest discrimination limit generalisability and standalone clinical utility, particularly given low specificity in the low-risk group.
