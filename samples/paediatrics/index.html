JAMA Pediatrics

Mobile Subthreshold Exercise Program (MSTEP) for Youth With Prolonged Concussion Recovery: A Randomized Clinical Trial

Chrisman SPD, Bollinger BJ, Flaat A et al. · 2026 Oct 19
Study Type: Multicentre randomised controlled trial (n=210; four US states; 6-month follow-up)
Key Question: Does a remotely delivered, 6-week subthreshold aerobic exercise programme improve recovery in 11–18-year-olds with concussion up to 1 year post-injury, compared with stretching?
Key Findings:
  • At 3 months, MSTEP gave greater improvement than stretching in concussive symptoms (β −3.15; 95% CI −6.05 to −0.25) and health-related quality of life (β 4.15; 95% CI 0.64 to 7.66); no between-group difference at 6 months.
  • Modelling showed faster symptom recovery with MSTEP; sleep, depression and anxiety improved in both groups without group differences.
  • Benefit was confined to those <3 months from injury (symptoms β −4.44; 95% CI −7.99 to −0.90); no moderation by age or sex.
Clinical Relevance: Supports remote, graded aerobic exercise for subacute concussion, which could be feasible within UK services given limited specialist rehabilitation capacity.
Limitations: Benefit was modest, transient, and absent at 6 months, and the subgroup finding is exploratory; US setting limits generalisability to NHS pathways.

JAMA Pediatrics

Ambulatory Electrocardiography Modalities in Pediatric Arrhythmia Detection and Management

Hsu YT, Tseng WC, Hsu YL et al. · 2026 Oct 19
Study Type: Single-centre prospective/retrospective cohort study (design timing not specified), n=435 children, with 1-year follow-up.
Key Question: Does 7-day continuous ambulatory ECG monitoring have a higher diagnostic yield than 24-hour Holter or 7-day event recording for arrhythmia detection in children?
Key Findings:
  • Major arrhythmia detection was 6.2% with 7-day Holter versus 2.3% with 24-hour Holter or 7-day event recording (P<.001).
  • Sensitivity was 73% for 7-day Holter versus 27% for each comparator; negative predictive value was 97.5% (95% CI 95.2–98.6%).
  • Management changed in 7.1% (7-day Holter) versus 1.6% (24-hour Holter) and 2.8% (event recorder); changes were driven mainly by tachyarrhythmias.
Clinical Relevance: Supports extended continuous monitoring over 24-hour Holter or event recorders when symptoms are intermittent, though NHS availability and cost of 7-day devices will influence uptake.
Limitations: Single-centre Taiwanese cohort excluding structural heart disease, with all modalities derived from one device, limiting generalisability to UK populations and independent device comparison.

JAMA Pediatrics

Evening Phone Use and Sleep Outcomes Among Youth

Zhao Y, Hale L, Christakis DA · 2026 Oct 19
Study Type: Intensive longitudinal cohort study (observational), using passive smartphone sensing and Fitbit-measured sleep.
Key Question: Is evening smartphone use associated with objectively measured sleep timing, duration and continuity in adolescents, both within and between individuals?
Key Findings:
  • Within-person: each additional minute of phone use after 10 pm above a participant's own average was associated with 0.87 min later sleep onset (95% CI 0.84–0.90) and 0.43 min shorter total sleep time (95% CI 0.39–0.47); on higher-use nights, adolescents slept about 20 min less.
  • Associations strengthened progressively for cutoffs from 8 pm to midnight.
  • Use in the final 5 minutes before sleep onset was associated with 0.74 min more sustained wake after sleep onset (95% CI 0.50–0.98), mainly in the first 30 minutes; negligible for the final 20 minutes.
Clinical Relevance: Supports specific, objectively informed advice on late-evening phone use in adolescent sleep and neurodevelopmental consultations, though the data are US-based and not UK-specific.
Limitations: Observational design with a small sample (n=425, Android users only) precludes causal inference; reverse causation (poor sleep prompting phone use) cannot be excluded.

The Journal of Pediatrics

Exercise Capacity, Muscle Mass, and Quality of Life in Pediatric Patients with Fontan Circulation

Jose JT, Bernstein D, Palaniappan L et al. · 2026 Oct 23
Study Type: Cross-sectional analysis of baseline data from a prospective cohort study (n=135, aged 8–19 years, Fontan circulation).
Key Question: How do cardiac, vascular and skeletal muscle measures relate to parent/self-reported quality of life (PedsQL) in children with Fontan circulation?
Key Findings:
  • Appendicular lean mass index z-score was low (median −0.84) and correlated positively with total PedsQL (ρ=0.28, P=0.001) and Physical Functioning domain (ρ=0.30, P<0.001).
  • Absolute peak VO2 showed only a weak correlation with PedsQL Physical Summary score (ρ=0.21, P=0.015); indexed, % predicted and Fontan-specific peak VO2 z-score showed no significant association.
  • Median total PedsQL was 76.1 (IQR 65.2–88.0).
Clinical Relevance: Muscle mass may be a more relevant, potentially modifiable target than VO2 for improving quality of life in Fontan patients, supporting consideration of strength and body-composition assessment in follow-up and rehabilitation.
Limitations: Cross-sectional design with modest correlations precludes causal inference, and the effects of confounders such as age, pubertal status and ventricular morphology are not reported in the abstract.

The Journal of Pediatrics

Somatic Growth Faltering Precedes Brain Growth Deceleration in Preterm Infants: A Window for Intervention

Ottolini KM, Watson TT, Basu SK et al. · 2026 Oct 23
Study Type: Prospective observational cohort study with serial anthropometry and brain MRI.
Key Question: When does somatic growth faltering occur in very preterm infants, what are its risk factors, and how does it relate to subsequent brain growth?
Key Findings:
  • Of 118 infants born <32 weeks, 68 had growth faltering (GF), with weight gain falling off after 33 weeks corrected age. Early systemic steroids (OR 3.8, 95% CI 1.2–14.8) and GI surgery (OR 7.8, 1.8–53.9) were associated with GF. GF infants had lower lipid, caloric and maternal milk intake trajectories.
  • Brain growth slowed after 37 weeks corrected age in GF infants across nearly all regions, about 4 weeks after weight faltering began.
  • At term-corrected age, cerebellar (β −1.74, p=0.01) and brainstem (β −0.29, p=0.02) volumes were lower in GF infants after adjustment.
Clinical Relevance: Supports early, proactive nutritional optimisation in NHS neonatal units, particularly for infants with steroid exposure or GI surgery, before brain growth is affected.
Limitations: Observational design with a small sample and wide confidence intervals precludes causal inference; neurodevelopmental outcomes were not reported.
