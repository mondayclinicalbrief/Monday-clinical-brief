Pediatrics

Complications in Pediatric Cancer Patients With Staphylococcus aureus Bloodstream Infection

Slatnick LR, Giles J, Miller TP et al. · 2026 Nov 04
Study Type: Multicentre retrospective cohort study (2005–2022), with propensity score-weighted multivariable regression.
Key Question: In children with cancer and *S. aureus* bloodstream infection, does central venous catheter (CVC) removal versus salvage affect the risk of subsequent complications?
Key Findings:
  • Of 292 episodes with a CVC, 21.2% had a major complication (recurrent BSI, deep abscess, endocarditis, death etc.); salvage 25.3% vs removal 16.2%.
  • Salvage was independently associated with complications (OR 3.37, 95% CI 2.08–5.61), strongest early (OR 21.8, 95% CI 7.09–95.4) and still significant later (OR 1.83, 95% CI 1.06–3.18).
  • Other independent risk factors: MRSA, male sex, invasive infection at presentation, implanted ports, sepsis, persistent positive cultures.
Clinical Relevance: Supports early CVC removal in paediatric oncology *S. aureus* bacteraemia, relevant to UK shared-care and Principal Treatment Centre decisions, though replacement access needs balancing.
Limitations: Retrospective design with likely confounding by indication, despite propensity weighting; the very wide early-period CI suggests imprecise estimates.

JAMA Pediatrics

SARS-CoV-2 mRNA Vaccination in Pregnancy and Child Neurodevelopment at 18 to 30 Months

Hughes BL, Clifton RG, Metz TD et al. · 2026 Nov 02
Study Type: Multicentre prospective matched cohort study (14 US centres, MFMU Network).
Key Question: Is maternal mRNA SARS-CoV-2 vaccination during, or within 30 days before, pregnancy associated with differences in offspring neurodevelopment at 18–30 months?
Key Findings:
  • Primary outcome (ASQ-3 total score) met the prespecified equivalence margin (±10): 255 (exposed) vs 260 (unexposed); adjusted median difference −3.4 (90% CI −9.8 to 3.0).
  • No significant differences in domain-specific ASQ-3 scores, Child Behavior Checklist, M-CHAT, or Early Childhood Behavior Questionnaire.
  • 434 children analysed (217 matched pairs); median age at assessment 25.8 months.
Clinical Relevance: Provides reassuring early-childhood neurodevelopmental data supporting continued maternal SARS-CoV-2 vaccination advice in pregnancy, relevant to UK counselling.
Limitations: Small sample (enrolled 511 of 3956 screened, so selection bias is possible), with a parent-reported screening tool, short follow-up, and a US-only population limiting generalisability and the ability to detect later-emerging or rare outcomes.

The Journal of Pediatrics

Empiric Diagnosis and Treatment of Gastroesophageal Reflux in Infants Born Extremely Preterm and Associations with Growth and Development

Lockyear C, Chahine RA, Malcolm W et al. · 2026 Nov 05
Study Type: Retrospective cohort study (multicentre, likely NICHD Neonatal Research Network data), n=3,483 infants born <27 weeks' gestation.
Key Question: Is anti-reflux medication (ARM) use at discharge in extremely preterm infants associated with growth and neurodevelopmental outcomes at 22–26 months corrected age?
Key Findings:
  • 15% (508/3,483) were discharged on ARM; this fell from 18% to 11% over 2012–2019 (P=0.01).
  • After adjustment, ARM at discharge was associated with a lower weight z-score at follow-up (mean difference −0.18, 95% CI −0.35 to −0.01) and higher risk of moderate/severe NDI (RR 1.16, 95% CI 1.01–1.35).
  • NDI risk was highest in infants still on ARM at follow-up (P=0.004).
Clinical Relevance: Supports cautious, de-prescribing-oriented practice for presumed GER in extremely preterm infants, relevant to UK neonatal follow-up and discharge planning.
Limitations: Confounding by indication cannot be excluded, so the associations do not demonstrate that ARM causes poorer outcomes; the effect estimates are also modest, with confidence intervals close to the null.

Pediatrics

Low-Level Pyuria and Antibiotic Prescribing for Suspected Urinary Tract Infection in Children

Wattier RL, Usac FR, Mama-Farah A et al. · 2026 Nov 05
Study Type: Cross-sectional study using electronic health record data and abstracted symptom data (4 Northern California emergency/urgent care settings, 2022–2023).
Key Question: Does low-level pyuria disproportionately drive antibiotic prescribing for suspected UTI in children, contributing to overtreatment?
Key Findings:
  • Of 1150 children (median age 6.3 years) undergoing urinalysis, 191 (16.6%) received antibiotics.
  • Low pyuria (trace leukocyte esterase, 10–69 WBC/mm³ or 5–10 WBC/hpf) was associated with antibiotic initiation compared with no pyuria (adjusted OR 6.62, 95% CI 3.72–11.79).
  • Low pyuria accounted for 17.0% (95% CI 10.3–23.2%) of overtreatment in culture-negative cases, but only 1.8% (95% CI 1.1–2.4%) of treatment in culture-confirmed UTI. Findings were consistent across age strata.
Clinical Relevance: Supports antimicrobial stewardship in UK urgent care and ED settings by cautioning against treating low-level pyuria in isolation, in line with the principle of integrating symptoms, age and urinalysis results.
Limitations: US-based, observational design from a single region, so prescribing patterns and urinalysis practice may not generalise to UK settings (e.g. NICE-guided dipstick use).

Penicillin Allergy Delabeling in Children

Larson AS, Lim PP, Angal J et al. · 2026 Nov 04
Study Type: Narrative review (clinical practice update). The abstract does not describe a systematic search or formal synthesis methodology.
Key Question: How should clinicians identify and remove inaccurate penicillin allergy labels in children?
Key Findings:
  • Around 10% of people carry a penicillin allergy label, but roughly 95% are not truly allergic; most labels are assigned in childhood and rarely evaluated.
  • Inaccurate labels are linked to worse clinical outcomes and higher costs through avoidance of first-line β-lactams (abstract gives no paediatric-specific effect sizes).
  • Recent guidelines recommend proactive delabelling, including direct oral amoxicillin challenge for children assessed as low-risk on history.
Clinical Relevance: Supports delabelling of low-risk children in routine practice, including outside specialist allergy services, with relevance to UK antimicrobial stewardship; note the policy context cited is US-specific.
Limitations: The abstract reports no primary data, methods, or quantified outcomes, so the strength of the underlying evidence cannot be judged from it.
