Overall Outcomes of Liver Transplantation Using Upfront Normothermic Machine Perfusion: A Real-World Single-Center Experience

Nguyen MC, Ohara SY, Barnhill M et al. · 2026 Jul 22
Study Type: Retrospective single-centre cohort study
Key Question: Does normothermic machine perfusion (NMP) close the survival gap between DCD and DBD liver transplantation compared with static cold storage (SCS)?
Key Findings:
  • 1-year graft survival: DCD-NMP 94% vs DCD-SCS 86% vs DBD 93%; patient survival 98% vs 93% vs 94–95%, respectively.
  • DCD-SCS carried significantly higher adjusted risk of graft loss (aHR 2.48, p<0.001) and death (aHR 2.39, p=0.004) versus DCD-NMP; DBD grafts showed no significant difference from DCD-NMP.
  • DCD-NMP reduced early allograft dysfunction (21.7% vs 50.2%) and ischemic cholangiopathy (7.7% vs 22%) compared with DCD-SCS, approaching DBD-level outcomes, despite higher-risk donor grafts.
Clinical Relevance: Supports wider adoption of NMP for DCD liver allografts in UK transplant centres to safely expand the donor pool without compromising outcomes, relevant amid ongoing organ shortage and NHSBT efforts to increase DCD utilisation.
Limitations: Single-centre, non-randomised design with potential selection bias in graft allocation to NMP versus SCS.

Burn Injury Rewires Immune and Neuronal Extracellular Vesicle Communication

Grimsrud KN, Green T, Tang Y et al. · 2026 Jul 29
Study Type: Cohort study (case-control, proteomic analysis)
Key Question: Does severe burn injury alter plasma extracellular vesicle (EV) protein cargo in ways that reveal immune-neural signalling pathways relevant to burn pathophysiology?
Key Findings:
  • Among 919 proteins detected in plasma EVs from 37 burn patients (≥15% TBSA) vs 21 controls, 326 were decreased and 593 increased; top discriminating proteins were CRP, ITGA11, MYH9, CALR, and ANTXR2 (adjusted P<0.05).
  • Pathway analyses (GO, Reactome GSEA, IPA) consistently showed enrichment for immune chemotaxis, acute-phase response, complement/coagulation, and cellular stress pathways, with similar findings in an age-restricted (<40 years) subcohort.
  • Exploratory cell-type-specific EV enrichment suggested distinct immune vs neuronal EV proteomic trends, though no subtype-specific proteins reached statistical significance.
Clinical Relevance: These findings support EVs as potential biomarkers for monitoring hyperinflammatory and neuroimmune responses in burn patients, which could inform future risk-stratification or therapeutic monitoring in UK burns/plastics-surgery units.
Limitations: Small sample size and exploratory subgroup analyses limit generalisability and statistical power, particularly for cell-type-specific EV findings.

Annals of surgery

An Interrupted Time Series Analysis of 2023 Hernia CPT Revisions and Differential Effects on Private Practice Versus Academic Surgeons

Sridalla K, Ly N, Melland-Smith M et al. · 2026 Jul 29
Study Type: Retrospective interrupted time series analysis (registry-based)
Key Question: Did the 2023 CPT coding revisions for ventral hernia repair differentially affect work RVUs (wRVUs) between academic and nonacademic surgeons?
Key Findings:
  • Academic centres saw a significant quarterly increase in mean wRVUs post-revision (+1.98/quarter; 95% CI 1.58–2.38); nonacademic centres showed no change.
  • Large hernia repairs (>10 cm) drove this rise (+1.80 wRVUs/quarter; 95% CI 1.22–2.39), while small and medium hernia wRVUs remained stable.
  • Academic sites coded large hernias more frequently after the revision, suggesting case-mix and coding behaviour shifts rather than uniform reimbursement effects.
Clinical Relevance: UK surgeons and coding leads should note that complexity-based coding reforms can widen reimbursement disparities between complex-case (often tertiary/academic) centres and general practice, relevant to NHS discussions on hernia complexity-based tariff/HRG structuring.
Limitations: The study is US-based (CPT/wRVU system, ACHQC registry), limiting direct generalisability to NHS tariff structures, and cannot establish causality between coding changes and case-mix shifts.

Annals of surgery

Right (H145678) and Left (H123458) 6-Segmentectomies for Malignant Liver Tumors: A Retrospective Analysis of 237 Cases Using Contemporary Surgery Quality Indicators

Azoulay D, Corleone P, Passand GT et al. · 2026 Jul 29
Study Type: Retrospective single-centre cohort study (n=237)
Key Question: What are the contemporary surgical and oncological outcomes—and associated futility rates—of right and left extended 6-segmentectomies (H145678/H123458) for malignant liver tumours, primarily perihilar cholangiocarcinoma?
Key Findings:
  • Surgical futility (90-day mortality) occurred in 15.6%, with a striking failure-to-rescue rate of 30.3%; oncological futility (early recurrence) was 14.5%, giving combined outcome futility of 27.8%.
  • Textbook outcome achieved in only 30.8% of patients; 5-year overall and disease-free survival were 30.8% and 18.9% respectively, over a median 26-month follow-up.
  • 94.9% of patients did not meet liver transplantation criteria, limiting this as an alternative strategy.
Clinical Relevance: These data provide benchmark futility and quality metrics for major hepatobiliary resections increasingly performed in UK tertiary HPB centres, supporting more informed shared decision-making and highlighting failure-to-rescue as a key target for improving outcomes.
Limitations: Single-centre retrospective design over a 31-year period introduces heterogeneity in surgical technique, perioperative care, and case mix, limiting generalisability.

Annals of surgery

Differential Effects of VEGFA and EGF Polymorphisms on Susceptibility and Postoperative Outcome in Cholangiocarcinoma

Pein J, Niklisch S, Horn P et al. · 2026 Jul 29
Study Type: Multi-cohort translational study (single-centre surgical cohort + independent transcriptomic validation cohort + UK Biobank population analysis)
Key Question: Do VEGFA (rs3025039) and EGF (rs4444903) polymorphisms influence susceptibility to and postoperative survival after resection for cholangiocarcinoma (CCA)?
Key Findings:
  • VEGFA rs3025039 T allele associated with improved cancer-specific survival post-resection (notably in intrahepatic CCA), but with increased susceptibility to intrahepatic CCA in UK Biobank (n>500,000).
  • EGF rs4444903 G allele linked to improved survival in perihilar CCA and higher circulating EGF levels, but this survival association was not independent of other prognostic factors when based on tumour mRNA expression.
  • High intratumoral VEGFA expression correlated with poorer survival and pro-proliferative, immune-suppressed transcriptional signatures; EGF expression showed weaker, non-independent effects.
Clinical Relevance: These germline variants may help refine risk stratification and prognostic counselling for UK CCA patients undergoing resection, relevant given the rising HPB cancer burden and interest in biomarker-guided pathways within NHS hepatobiliary MDTs.
Limitations: Findings are largely associative and derived from a modest single-centre surgical cohort, limiting causal inference and generalisability pending prospective validation.

Annals of surgery

Neoadjuvant Immunotherapy Using PD-1 Inhibitors for Resectable Stage III Cutaneous Melanoma-Different Outcomes Between Patients With Lymph Node and In-transit Metastases

Krabbe E, Huibers A, Carneiro A et al. · 2026 Jul 29
Study Type: Population-based multicenter retrospective/observational cohort study
Key Question: Does neoadjuvant PD-1 inhibitor response and outcome differ between stage III melanoma patients with lymph node metastases versus in-transit metastases (ITM)?
Key Findings:
  • Major pathological response was significantly lower in ITM-only (22%) and combined LN+ITM (25%) groups versus LN-only (52%), P<0.001.
  • ITM was independently associated with worse event-free survival (HR 2.00, 95% CI 1.08–3.71) and locoregional recurrence-free interval (HR 4.41, 95% CI 1.76–11.02) versus LN metastases.
  • No significant difference in distant metastasis-free survival after adjustment (HR 1.04, 95% CI 0.46–2.33), suggesting ITM's risk is predominantly locoregional rather than systemic.
Clinical Relevance: UK surgical teams managing stage III melanoma via skin cancer MDTs should recognise ITM as a distinct phenotype with poorer response to neoadjuvant immunotherapy and higher locoregional recurrence risk, warranting closer surveillance and potential consideration of alternative or adjunctive locoregional strategies.
Limitations: Relatively small ITM subgroups (n=41 and n=12) and short median follow-up (14.7 months) limit statistical power and long-term outcome interpretation.

Annals of surgery

The Prognostic Impact of Lymph Node Yield Upon Survival in Esophageal Cancer Broken Down by Neoadjuvant Strategy: A Multicenter European Cohort Study

Guidozzi N, Chou WK, Giorgi L et al. · 2026 Jul 31
Study Type: Multicentre European retrospective cohort study
Key Question: Does lymph node yield (LNY) at esophagectomy influence survival and recurrence differently depending on neoadjuvant strategy (chemotherapy vs chemoradiotherapy) in oesophageal adenocarcinoma?
Key Findings:
  • In the chemotherapy (CT) group, an LNY ≥25 was associated with a 29% reduction in mortality (HR 0.714, P=0.0017); no such LNY-survival relationship existed in the chemoradiotherapy (CRT) group (P=0.66).
  • CT with ≥25 nodes harvested showed ~44% lower mortality than CRT overall (HR 0.563, P<0.001); recurrence was lower with CT (32.7%) than CRT (38.3%, P=0.001).
  • Each additional positive node increased death hazard by 7.3% (CT) and 11.2% (CRT).
Clinical Relevance: Supports tailoring extent of lymphadenectomy to neoadjuvant regimen, suggesting UK units using CT protocols should aim for ≥25 nodes harvested to optimise oncological outcomes.
Limitations: Retrospective design with potential selection and treatment-allocation bias, limiting causal inference regarding LNY's role in CRT-treated patients.

Annals of surgery

Medicaid Expansion Is Associated With Decreased Mortality in Patients Diagnosed With Colorectal Cancer and Synchronous Liver Metastasis

Hohenleitner JT, Gawdi R, Hyman D et al. · 2026 Jul 31
Study Type: Retrospective cohort study using difference-in-differences analysis (SEER database)
Key Question: Does Medicaid expansion (post-ACA) affect treatment patterns and survival in patients under 65 with colorectal cancer and synchronous liver metastasis (CRLM)?
Key Findings:
  • In expansion states, 3-year disease-specific mortality fell from 75% pre-expansion to 68% at ≥6 years post-expansion; overall HR 0.90–0.95 vs non-expansion states, emerging 2–3 years after expansion.
  • Survival benefit was greatest among patients undergoing liver metastasectomy (HR 0.71–0.85).
  • Expansion was not associated with increased rates of metastasectomy (13.4% overall), suggesting benefit relates to continuity of care/systemic therapy access rather than surgical uptake.
Clinical Relevance: Highlights how insurance access and policy-level continuity of care influence oncological outcomes in complex multidisciplinary CRLM management—relevant to NHS clinicians considering equity of access and pathway continuity as determinants of survival, independent of surgical technique.
Limitations: Retrospective registry data cannot fully control for unmeasured confounders (e.g., comorbidity, systemic therapy details, surgical quality) influencing survival differences.
