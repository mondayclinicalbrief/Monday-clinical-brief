American Journal of Orthodontics and Dentofacial Orthopedics

Mechanical properties of bidimensional T-loop closing springs compared with conventional T-loops: A 3-dimensional finite element analysis

Turkmen N, Marsan G · 2026 Oct 24
Study Type: In silico finite element analysis (no clinical or physical testing).
Key Question: Does adding a 90° torsion bend (bidimensional design) to a 0.016 × 0.022-in stainless steel T-loop alter the force system compared with a conventional T-loop?
Key Findings:
  • Bidimensional T-loops produced higher horizontal forces and moments across all interbracket distances (6, 8, 10 mm) and activations (2 mm, 4 mm). At 6 mm IBD and 2 mm activation, alpha-end horizontal force was 3.29 N vs 2.52 N (~30% higher).
  • Alpha-end M/F ratios were higher: 4.14–5.58 mm (bidimensional) vs 3.95–5.11 mm (conventional).
  • The torsion bend reduced alpha bracket-slot angular clearance from 3.8° to 0.95°.
Clinical Relevance: If validated, this design may improve anterior torque control in friction-free space closure, though higher forces could be unwelcome in anchorage-critical or periodontally compromised cases.
Limitations: Findings are purely theoretical; there is no experimental or clinical validation, and the model assumes idealised conditions (single wire, fixed slot dimensions, no biological variables).

American Journal of Orthodontics and Dentofacial Orthopedics

Mandibular symphysis morphology and mandibular incisor inclination across skeletal patterns: A 3-dimensional cone-beam computed tomography analysis

Özkan EÇ, Olcay R · 2026 Oct 26
Study Type: Retrospective cross-sectional CBCT analysis (n=108 adults)
Key Question: How do mandibular symphyseal dimensions relate to mandibular incisor inclination (IMPA) across sagittal and vertical skeletal patterns?
Key Findings:
  • Symphyseal morphology varied significantly by vertical pattern but not sagittal pattern; hyperdivergent patients had larger symphyseal ratios and narrower apical widths (P<0.01).
  • IMPA correlated positively with symphyseal angle and apical width (P<0.001).
  • Sagittal Class II predicted increased IMPA (P<0.001), with no significant sagittal-by-vertical interaction (P>0.05).
Clinical Relevance: Hyperdivergent patients may have less apical bone available for incisor movement, supporting CBCT-informed assessment of lower incisor decompensation limits, though CBCT use is restricted in UK practice by justification and dose considerations (IR(ME)R).
Limitations: Retrospective, cross-sectional design cannot establish causation, and the abstract provides no effect sizes or data on periodontal or treatment outcomes.

American Journal of Orthodontics and Dentofacial Orthopedics

Multiloop edgewise archwire for anterior open bite: A systematic review and meta-analysis of observational studies

Correa E, Michelogiannakis D, Barmak AB et al. · 2026 Oct 29
Study Type: Systematic review and meta-analysis of five retrospective observational studies.
Key Question: Is the multiloop edgewise archwire (MEAW) technique effective for correcting anterior open bite in patients with permanent dentition?
Key Findings:
  • Pooled overbite change was 3.24 mm (95% CI 2.29-4.18 mm). The abstract reports this as MD = -3.24 mm while describing it as an overbite increase, so sign convention is unclear.
  • No significant change in mandibular plane angle.
  • Sensitivity analyses gave consistent effects of roughly 2-4 mm. Certainty of evidence was low (GRADE) for both outcomes, and all studies had moderate risk of bias.
Clinical Relevance: MEAW may achieve clinically meaningful overbite gain without altering vertical skeletal pattern, but the evidence is too weak to support routine use over alternatives such as TADs or elastics in NHS practice.
Limitations: Only five retrospective studies with heterogeneous populations (growing and non-growing, extraction and non-extraction, ages 13-26) and no control groups or RCTs, so the authors concluded effectiveness remains inconclusive.

European Journal of Orthodontics

Osteoblast secretome mediates periodontal ligament stem cells differentiation in response to orthodontic compressive forces: an in vitro study

Brézulier D, Novello S, Le Hyuen KB et al. · 2026 Oct 06
Study Type: In vitro laboratory study (cell culture; no clinical participants).
Key Question: How do secretomes from compressed osteoblasts affect the differentiation of human periodontal ligament stem cells (hPDLSCs)?
Key Findings:
  • Conditioned media from osteoblast spheroids compressed at 1 or 4 g/cm² (24/72 hours) reduced osteogenic marker expression (RunX2, ALP, Col1, OPN) in hPDLSCs compared with uncompressed controls; osteocalcin was unaffected.
  • Sox9 (chondrogenic) was downregulated; adipogenic markers (PPARγ, Adipsin) were unchanged.
  • No mineralisation occurred in any condition, and cell viability was unaffected. Effect sizes and statistics were not reported in the abstract.
Clinical Relevance: This offers a possible paracrine mechanism for suppressed osteogenic activity at compression sites during tooth movement, though it is hypothesis-generating and has no direct bearing on NHS clinical practice at present.
Limitations: A monoculture in vitro model with short exposure cannot replicate in vivo cell-cell signalling, matrix effects, or force dynamics, and the absence of mineralisation in all groups limits interpretation.

Journal of Orthodontics

Does interproximal reduction during clear aligner treatment increase the risk of caries? A retrospective cohort study

Chou AHK, Lee YS, Vaiid N et al. · 2026 Oct 29
Study Type: Retrospective cohort study
Key Question: Does interproximal enamel reduction (IPER) during clear aligner treatment increase proximal caries risk?
Key Findings:
  • 344 patients, 4,800 proximal surfaces assessed; caries found on 66 surfaces (36 patients), so incidence was low in all site and reduction-amount groups.
  • GEE analysis showed some differences by site and amount of IPER, but the authors advise caution given the very low event numbers.
  • IPER up to 1.0 mm per contact (about 0.5 mm per surface), with polishing, hygiene advice and follow-up, was associated with minimal caries.
Clinical Relevance: Offers reassurance that conservative IPER is low-risk in compliant aligner patients, relevant to NHS and private aligner planning, though it does not establish safety for higher-risk patients.
Limitations: There was no untreated control group, and the cohort was low-risk and highly compliant, so the study cannot show whether IPER raises caries risk compared with non-reduced surfaces.

Orthodontics & Craniofacial Research

Curing Capacity of Band-Bonding Resinous Adhesives Under 3D-Printed Molar Bands

Sideri S, Dimitriadi M, Panayi N et al. · 2026 Oct 25
Study Type: In vitro laboratory study (n=15 band specimens, 3 per... five per adhesive group implied).
Key Question: How effectively do three band-bonding resin adhesives cure beneath 3D-printed metallic molar bands?
Key Findings:
  • Conversion beneath bands was reduced versus directly irradiated controls, with the effect depending on adhesive and depth.
  • Band Lock Blue (dual-cured) showed uniformly low DC% in both banded and control specimens. Optiband Ultra Blue was significantly lower than controls at all depths; Transbond Plus was significantly lower only at middle and bottom depths.
  • Authors conclude conversion was "acceptable" with 30 s at 1200 mW/cm². Numerical DC% values and an acceptability threshold are not given in the abstract.
Clinical Relevance: Adhesive choice and light-cure protocol may affect bond integrity under 3D-printed bands, which is relevant as digital workflows enter UK practice; use a high-output unit with the full 30 s.
Limitations: Small, in vitro ATR-FTIR sample without bond strength, clinical failure, or ageing data, so clinical significance of the DC% differences is unestablished.

Orthodontics & Craniofacial Research

Three-Dimensional CBCT Assessment of Potential Orthodontic Mini-Implant Sites in the Pterygomaxillary Region and Pyramidal Process

Alpaydin T, Alpaydin MT, Unal Erzurumlu Z et al. · 2026 Oct 31
Study Type: Retrospective CBCT anatomical study with virtual mini-implant placement (n = 50); no clinical placement performed.
Key Question: Do the pterygomaxillary and pyramidal regions have adequate bone dimensions and density to serve as alternative mini-implant sites?
Key Findings:
  • Pterygomaxillary suture width was significantly greater in males (p < 0.001); pyramidal region dimensions showed no significant sex difference.
  • Virtually placed 2 mm diameter mini-implants were fully surrounded by bone in both regions, maintaining at least a 1 mm margin from vital structures.
  • Abstract reports voxel intensity (bone density proxy) as sufficient but gives no numerical values, implant lengths or angulations.
Clinical Relevance: These sites could offer distal anchorage for maxillary molar or total arch distalisation, though use is likely to remain specialist-level and limited by CBCT access in NHS practice.
Limitations: Findings are purely virtual, with no clinical validation of placement accuracy, stability, failure rates or complications, and voxel intensity is an indirect measure of bone quality.
