American Journal of Orthodontics and Dentofacial Orthopedics

Efficacy of clear aligner treatment in patients with cleft lip and palate: A 3-dimensional linear, angular, and morphometric study of the maxillary arch

Oliva G, D'Antò V, Cirillo L et al. · 2026 Sep 21
Study Type: Retrospective analysis (48 patients with cleft lip with or without palate, first aligner set only).
Key Question: How accurately do clear aligners achieve planned tooth movements and maxillary arch shape changes in patients with cleft lip and palate?
Key Findings:
  • Median accuracy was 51.2% for mesiodistal movements (largest planned, median 2.20 mm) and 60.2% for rotations (median 8.4°); vertical movement was least accurate.
  • For most teeth and movement types, lack of correction exceeded clinically significant thresholds (0.5 mm; 2°).
  • Symmetrical arch shape changes matched the plan (P = 0.499); asymmetrical changes differed significantly from planned (P = 0.02) and produced side effects.
Clinical Relevance: When offering aligners to cleft patients, including those under NHS-commissioned cleft services, expect substantial under-expression of planned movements and plan for refinements, particularly where asymmetric correction is intended.
Limitations: Only the first aligner set was assessed, so findings do not reflect final outcomes after refinement, and no comparison group is described.

American Journal of Orthodontics and Dentofacial Orthopedics

Three-dimensional U-Net-based segmentation of the maxillary incisive canal on cone-beam computed tomography

Huang CI, Xia X, Chen W et al. · 2026 Sep 23
Study Type: Retrospective diagnostic/technical validation study (AI model development and testing)
Key Question: Can a 3D U-Net deep learning model accurately and efficiently segment the maxillary incisive canal on CBCT?
Key Findings:
  • Against manual reference segmentation in 60 test scans, the model achieved a mean Dice coefficient of 0.905, precision 0.892, recall 0.917, and 95% Hausdorff distance of 1.551 mm.
  • Segmentation took about 11 seconds per case versus roughly 30 minutes for manual segmentation.
  • Canal volume and surface area measurements showed high agreement with manual values across morphologic subtypes; some false-positive segmentations occurred.
Clinical Relevance: Automated canal segmentation could support CBCT-based planning of anterior retraction and implant or TAD placement, where the incisive canal is a relevant anatomical constraint, although CBCT use remains restricted to justified cases under IR(ME)R principles.
Limitations: Small single-dataset sample (200 scans) without external validation, and the authors describe it as a preliminary methodologic exploration.

American Journal of Orthodontics and Dentofacial Orthopedics

Clinical considerations for orthodontic management of regenerative endodontic procedure-treated teeth: From immature necrotic roots to ankylosed space maintainer

Einy S, Kaufman AY, Yoshpe M et al. · 2026 Sep 24
Study Type: Narrative review (synthesis of published case reports and case series).
Key Question: How should teeth treated with regenerative endodontic procedures (REPs), including immature necrotic teeth and ankylosed/ERRR teeth, be managed orthodontically?
Key Findings:
  • Orthodontic movement of REP-treated necrotic teeth appears feasible with light, controlled, time-limited forces, ideally using segmental mechanics and close radiographic monitoring. No quantitative data were reported.
  • Outcomes appear more favourable in younger patients, particularly pre-adolescence.
  • In selected cases, REP-treated ERRR/ankylosed teeth may act as temporary natural space maintainers during growth, potentially preserving alveolar bone and delaying decoronation or prosthetic replacement.
Clinical Relevance: Referrals involving REP-treated teeth are likely to increase, and this offers a practical interdisciplinary framework, including confirming clinical and radiographic stability before planning tooth movement.
Limitations: Recommendations rest on low-level evidence (case reports and series) without systematic search methods, so they are best viewed as expert-informed guidance rather than evidence-based protocols.

American Journal of Orthodontics and Dentofacial Orthopedics

Enhancing the predictability of tooth movements with clear aligners-Part 2: Clinical strategies

Long H, Park JH · 2026 Sep 30
Study Type: Narrative review / clinical strategy paper (Part 2 of a two-part series); not original research.
Key Question: What practical strategies can clinicians use to improve the predictability of tooth movement with clear aligners?
Key Findings:
  • Strategies are grouped into three domains: patient-centred (case selection, aligner-favourable planning, difficulty-based management), aligner-mediated (material choice, reducing stress concentration, design optimisation), and practitioner-directed (overtreatment, attachments and auxiliaries, staging that prioritises predictable movements, monitoring and troubleshooting).
  • The framework builds on a biomechanical triad from Part 1: anchorage, stress continuity, and aligner-tooth contact.
  • The authors advise integrating all three domains rather than applying them in isolation. The abstract reports no quantitative outcomes or effect sizes.
Clinical Relevance: Provides a structured approach for UK clinicians managing the common problem of discrepancy between planned and achieved aligner movements, supporting case selection and refinement planning in both private and NHS-adjacent practice.
Limitations: As a narrative strategy paper, the abstract gives no indication of systematic evidence appraisal, so recommendations may reflect expert opinion rather than graded evidence.

European Journal of Orthodontics

Comparison of the effectiveness and efficiency of clear aligners and fixed appliances in the presurgical orthodontic treatment of skeletal Class III malocclusion: a retrospective study

Bai ZP, Wei Z, Zhang XQ et al. · 2026 Aug 06
Study Type: Single-centre retrospective cohort study (n = 95)
Key Question: Do clear aligners achieve presurgical decompensation as effectively and efficiently as fixed appliances in skeletal Class III patients undergoing orthodontic-orthognathic treatment?
Key Findings:
  • Clear aligners were associated with shorter recorded presurgical duration (nonextraction −143.6 days, P = .020; extraction −263.0 days, P = .0005) and fewer visits (−8.41 and −13.52; both P < .001).
  • Adding aligner delivery wait to the model left the extraction difference intact (−231.7 days) but made the nonextraction difference marginal (−117.6 days, P = .055).
  • Maxillary incisor decompensation (primary outcome) was greater with aligners (+4.63° nonextraction, P = .027; +4.37° extraction, P = .044, marginal). No secondary effectiveness outcome survived correction; simulated surgical readiness was equivalent.
Clinical Relevance: Aligners may offer a viable, lower-visit option for presurgical Class III decompensation, which matters for NHS capacity, though the evidence is preliminary.
Limitations: Retrospective, non-randomised design with small subgroups, so confounding by patient selection and appliance choice cannot be excluded.

Journal of Orthodontics

The correlation between mandibular buccal shelf thickness and different vertical facial patterns: A CBCT study

Mirmalek SP, Safi Y, Morvaridi Farimani R · 2026 Sep 21
Study Type: Retrospective cross-sectional CBCT study (n=90 Iranian adults, 22–40 years; single institution).
Key Question: How does mandibular buccal shelf thickness vary with vertical facial pattern, and where is the optimal site for extra-alveolar mini-implant placement?
Key Findings:
  • SN-GoMe angle was negatively correlated with buccal shelf thickness; low-angle patients had thicker bone (correlation coefficient not reported in the abstract).
  • Thickness increased apically and distally: thinnest at the distal root of the first molar at 6 mm from the CEJ (~1.03–1.08 mm), thickest at the distal root of the second molar at 11 mm (~4.74–4.86 mm).
  • No significant sex differences.
Clinical Relevance: Supports targeting the distal second molar region, around 11 mm apical to the CEJ, for buccal shelf mini-screws, with particular caution in hyperdivergent patients, who have thinner bone.
Limitations: Small, single-ethnicity sample with ANB restricted to Class I, so findings may not extend to Class II/III patients or the UK population.

Journal of Orthodontics

Salivary biomarkers during en-masse retraction into recent and healed extraction sites: A randomised controlled trial

Niraj V, Sardana R, Banerjee M et al. · 2026 Sep 28
Study Type: Randomised controlled trial (n=20), prospectively registered
Key Question: Does extracting first premolars immediately before en-masse retraction, rather than at the start of treatment, alter salivary bone-remodelling biomarkers and the rate of space closure?
Key Findings:
  • Both groups showed rising salivary RANKL and falling OPG over 12 weeks; the RANKL:OPG ratio showed a significant time-by-group interaction (P<0.05), but between-group differences at individual time points did not survive multiple-comparison correction.
  • Tooth movement was faster in the recent extraction group (mean difference 0.07 mm/week, 95% CI 0.04–0.09), which the authors consider probably not clinically significant.
Clinical Relevance: Extraction timing is unlikely to meaningfully change space closure speed, so scheduling in NHS practice can follow logistics rather than expected treatment efficiency.
Limitations: The very small sample (n=20) limits power and generalisability, and salivary biomarkers remain unvalidated as surrogates for clinical outcomes.

Orthodontics & Craniofacial Research

Finite Element Analysis of Maxillary Molar Intrusion Using Temporary Anchorage Devices: A Comparison of Direct and Indirect Force Loading

Chou AHK, Lee YS, Shoaib A et al. · 2026 Sep 20
Study Type: Finite element analysis (in silico biomechanical study); no clinical participants.
Key Question: Does indirect force loading via a modified C-palatal plate (MCPP) intrude maxillary first molars as effectively as direct TAD-based loading, and with what root stress?
Key Findings:
  • Hook position on the MCPP determined movement: 3 mm coronal to the centre of resistance caused distalisation with extrusion; at the centre of resistance, distalisation with negligible vertical change; 5 mm apical, intrusion with minimal distalisation.
  • With the same force magnitude, the apical hook position (M1-C) produced intrusion similar to buccal-palatal TADs with elastic.
  • M1-C generated lower Von Mises stress at the root surfaces than the TAD configuration (no numerical values given in abstract).
Clinical Relevance: Suggests palatal plate hook position could offer controlled molar intrusion or distalisation, relevant for anterior open bite or space management where skeletal anchorage is considered.
Limitations: Single virtual model from one skull-derived CT, with no clinical validation, so findings may not reflect periodontal or bone remodelling responses in patients.
