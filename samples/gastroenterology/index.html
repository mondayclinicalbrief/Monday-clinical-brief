Serotonergic signaling in gastric physiology and motility disorders

Konings B, Balsiger LM, Schol J et al. · 2026 Jul 22
Study Type: Narrative review
Key Question: What is the role of serotonergic signalling in gastric physiology, and how can this inform therapeutic strategies in gastroparesis (GP) and functional dyspepsia (FD)?
Key Findings:
  • 5-HT4 agonists have the most consistent guideline support for GP, improving gastric emptying and symptoms; 5-HT3 antagonists remain useful for symptomatic nausea/vomiting control.
  • In FD, serotonergic agents may be selectively used off-label by symptom phenotype: mirtazapine (5-HT2 antagonist) for early satiety with weight loss; buspirone (5-HT1A agonist) for early satiety, fullness, or bloating.
  • Newer selective 5-HT4 agonists (e.g., naronapride) may offer efficacy with improved cardiovascular safety versus earlier agents like cisapride; SSRIs/SNRIs are not currently recommended despite theoretical rationale from chronic pain literature.
Clinical Relevance: Provides UK gastroenterologists a structured, mechanism-based framework for selecting serotonergic therapies in GP and FD, an area with limited licensed options and significant unmet need within NHS dyspepsia/motility pathways.
Limitations: As a narrative review, it highlights an absence of large controlled trials, limiting the strength of therapeutic recommendations.

AI-Assisted Risk Stratification in Stage II Colorectal Cancer: Multi-Institutional Validation of Semantically-Enhanced Deep Learning

Magisson F, He Z, Millward J et al. · 2026 Jul 28
Study Type: Multi-institutional retrospective validation study (AI model development and external validation)
Key Question: Can a semantically-enhanced deep learning model (SÉMIL) improve prognostic risk stratification in stage II colorectal cancer beyond current histopathological assessment?
Key Findings:
  • SÉMIL outperformed non-semantic MIL for binary invasiveness classification across external cohorts (AUC 0.713–0.821 vs 0.686–0.803).
  • Significant survival stratification demonstrated internally (HR=4.73, p=0.0012) and externally (MCO HR=2.84; DYNAMIC HR=2.10), including within NCCN-defined high-risk stage II patients (HR 2.96–3.50 across cohorts).
  • Retained independent prognostic value on multivariate analysis (HR=1.98, p=0.005) adjusting for T stage, MMR status, and nodal yield.
Clinical Relevance: This tool could refine adjuvant chemotherapy decisions in stage II colorectal cancer, particularly within the NCCN high-risk subgroup where treatment benefit is currently uncertain—relevant to NHS MDT decision-making on adjuvant therapy pathways.
Limitations: Retrospective design without prospective clinical outcome validation or assessment of impact on actual treatment decisions.

Clonal diversity underpins distinct modes of recurrence in hepatocellular carcinoma: the PLANet cohort study

Zhang Y, Sekar K, Phua CZJ et al. · 2026 Jul 21
Study Type: Prospective multi-region genomic/transcriptomic cohort study (PLANet cohort)
Key Question: What clonal and molecular mechanisms underlie post-resection recurrence patterns in hepatocellular carcinoma, and can these inform risk stratification?
Key Findings:
  • Two distinct recurrence patterns identified: polyclonal seeding (>50% of intrahepatic recurrences), associated with early recurrence, high phenotypic plasticity, and a Treg-enriched immunosuppressive microenvironment; versus monoclonal seeding, predominant in distant metastases, typically arising from an aggressive C5/C6 primary subclone.
  • Polyclonal intrahepatic recurrences showed greater genomic instability and higher predicted sensitivity to immune-checkpoint blockade.
  • A multi-omics predictive model achieved AUC=0.86 for identifying high-risk patients despite curative-intent resection.
Clinical Relevance: These findings could refine post-resection surveillance and support biomarker-guided selection of patients for adjuvant immunotherapy trials within UK HCC surgical pathways, where recurrence remains a major driver of poor outcomes.
Limitations: Small recurrence cohort (n=24) limits generalisability and statistical power for subgroup and predictive model validation.

Deciphering the prodrome of inflammatory bowel disease up to 10 years before disease onset by massively parallel serology

Bourgonje AR, Innocenti G, Livanos AE et al. · 2026 Jul 23
Study Type: Retrospective longitudinal cohort study (biomarker discovery, nested case-control design)
Key Question: Can broad serological antibody profiling reveal disease-specific immune changes years before clinical onset of Crohn's disease (CD) and ulcerative colitis (UC)?
Key Findings:
  • Antibody repertoire variability increased up to ~4 years before diagnosis in both pre-CD and pre-UC individuals.
  • Pre-CD showed elevated herpesvirus (especially EBV) and anti-flagellin antibodies up to 10 years pre-diagnosis, more pronounced in complicated/ileal phenotypes; responses to encapsulated bacteria (pneumococcus, Haemophilus, Neisseria) progressively declined towards diagnosis.
  • Pre-UC exhibited combined antimicrobial, antiviral, and autoantibody signatures (including anti-MAP kinase-activating death domain protein antibodies).
Clinical Relevance: These findings suggest distinct, detectable immunological trajectories predate IBD onset by up to a decade, offering potential for future risk stratification or early-detection strategies relevant to at-risk populations (e.g., first-degree relatives) within UK IBD services.
Limitations: The cohort derives from a US military population (younger, predominantly male), limiting generalisability to the broader UK IBD demographic, and findings are associative rather than mechanistic.

Ustekinumab for fistulising perianal Crohn's disease: a randomised placebo-controlled trial from the GETAID

Wils P, Nancey S, Messmer E et al. · 2026 Jul 24
Study Type: Randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial (multicentre, GETAID)
Key Question: Is ustekinumab more effective than placebo for inducing combined clinical and radiological remission in fistulising perianal Crohn's disease after standardised surgical management?
Key Findings:
  • Combined remission at week 12: 62% (UST) vs 25% (placebo), OR 5.1 (95% CI 1.07–24.4).
  • Clinical remission 62.5% vs 31% (OR 3.75, 95% CI 0.84–16.8); radiological remission 87.5% vs 75% (OR 2.7, 95% CI 0.34–21.1) — not statistically significant.
  • 69% had prior anti-TNF failure; by week 48, remission rates converged (31% UST vs 50% placebo-switched), suggesting attenuation of benefit over time.
Clinical Relevance: Provides the first placebo-controlled RCT evidence supporting ustekinumab as a viable option for anti-TNF-refractory fistulising perianal CD, relevant to UK biologic sequencing decisions in complex perianal disease pathways.
Limitations: Small sample size (n=32) limits statistical power and generalisability, particularly for secondary and long-term endpoints.

Impact of initial severity and progression pattern of new-onset diabetes on pancreatic cancer risk: a 15-year longitudinal nationwide cohort study

Lee M, Yu MH, Chae W et al. · 2026 Jul 24
Study Type: Retrospective nationwide cohort study
Key Question: Does the severity and early trajectory of new-onset diabetes (NOD) predict subsequent pancreatic cancer risk?
Key Findings:
  • Pancreatic cancer risk rose stepwise with baseline treatment intensity versus no diabetes: aHR 3.01 (no antidiabetics), 4.32 (oral agents), 5.60 (insulin).
  • Rapid treatment escalation within 6 months conferred higher risk than stable/decreased intensity within the same baseline category.
  • Drug-naïve patients who required treatment within 6 months had higher risk (aHR 6.50) than those started on stable oral therapy (aHR 3.67); associations were strongest for cancers diagnosed within 3 years of NOD.
Clinical Relevance: These findings support using NOD severity and early glycaemic trajectory as a pragmatic risk-stratification tool to identify patients warranting closer surveillance or referral for occult pancreatic cancer within UK diabetes and gastroenterology pathways.
Limitations: Observational design using treatment intensity as a proxy for diabetes severity limits causal inference and may not fully capture reverse causation from subclinical cancer.

In-person therapist-delivered hypnotherapy versus smartphone-based self-guided hypnotherapy in IBS: a multicentre three-armed randomised controlled trial

Snijkers JTW, Bosman MHMA, Winkens B et al. · 2026 Jul 25
Study Type: RCT (multicentre, three-armed, non-inferiority)
Key Question: Is smartphone-based self-guided hypnotherapy non-inferior to in-person therapist-delivered hypnotherapy for reducing abdominal pain in IBS?
Key Findings:
  • FDA abdominal pain response rates: 48% (in-person hypnotherapy) vs 33% (app-based hypnotherapy) vs 22% (psychoeducation); non-inferiority not demonstrated (difference -14.7%, 95% CI -29.3% to 0.9%).
  • In-person hypnotherapy outperformed psychoeducation at week 16 (OR 3.48, 95% CI 1.68–7.20); both hypnotherapy arms remained superior to psychoeducation at 6 months (OR 3.11 and 4.68 respectively, both p≤0.007).
  • No significant between-group differences in psychological symptom outcomes.
Clinical Relevance: Given constrained access to gut-directed hypnotherapy within NHS gastroenterology/psychological services, app-based self-guided hypnotherapy offers a scalable, lower-cost option with meaningful (if inferior) efficacy compared with therapist-led care.
Limitations: Non-inferiority margin was not met, limiting confidence in equivalence, and durability/self-selection effects in a self-guided digital format warrant further real-world evaluation.

Galectin-14, a primate-specific oncofetal protein, impairs anti-tumour immunity in hepatocellular carcinoma by driving elevated heparan sulphate modification

Yang G, Gou L, Liu X et al. · 2026 Jul 28
Study Type: Translational/mechanistic research (combined clinical correlation, in vitro, and transgenic mouse models)
Key Question: What drives aberrant heparan sulphate (HS) modification in hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC), and how does this contribute to T cell exhaustion and immune evasion?
Key Findings:
  • Elevated tumour HS modification correlated with poorer survival and CD8+ T cell exhaustion in HCC patients.
  • Galectin-14, a primate-specific oncofetal protein, was identified as the upstream driver of HS synthesis, inducing contact-dependent CD8+ T cell exhaustion partly via FGFR1 signalling.
  • Galectin-14 binds UGP2, promoting its oligomerisation and activity, boosting UDP-glycosyl donor production (UDP-Glc, UDP-GlcNAc, UDP-GlcA) and thereby HS chain synthesis.
Clinical Relevance: Identifies galectin-14 and HS pathway components as candidate biomarkers and immunotherapy targets for HCC, a cancer with limited treatment options and rising incidence in UK liver disease populations.
Limitations: Findings are largely mechanistic (in vitro/transgenic models) with correlative human data; clinical validation and therapeutic targeting in patients are yet to be established.
