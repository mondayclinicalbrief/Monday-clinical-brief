The Journal of Thoracic and Cardiovascular Surgery

Contemporary Results of Operations for Mitral Regurgitation Caused by Posterior Mitral Valve Prolapse: The AATS Adult Quality Gateway

Gillinov M, Svensson LG, Blackstone EH et al. · 2026 Oct 21
Study Type: Retrospective multicentre registry-based analysis, benchmarking observed outcomes against two risk-prediction tools (AATS AQG and STS-ACSD).
Key Question: How do outcomes of surgery for posterior mitral valve prolapse in high- versus low-volume hospitals compare with those predicted by the AQG and STS-ACSD models?
Key Findings:
  • High-volume centres (n=1,517; 97% repair) had observed mortality of 0.070% (1 death) against predicted 0.87% (AQG) and 0.77% (STS-ACSD); major morbidity/mortality was 4.0% versus 8.1% and 6.5% predicted.
  • Low-volume hospitals (n=159; 91% repair) had worse repair outcomes, including operative mortality of 2.1% (3/145).
  • AQG, which needs limited data entry, gave predictions comparable to STS-ACSD.
Clinical Relevance: Supports a strong volume-outcome relationship in degenerative mitral repair, relevant to UK debates on centralisation and surgeon-level benchmarking against risk models.
Limitations: Low-volume cohort was small, and the abstract gives no adjusted comparison or confidence intervals for the volume effect.

The Annals of Thoracic Surgery

Lifetime Management of Aortic Stenosis: Expert Review of Evidence and Consensus Guidelines

Centofanti P, Rinaldi M, Di Eusanio M et al. · 2026 Oct 26
Study Type: Narrative expert review with consensus recommendations (Italian Cardiac Surgery Working Group); not a systematic review, with no stated search strategy or risk-of-bias assessment.
Key Question: How should the 2025 ESC/EACTS Class I recommendation for transfemoral TAVI in patients aged ≥70 years be applied to patients with long life expectancy, considering durability and future reintervention?
Key Findings:
  • TAVI's early non-inferiority to SAVR may attenuate with longer follow-up, particularly in patients with substantial life expectancy.
  • Randomised durability data are limited; propensity-matched registries suggest possible higher late mortality after TAVI in younger, lower-risk patients.
  • Reintervention after TAVI-first strategies may carry increasing technical complexity and procedural risk.
Clinical Relevance: Supports Heart Team discussions of lifetime management as UK practice adopts the 2025 guidelines, favouring SAVR as a reference strategy in long-survival patients and TAVI where life expectancy is limited or surgical risk is high.
Limitations: Conclusions rest on a non-systematic synthesis, with the late-mortality signal drawn from observational registries vulnerable to residual confounding.

The Journal of Thoracic and Cardiovascular Surgery

Distal Reinterventions after Branched Stented Anastomosis Frozen Elephant Trunk Repair (B-SAFER): A Report from a Prospective Trial

Vargo PR, Leib P, Pasricha A et al. · 2026 Oct 23
Study Type: Prospective, single-arm, non-randomised trial (PS-IDE cohort), single centre; analysis of distal reinterventions after B-SAFER.
Key Question: What are the rates, outcomes and predictors of distal aortic reintervention after branched frozen elephant trunk repair (B-SAFER)?
Key Findings:
  • 117/405 patients (29%) required 148 distal reinterventions, 92% planned; median time to reintervention 8.7 months.
  • Mortality after first reintervention was 6.5% (n=8); one permanent stroke (0.8%), no paralysis. Freedom from reintervention at 2 years was 64% overall (chronic dissection 48%, degenerative 71%, acute dissection 74%).
  • Larger descending and abdominal aortic diameters were the strongest predictors; survival at 2.5 years was 77–81% across groups.
Clinical Relevance: Staged distal repair after FET should be anticipated, particularly in chronic dissection, which is relevant to UK aortic MDT planning and surveillance; B-SAFER is an investigational device platform, not currently routine NHS practice.
Limitations: Short median follow-up (1.3 years), single-arm design with no comparator, and no data on timing thresholds, so conclusions on earlier reintervention remain hypothesis-generating.

The Annals of Thoracic Surgery

Mitral Annular Calcification: Operative Decision Making and Technical Considerations

Kreso A, Bihun R, Melnitchouk S · 2026 Oct 22
Study Type: Narrative expert review with a proposed decision algorithm (not a systematic review; no formal search methodology or quantitative synthesis described).
Key Question: How should operative strategy for mitral valve surgery be selected and executed in patients with mitral annular calcification (MAC)?
Key Findings:
  • A four-category, severity-based decision matrix guides planning, with management spanning "respect the MAC" (work around calcium) to "resect the MAC" (full resection with atrioventricular groove reconstruction).
  • Mild MAC: repair remains feasible and preferred. Moderate MAC: individualised; repair is reasonable if leaflet mobility is preserved and decalcification allows secure annuloplasty, otherwise replacement.
  • Severe or circumferential MAC warrants tailored approaches, including transcatheter or hybrid strategies. Decalcification extent should reflect patient risk and operator expertise.
Clinical Relevance: MAC is increasingly encountered in an older, comorbid UK surgical population, and the framework may support MDT discussion of repair, replacement, or transcatheter options.
Limitations: The algorithm is expert-derived, not validated against outcomes, and the abstract provides no quantitative data on complication rates or comparative durability.

The Annals of Thoracic Surgery

One Year Survival of High-Risk Patients Undergoing Coronary Revascularization

Caldeira CCB, Mueller DK, Dewey TM et al. · 2026 Oct 24
Study Type: Retrospective registry cohort study (STS ACSD linked to CMS claims, 2012–2022)
Key Question: Do high-risk features in isolated CABG patients (≥65 years, STS PROM >4%) carry different prognostic weight for perioperative versus one-year mortality?
Key Findings:
  • Of 75,385 patients, 89.4% survived 30 days; 13.4% of those 30-day survivors died by one year.
  • Liver disease and home oxygen predicted mortality at both 30 days and one year.
  • Unresponsive state, prior CABG, PCI within 6 hours, cardiogenic shock and temporary mechanical support predicted early mortality only. Cancer, dialysis and Medicaid coverage were neutral at 30 days but independently predicted one-year mortality.
Clinical Relevance: Supports a more nuanced risk discussion in heart team and consent settings, as PROM-type scores may not distinguish early-hazard from late-hazard phenotypes, though this US Medicare-aged cohort may not map directly onto UK practice.
Limitations: Observational design with no effect sizes in the abstract, and Medicaid status is a US-specific variable of limited transferability to the NHS.

European Journal of Cardio-Thoracic Surgery

Electrosurgical septotomy across the full spectrum of aortic dissection

Weiss G, Trnka H, Tauber S et al. · 2026 Oct 21
Study Type: Single-centre retrospective intention-to-treat case series (n=21, consecutive).
Key Question: Is transcatheter electrosurgical septotomy feasible and safe across the full spectrum of descending aortic dissection, including as stand-alone treatment for malperfusion?
Key Findings:
  • Technical success 95% (20/21). Indications: landing-zone preparation (57%), malperfusion (33%), preparation for open repair, true-lumen collapse.
  • Malperfusion resolved in all 7 treated patients; median perfused-lumen index rose from 0.16 to 0.98 (n=18, P<0.001).
  • No stroke, spinal cord ischaemia or retrograde type A dissection. Mortality: 10% at 30 days, 14% in-hospital, 19% overall (one fatal delayed rupture); 3 unplanned reinterventions at median 5.1 months.
Clinical Relevance: Offers a possible alternative or adjunct to fenestration or stenting for dissection malperfusion using standard electrosurgical equipment, though UK practice would need formal evaluation before adoption.
Limitations: Small, heterogeneous, retrospective cohort with short follow-up and no comparator, so feasibility is shown but durability and comparative safety are not.

Interdisciplinary CardioVascular and Thoracic Surgery

Stenosis of a novel frozen elephant trunk in a patient with chronic dissection

Tokunaga S, Okamura H, Yamaguchi A · 2026 Oct 20
Study Type: Single case report.
Key Question: Can a novel low-radial-force partial elephant trunk (PET) fail to expand adequately in chronic aortic dissection, and how should this be managed?
Key Findings:
  • After total arch replacement with a PET for chronic dissection, lower-to-upper limb blood pressure ratio fell to 90% post-bypass, with incomplete stent expansion on intraoperative TOE.
  • By postoperative day 1 the ratio had worsened to 70%, attributed to PET stenosis at a steeply angulated arch.
  • Emergency TEVAR within the PET fully resolved the pressure gradient.
Clinical Relevance: Surgeons adopting newer frozen elephant trunk devices designed to reduce distal stent graft-induced new entry should anticipate possible under-expansion in chronic dissection, particularly with steep arch angulation, and maintain a low threshold for limb pressure monitoring and bailout TEVAR.
Limitations: A single case provides no incidence data, and the proposed mechanism (thinner nitinol wire, lower radial force, arch angulation) is inferred rather than demonstrated.

Interdisciplinary CardioVascular and Thoracic Surgery

Operating Room-Complete Venovenous Extracorporeal Membrane Oxygenation for Thoracoscopic Surgery Without Repositioning

Murakami J, Yoshimine S, Tanaka T et al. · 2026 Oct 21
Study Type: Small single-centre case series (n=5) describing a technique.
Key Question: Can short-duration VV-ECMO be established and managed entirely in the operating room, without repositioning, to support thoracoscopic surgery in patients who cannot tolerate one-lung ventilation?
Key Findings:
  • Semi-supine positioning with femoral cannulation (purse-string closure) and table rotation allowed VATS without turning the patient while circuit-dependent.
  • Five of 548 thoracoscopic procedures (Oct 2022–Jun 2025) used the approach; operative time 124–172 min, ECMO support 27–69 min.
  • All patients were weaned in theatre. No thromboembolic or haemorrhagic complications occurred despite no systemic or prime heparin (heparin-bonded circuit, 2.5–3.5 L/min flow).
Clinical Relevance: May offer a simpler, safer option for high-risk patients needing brief VATS, though UK practice and ECMO governance would need local consideration.
Limitations: Only five cases with no comparator group, so safety and generalisability, particularly of the heparin-free strategy, cannot be established.
