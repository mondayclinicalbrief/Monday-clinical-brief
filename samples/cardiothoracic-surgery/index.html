The role of selective multidetector computed tomography after transcatheter aortic valve implantation

Moscarelli M, Pibarot P, Van Mieghem N et al. · 2026 Jul 22
Study Type: State-of-the-Art review (narrative), not original research.
Key Question: What is the role of multidetector CT alongside echocardiography in surveillance for subclinical leaflet thrombosis and structural abnormalities after TAVI?
Key Findings:
  • Echocardiography alone may miss early structural/geometric changes; CT enables detailed assessment of leaflet morphology, perivalvular thrombus and prosthesis geometry.
  • Prosthesis under-expansion, ellipticity, commissural misalignment, canting and implantation depth are identified as mechanical contributors to altered neo-sinus flow and thrombogenesis.
  • The authors propose a pragmatic surveillance framework combining routine echocardiography with selective CT imaging, rather than universal CT use.
Clinical Relevance: As TAVI expands into younger, lower-risk UK patients with longer expected prosthesis lifespan, structured imaging surveillance strategies may help identify thrombogenic valve phenotypes early, informing individualised follow-up and anticoagulation decisions within NHS TAVI services.
Limitations: As a narrative review, it synthesises existing evidence without new clinical data, and lacks validated criteria for selecting which patients require CT surveillance.

European journal of cardio-thoracic surgery : official journal of the European Association for Cardio-thoracic Surgery

Anti-thrombotic therapy after mitral valve surgery

Kirmani BH, Gillinov AM, Rogers LJ et al. · 2026 Jul 29
Study Type: Narrative review
Key Question: What is the evidence base for antithrombotic therapy following mitral valve surgery, and how robust are current guideline recommendations?
Key Findings:
  • Current guideline recommendations for post-mitral valve surgery antithrombotic therapy are largely extrapolated from aortic valve procedure data, despite mitral valve disease carrying substantially higher thrombotic risk.
  • The review outlines pathophysiological mechanisms underlying elevated thrombogenicity in mitral valve conditions.
  • Significant evidence gaps and guideline uncertainties are identified, without abstract-reported comparative outcome data.
Clinical Relevance: UK cardiothoracic teams frequently manage anticoagulation/antiplatelet decisions after mitral repair or replacement, and this review highlights that current practice may be based on inappropriately extrapolated evidence, prompting more cautious, mitral-specific decision-making.
Limitations: As a narrative review, it synthesises existing guideline evidence rather than presenting new primary data, limiting the strength of practice-changing conclusions.

European journal of cardio-thoracic surgery : official journal of the European Association for Cardio-thoracic Surgery

Intercostal nerve cryoablation versus thoracic epidural analgesia for minimal invasive repair of pectus excavatum: a randomized clinical trial (ICE-trial)

van Polen EJ, Duijsens GH, Janssen N et al. · 2026 Jul 29
Study Type: Randomized controlled trial (single-centre, unblinded, superiority design)
Key Question: Does intercostal nerve cryoablation (INC) improve recovery after the Nuss procedure compared with thoracic epidural analgesia (TEA)?
Key Findings:
  • Length of hospital stay markedly shorter with INC (median 1 day [IQR 1–1] vs 4 days [IQR 4–4], p<0.001, large effect size r=-0.94).
  • Opioid consumption reduced by 83% in-hospital and 97% post-discharge with INC (p<0.001); pain scores lower at days 7 and 14 (p=0.010, p=0.035); earlier mobilisation (days 1–2, p<0.05).
  • No significant difference in return to work/school; 56% of INC patients had persistent chest wall hypoesthesia at 6 months.
Clinical Relevance: For NHS paediatric/adolescent thoracic services, INC could substantially cut inpatient stay and opioid burden after Nuss repair, supporting enhanced recovery pathways, though sensory sequelae need patient counselling.
Limitations: Single-centre, unblinded design limits generalisability and introduces potential bias, with long-term sensory outcomes beyond 6 months not yet established.

Interdisciplinary cardiovascular and thoracic surgery

An unusual pulmonary vascular connection encountered during right upper lobectomy

Sencan TB, Arafa M, Arici FT et al. · 2026 Jul 22
Study Type: Case report
Key Question: How should surgeons manage an unexpected pulmonary arteriovenous fistula encountered intraoperatively during lobectomy for lung cancer?
Key Findings:
  • A 77-year-old woman undergoing right upper lobectomy for Stage IA invasive mucinous adenocarcinoma (mixed with large-cell neuroendocrine carcinoma) was found intraoperatively to have an anomalous vascular connection between the superior pulmonary vein and interlobar pulmonary artery, consistent with an arteriovenous fistula.
  • The anomaly was not identified on preoperative imaging (PET-CT showed only a 2 cm nodule with low FDG uptake) and was managed successfully by intraoperative ligation.
Clinical Relevance: Highlights the need for vigilance regarding unrecognised pulmonary vascular anomalies during VATS/thoracotomy lobectomy, as unexpected vascular variants can precipitate major haemorrhage if not anticipated and controlled—relevant to UK thoracic surgical training and intraoperative risk management.
Limitations: Single case report, limiting generalisability regarding incidence, preoperative detection, and optimal management strategy.

Interdisciplinary cardiovascular and thoracic surgery

Spread through air spaces in multiple synchronous bilateral primary lung cancer: prognostic implications in a surgical cohort

Bongiolatti S, Tombelli S, Burlone A et al. · 2026 Jul 25
Study Type: Retrospective cohort study (single-arm surgical series)
Key Question: In patients undergoing staged bilateral resection for multiple synchronous bilateral primary lung cancer (mSBPLC), what oncologic outcomes result and which factors predict worse survival?
Key Findings:
  • Among 45 operated patients (median follow-up 34 months), 5-year overall survival was 77% and disease-free survival 57%.
  • STAS (present in 17.8%) was strongly associated with worse outcomes: 5-year OS 0% vs 85% (p<0.01) and DFS 0% vs 63% (p<0.01) compared with STAS-negative patients.
  • STAS independently predicted worse OS (HR 7.08, p=0.02) and DFS (HR 5.63, p=0.017) on multivariable analysis.
Clinical Relevance: Supports staged bilateral anatomical resection as a safe, oncologically viable strategy for selected UK patients with mSBPLC, while highlighting STAS as a key pathological marker that could inform postoperative surveillance intensity and adjuvant therapy discussions in MDT settings.
Limitations: Small, highly selected single-centre cohort with limited statistical power, restricting generalisability and robustness of the STAS association.

Interdisciplinary cardiovascular and thoracic surgery

Haematocrit during cardiopulmonary bypass and risk for acute kidney injury: An observational single centre study

Hjärpe AK, Jeppsson A, Lannemyr L et al. · 2026 Jul 25
Study Type: Observational cohort study (registry-based, single centre)
Key Question: Is there a haematocrit range during cardiopulmonary bypass, specifically during aortic cross-clamp, associated with reduced risk of postoperative acute kidney injury (AKI)?
Key Findings:
  • Among 3798 patients, AKI occurred in 15.7% (598 patients).
  • A non-linear, U-shaped association was found: AKI risk rose with weighted haematocrit exposure below 25% (aOR 1.006 per 1%×min; 95% CI 1.002–1.009; p<0.001) and above 38% (aOR 1.006 per 1%×min; 95% CI 1.001–1.011; p=0.019).
  • Findings suggest a "safe" haematocrit window (25–38%) during cross-clamp may minimise AKI risk.
Clinical Relevance: These findings support cautious haemodilution and transfusion practice during CPB, relevant to UK bypass protocols where haematocrit targets are often guided by transfusion-avoidance strategies without upper-limit consideration.
Limitations: Observational design with registry-derived AKI definitions limits causal inference and generalisability; residual confounding cannot be excluded.

Clinical outcomes of ascending aortic replacement for aneurysm and the impact of center volume: a nationwide population-based study

Kim SH, Kim J, Kang Y et al. · 2026 Jul 27
Study Type: Retrospective nationwide population-based cohort study
Key Question: Does institutional case volume affect outcomes after elective ascending aortic replacement (AAR) for aneurysm?
Key Findings:
  • Among 5,209 patients, operative mortality was 2.9% and stroke rate 2.2%.
  • High-volume centers (≥10 cases/year) had lower operative mortality (OR 0.26, 95% CI 0.18–0.37) and lower long-term mortality (HR 0.64, 95% CI 0.57–0.73), with a non-linear inverse volume-outcome relationship.
  • Bicuspid aortic valve and concomitant aortic valve surgery were associated with more favourable survival.
Clinical Relevance: Supports centralisation of elective aortic surgery to high-volume centres, relevant to NHS service planning and commissioning of specialist aortic networks.
Limitations: Retrospective registry design limits control for unmeasured confounders (e.g., surgeon experience, anatomical severity) despite adjustment.

Haemodynamics from induction to cardiopulmonary bypass improve prediction of right ventricular failure following left ventricular assist device

Rajendra S, Salmasi Y, Raj B et al. · 2026 Jul 29
Study Type: Retrospective single-centre cohort study
Key Question: Does incorporating pre-CPB intraoperative haemodynamic data improve prediction of right ventricular failure (RVF) after LVAD implantation compared with preoperative assessment alone?
Key Findings:
  • RVF occurred in 36/203 patients (17.7%); no preoperative RHC parameter independently predicted RVF.
  • A pre-CPB model incorporating MAP, PAPi, and maximum vasoactive-inotropic score achieved strong discrimination (AUC 0.834), significantly outperforming both the preoperative model (AUC 0.747, p=0.049) and EUROMACS-RHF score (p=0.0299).
  • The preoperative model alone did not significantly outperform EUROMACS-RHF (p=0.222).
Clinical Relevance: For UK LVAD centres, real-time intraoperative haemodynamic markers could refine RVF risk stratification and prompt earlier mechanical RV support, potentially improving outcomes in a resource-intensive, high-risk patient group.
Limitations: Single-centre retrospective design limits generalisability and requires external validation before clinical adoption.
