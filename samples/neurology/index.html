Age Is an Effect Modifier of Thrombectomy Benefit in a Socioeconomically Vulnerable Stroke Population

Lima FO, Mont'Alverne FJA, Rebello LC et al. · 2026 Jul 24
Study Type: Pre-specified subgroup analysis of a randomised controlled trial (RESILIENT trial)
Key Question: Does age modify the benefit of endovascular thrombectomy (EVT) for large vessel occlusion stroke in a resource-limited public healthcare system?
Key Findings:
  • Increasing age independently reduced odds of 90-day functional independence (OR 0.97, 95% CI 0.95–0.99, p=0.01).
  • EVT benefit was substantial in patients <70 years (OR 4.08, 95% CI 1.71–9.76) but attenuated in elderly (≥70, OR 0.90) and very elderly (≥80, OR 0.36) patients, with significant age–treatment interaction (p=0.03 for both).
Clinical Relevance: These findings challenge assumptions from high-income-country trials that EVT benefit is age-independent, suggesting UK clinicians should consider system-level and population factors (e.g., resource constraints, comorbidity burden) when extrapolating thrombectomy eligibility criteria to socioeconomically vulnerable groups.
Limitations: Small subgroup sizes (particularly very elderly, n=37) limit statistical power and generalisability of the interaction findings.

Regional and Compartmental Changes Drive Progressive Patterns of Striatal Degeneration in X-Linked Dystonia Parkinsonism

Tan AYS, Thai NSSS, Arasaratnam CJ et al. · 2026 Jul 26
Study Type: Post-mortem neuropathological case-control study
Key Question: How does striatal neurodegeneration progress regionally and by compartment (matrix vs striosome) in X-linked dystonia parkinsonism (XDP), and how does this relate to clinical course?
Key Findings:
  • Striatal degeneration in XDP follows a dorsoventral gradient, with the matrix compartment showing greater vulnerability than striosomes, contrary to earlier assumptions.
  • Degeneration pattern correlates more strongly with disease duration than with SINE-VNTR-Alu repeat length.
  • Repeat length inversely correlates with age of symptom onset, indirectly shaping the clinical timeline via onset rather than degeneration pattern itself.
Clinical Relevance: These findings refine understanding of XDP pathophysiology as a progressive cortico-basal ganglia circuit disorder, relevant to UK clinicians managing patients of Filipino descent (the population predominantly affected) and informing prognostic counselling and future biomarker/therapeutic targeting.
Limitations: Small post-mortem sample (18 XDP cases) limits generalisability and precludes definitive longitudinal correlation with clinical progression.

Blood Biomarkers of Alzheimer's Disease and Patterns of Structural Brain Changes in the Community

Valletta M, Vetrano DL, Laukka EJ et al. · 2026 Jul 27
Study Type: Prospective population-based cohort study
Key Question: Do blood-based AD biomarkers (p-tau217, NfL, GFAP) predict distinct patterns of structural brain change in cognitively unimpaired community-dwelling older adults?
Key Findings:
  • Higher p-tau217 predicted selective accelerated atrophy in hippocampus (β=-0.047/year, 95% CI -0.077 to -0.018) and amygdala (β=-0.064, 95% CI -0.105 to -0.022).
  • Elevated GFAP was associated with global brain volume decline, ventricular enlargement, and hippocampal shrinkage.
  • Higher NfL was linked to the broadest pattern: global atrophy, ventricular enlargement, hippocampal/amygdala shrinkage, and faster white matter hyperintensity accumulation; findings persisted after excluding baseline MCI.
Clinical Relevance: Supports the potential utility of blood biomarkers for early, non-invasive stratification of neurodegenerative versus mixed (vascular) pathology in pre-symptomatic populations, relevant to emerging NHS pathways for blood-based AD diagnostics.
Limitations: Observational design with biomarker measurement at a single baseline timepoint limits causal inference and generalisability beyond a Swedish older-adult cohort.

Outcomes of Bilateral Globus Pallidus Internus Deep Brain Stimulation in GNAO1-Related Disorder: An International Multicenter Experience

Bernardi K, Rong J, Northam WT et al. · 2026 Jul 27
Study Type: Retrospective multicentre cohort study
Key Question: Does bilateral GPi-DBS reduce dyskinetic crisis burden and improve outcomes in patients with GNAO1-related disorder?
Key Findings:
  • Dyskinetic crises reduced in 95% (38/40) of patients; ICU admissions fell by 86.1%
  • BFMDRS motor scores improved 27.5% (p<0.001, Cohen's d=1.12); CGI improved in 93.5%, with greater benefit for chorea than dystonia
  • Complications in 19.6%, mostly in emergently implanted patients; functional classification largely unchanged despite symptomatic benefit
Clinical Relevance: For UK paediatric/adult neurology and neurosurgical MDTs managing this rare but life-threatening disorder, these data—the largest cohort to date—support considering earlier elective GPi-DBS referral rather than reserving it for crisis management, potentially reducing cumulative morbidity and ICU burden.
Limitations: Retrospective design without a control group limits causal inference and introduces potential selection/reporting bias across 17 centres.

Two Years of Ocrelizumab Treatment in Black and Hispanic People with Multiple Sclerosis in CHIMES: A Single-Arm Clinical Trial

Amezcua L, Reder AT, Bernitsas E et al. · 2026 Jul 30
Study Type: Prospective, open-label, single-arm phase 4 trial
Key Question: Is ocrelizumab effective and safe in Black and Hispanic people with relapsing MS, populations historically underrepresented in MS trials?
Key Findings:
  • At week 48, 50.5% achieved no evidence of disease activity (NEDA-3); 94.5% relapse-free from progression, 95.1% relapse-free, 95.6% free of gadolinium-enhancing lesions, 52.7% free of new/enlarging T2 lesions.
  • Serious adverse events occurred in 8.2%; only 2.2% discontinued treatment.
  • Infusion reactions were more common in Hispanic (42.0%) vs Black participants (29.2%); GI adverse events more common in Black (23.0%) vs Hispanic participants (1.4%).
Clinical Relevance: Provides reassurance for UK clinicians prescribing ocrelizumab to ethnically diverse MS populations, supporting efficacy and safety comparable to pivotal trials predominantly conducted in white cohorts.
Limitations: Single-arm design without a comparator group limits causal inference regarding efficacy relative to other disease-modifying therapies.

Annals of neurology

Toward a Behavioral Reserve Model in Amyotrophic Lateral Sclerosis

Palumbo F, Iazzolino B, Moglia C et al. · 2026 Jul 30
Study Type: Prospective cohort study (single tertiary centre)
Key Question: Do premorbid regulatory traits and socio-educational exposures ("behavioral reserve") predict behavioral impairment in ALS?
Key Findings:
  • Premorbid behavioral regulation was the strongest predictor of behavioral impairment across all FrSBe models (n=965).
  • Higher combined education and social exposure were associated with lower odds of impairment, with a significant interaction across multiple behavioral domains; effects persisted in sensitivity analyses.
  • Reserve proxies did not significantly predict overall ECAS-CI impairment (n=633 subset), though some domain-specific associations were seen.
Clinical Relevance: This supports a "behavioral reserve" concept in ALS analogous to cognitive reserve in dementia, potentially informing early risk stratification and caregiver counselling in UK MND multidisciplinary clinics, where behavioral change significantly affects care planning and burden.
Limitations: Premorbid traits were retrospectively family-rated, limiting causal inference and introducing potential recall bias despite sensitivity analyses.

Brain : a journal of neurology

Bilateral gamma knife radiosurgery in severe essential tremor: a prospective trial

Régis J, Mira V, Pinto S et al. · 2026 Jul 23
Study Type: Prospective single-arm phase 2 trial
Key Question: Is staged bilateral Gamma Knife thalamotomy (VIM-GKS) safe and effective in patients with severe, bilaterally disabling, drug-resistant essential tremor without pre-existing speech/gait/balance concerns?
Key Findings:
  • Only one adverse event (hemi-proprioceptive ataxia and dysarthria) in 33 patients over 12 months; no significant worsening in cognition, speech, gait, or balance.
  • Tremor severity improved 58.5%, disability score 84.8%, functional impact 68.6%; among 29/33 responders, treated-limb tremor improved 74.4%.
  • Quality of life significantly improved at 12 months; mean interval between first and second GKS was 28.7 months.
Clinical Relevance: For carefully selected NHS patients with severe bilateral essential tremor unsuitable for or declining bilateral DBS, staged bilateral VIM-GKS may offer a non-invasive alternative with a favourable short-term safety profile.
Limitations: Small single-arm cohort without a control group and only 12-month follow-up limits conclusions on long-term safety and durability of effect.

Brain : a journal of neurology

Photobiomodulation of immune crosstalk rescues neuroinflammation in Alzheimer's disease models

Shen Q, Chang H, Li J et al. · 2026 Jul 23
Study Type: Preclinical experimental study (mouse models of Alzheimer's disease)
Key Question: Does photobiomodulation (PBM) improve cognition and pathology in AD by modulating neuroinflammatory crosstalk between microglia, astrocytes, and peripheral CD8+ T cells?
Key Findings:
  • PBM reduced cortical CD8+ T cell infiltration in APP/PS1 and 3×Tg AD mice, improving cognition and reducing neuronal damage/gliosis.
  • Mechanistically, PBM suppressed microglial chemokine/cytokine release (TNF-α, IL-1α, C1q) and endothelial adhesion molecule-mediated T cell migration, reducing neurotoxic A1 astrocyte induction.
  • PBM's effects were mediated via inhibition of microglial NLRP3 inflammasome activation, confirmed by genetic and pharmacological manipulation.
Clinical Relevance: PBM is a non-invasive, low-risk intervention already explored in other neurological conditions; these findings support its potential as a disease-modifying strategy for AD-related neuroinflammation, relevant to future NHS dementia treatment pathways pending human trials.
Limitations: Findings are confined to transgenic mouse models and require validation in human AD before clinical translation.
