The Journal of Urology

A Randomized Trial of Multimodal Prehabilitation Before Robotic-Assisted Radical Prostatectomy to Improve Postoperative Quality of Life and Functional Outcomes

Carbonell E, Dana F, Tena B et al. · 2026 Oct 28
Study Type: Prospective, single-centre randomised controlled trial (n=100 randomised; 95 analysed)
Key Question: Does a 4-week multimodal prehabilitation programme before RARP improve early postoperative quality of life and functional recovery compared with usual care?
Key Findings:
  • Primary outcome: at 1 month, EORTC QLQ-C30 global health status was 15 points higher with prehabilitation (adjusted mean difference; 95% CI 7–22; p<0.001), but the benefit fell to +4.5 points by 6 months.
  • Urinary continence (ICIQ-UI SF) was better at 1 month (mean difference −2.7; p=0.032), with benefits in mental health and selected HRQoL domains maintained through follow-up.
  • No differences in physical performance or erectile function.
Clinical Relevance: Supports considering prehabilitation (including pelvic floor training) within NHS RARP pathways, though the durable benefit appears limited to continence, mental health and selected HRQoL domains.
Limitations: Single-centre, unblinded design with unbalanced arms (53 vs 42 analysed) and no reported oncological or long-term outcomes limits generalisability, and the conclusion that prehabilitation "should be integrated into clinical practice" is stronger than the attenuating global benefit supports.

World Journal of Urology

Implementation of a same-session outpatient diode laser ablation pathway for suspected recurrent low-grade NMIBC: feasibility, safety, and early outcomes

Ola L, Busacca G, Dalmasso E et al. · 2026 Oct 29
Study Type: Retrospective analysis of a prospectively maintained single-centre database (case series, n=53).
Key Question: Is same-session cold-cup biopsy plus outpatient diode laser ablation feasible and safe for suspected recurrent low-grade NMIBC found at surveillance cystoscopy?
Key Findings:
  • All 53 procedures were completed without rescheduling, conversion to TURBT, or admission. Median pain score was 1 (IQR 1-2), and all patients preferred TULA to TURBT.
  • No Clavien-Dindo grade ≥III events occurred. At 30 days, 3.8% had grade I haematuria and 3.8% had UTIs needing antibiotics (grade II).
  • Biopsy showed pTa low-grade in 34.0%, pT0 in 50.9%, unexpected CIS in 7.5% (n=4), and adenocarcinoma in 1.9% (n=1). At 3 months, 93.9% (46/49) had negative cystoscopy.
Clinical Relevance: Supports "see-and-treat" office-based ablation as a means of reducing theatre demand and waiting times in NHS surveillance pathways, with biopsy retained for histological confirmation.
Limitations: Small, retrospective, single-centre, non-comparative series with only 3 months' follow-up and highly selected patients (1-2 lesions ≤1 cm), so durable oncological efficacy is unproven.

Intraoperative performance errors predict patient outcomes in robot-assisted radical prostatectomy

Graefen M, Wiklund P, Brancelli C et al. · 2026 Oct 20
Study Type: Retrospective video-based analysis of prospectively collected registry data (n=100; age-matched subgroup n=66).
Key Question: Does objectively assessed intraoperative surgical performance predict 12-month erectile function after bilateral nerve-sparing RARP?
Key Findings:
  • Cases with erectile dysfunction at 12 months had 43% more total errors and 67% more NVB dissection errors than cases with good outcomes (P<0.001); age-matched analysis showed 31–54% differences (P<0.001).
  • Step completion and critical errors did not differ between groups; inter-rater reliability of video scoring was high (0.91).
  • Demographic and clinical variables did not differ between outcome groups.
Clinical Relevance: Supports structured video-based performance assessment as a basis for surgeon feedback, credentialing and quality assurance in UK robotic prostatectomy programmes.
Limitations: Small sample from seven experienced surgeons with selected preoperatively potent patients; the abstract does not report adjusted effect sizes or confidence intervals, and the outcome threshold and ED definition are not specified.

BJU International

Hautmann versus modified Studer orthotopic neobladder after radical cystectomy

Mangold MH, Carl N, Renner LV et al. · 2026 Oct 22
Study Type: Single-centre retrospective cohort study with propensity score matching (104 matched pairs from 340 open radical cystectomy patients)
Key Question: Do Hautmann and modified Studer orthotopic neobladder configurations differ in urinary diversion-specific complications, perioperative outcomes, or patient-reported outcomes?
Key Findings:
  • At median 3 years, composite UD-specific complications (upper tract, anastomotic leak, infection), Clavien-Dindo ≥III complications, short-term mortality, HRQoL and decision regret did not differ significantly.
  • Exploratory multivariable analysis linked modified Studer to higher neobladder outlet obstruction (OR 2.99, 95% CI 1.15–8.78) and 90-day readmission (OR 1.87, 95% CI 1.05–3.39).
  • Among ISC users, modified Studer scored higher on ISC ease-of-use (β +17.4, 95% CI 1.40–33.5).
Clinical Relevance: Reassures that either configuration is reasonable, though the signal for outlet obstruction with modified Studer may inform configuration choice and counselling in UK centres offering ONB.
Limitations: Single-centre, open-surgery cohort with wide confidence intervals; the outlet obstruction and readmission findings were exploratory and require multicentre validation.

BJU International

Opposing roles of acid phosphatase 1 (ACP1) and ACP3 in prostate cancer progression

Faria AVS, Ferreira-Halder CV, Peppelenbosch MP et al. · 2026 Oct 28
Study Type: Systematic review and meta-analysis
Key Question: Are intracellular ACP1 and ACP3 expression associated with prostate cancer presence, aggressiveness, and survival?
Key Findings:
  • Low ACP3 (the gene encoding prostatic acid phosphatase) expression was seen in cancer tissue (OR 0.30, P = 0.006) and associated with higher Gleason grade (OR 0.44, P = 0.031), advanced stage, nodal involvement, and poorer survival.
  • ACP1 was more frequently elevated in patients (HR 2.24, P < 0.001) and correlated with high Gleason grade (HR 3.75, P < 0.001), advanced stage, nodal metastasis, and worse survival.
  • The ACP1 evidence came from only a limited number of studies and is described as preliminary.
Clinical Relevance: ACP3 and ACP1 may eventually contribute to tissue-based risk stratification, but this remains hypothesis-generating with no immediate change to NHS practice.
Limitations: The ACP1 findings rest on few studies, and the abstract reports no heterogeneity, study numbers, or risk-of-bias assessment, so confidence in the pooled estimates cannot be judged.

BJU International

Evaluation of StoneSense™ automatic target recognition in thulium fibre laser lithotripsy: a multi-model in vitro study

Thoman ME, Padovani PZ, Whiles BB et al. · 2026 Oct 29
Study Type: In vitro experimental study (three complementary bench models using BegoStone phantoms; prototype device).
Key Question: Does StoneSense™ automatic target recognition, integrated into a thulium fibre laser, restrict firing to a defined stone-to-fibre distance, and does this alter ablation efficiency, energy delivery and thermal rise?
Key Findings:
  • Laser firing was confined to ~2.3-2.7 mm from the stone, with no significant effect of LED intensity or pulse frequency (10 vs 20 Hz at 1 J).
  • In the cuvette model, ATR reduced active laser time (146 vs 179 s) and energy delivered (1.46 vs 1.79 kJ), improved ablation efficiency (1.77 vs 1.42 mg/J) and lowered temperature rise (4.2 vs 5.1°C; P = 0.001), at the cost of modestly longer procedure time.
  • In simulated RIRS, ATR increased ablated mass and efficiency (P < 0.05) with longer procedural duration.
Clinical Relevance: ATR could reduce off-target laser emission and thermal load during ureteroscopic lithotripsy, but it is not yet clinically validated and should not influence UK practice until in vivo data emerge.
Limitations: Bench-only testing on phantom stones with a prototype and small sample sizes, with no clinical outcomes (stone-free rate, complications, tissue injury) and a ~1 °C temperature difference of uncertain clinical significance.

BJU International

Urethroplasty for bulbomembranous strictures after benign prostatic hyperplasia surgery: systematic review and meta-analysis

Scilipoti P, Zorzi F, Folcia A et al. · 2026 Oct 31
Study Type: Systematic review and meta-analysis of observational studies (PROSPERO-registered, PRISMA 2020; ROBINS-I risk of bias).
Key Question: What are the patency, continence and erectile outcomes of urethroplasty for bulbomembranous strictures after endoscopic BPH surgery?
Key Findings:
  • Pooled patency 90% (95% CI 85-93%; 9 studies, 417 patients), mostly defined as freedom from re-intervention. Ventral onlay BMG 87%, anastomotic 94%, dorsal onlay BMG 91% (one study only).
  • Pooled urinary incontinence 5% (95% CI 3-10%); results consistent after excluding simple prostatectomy cases.
  • Erectile data insufficient to pool; two studies reported no significant SHIM deterioration.
Clinical Relevance: Supports urethroplasty as an option with low incontinence risk in this difficult post-BPH population, relevant to counselling and referral to reconstructive centres in the UK.
Limitations: Evidence is non-comparative, retrospective or observational with moderate-to-serious bias, and uses heterogeneous patency and continence definitions, so technique comparisons are not reliable.

Surgery in Motion: Different Surgical Techniques for Inguinal Lymph Node Dissection in Penile Cancer

Verdijk RWA, Patterson K, Brassetti A et al. · 2026 Oct 22
Study Type: Narrative technical review with accompanying surgical video (Surgery in Motion); not original research.
Key Question: What are the current open, video-endoscopic (VEIL) and robot-assisted (RAVEIL) techniques for inguinal lymph node dissection in penile cancer, and where does each have a role?
Key Findings:
  • Open ILND remains the reference approach, particularly for advanced nodal disease.
  • Minimally invasive techniques may reduce wound-related morbidity and can be considered in selected patients with limited nodal disease at experienced centres.
  • Across approaches, adherence to anatomical templates, careful patient selection and preservation of lymphatic/vascular structures where oncologically safe are central to limiting morbidity.
  • The abstract provides no quantitative outcome data.
Clinical Relevance: Provides practical technical guidance relevant to UK penile cancer centres considering MI-ILND, supporting training and standardisation within the national supra-regional network.
Limitations: Educational overview without reported outcome data; long-term oncological efficacy of MI approaches and emerging technologies remains unproven.
